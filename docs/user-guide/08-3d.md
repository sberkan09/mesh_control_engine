# 8. 3D games

A 3D game is a scene of **kit3d components** plus a game module, played by the stock player, the
same way a 2D game is a scene of 2D kit components (chapter 7). The scene places models, lights, a
camera, the sky and static colliders; the module's systems move things and drive the camera; the
player draws it all with the physically based forward renderer (cascaded shadows, Forward+ point
and spot lights, sky, fog, bloom, tonemap, FXAA) and runs it headless, with `-capture_tick`,
`-input` scripts and `-save` slots like any other game. The editor viewport shows the same scene.

`projects/Room3D` is the small reference (`mce example Room3D` copies it out of the SDK) and
`projects/SolsticeReach` the large one: an island with a time of day, scatter, beams and a HUD,
all from a module on the kit. A program that owns its renderer (`samples/04_cubes`,
`05_ashen_court`) still works and is covered at the end of the chapter.

## 8.1 A 3D scene on the kit

The components live in `mce/kit/kit3d.h`; the editor's inspector, the cook and the MCP server know
them. Conventions: right-handed, Y up, metres; an entity's `LocalTransform` places it (its
`WorldTransform` when it has a parent), and cameras look down their local −Z.

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| `MeshRef` | draws `mesh` at the entity: `tint`, `emissive_tint` (> 1 feeds bloom), `cull_radius`, `cast_shadows`, `visible`; `interpolate = 1` draws it between the last two ticks (anything the game moves); `skinning = 0` draws a skinned mesh rigidly; `material` (a `.mcmat`, section 8.5) replaces every submesh's own material |
| `Animator` | plays a clip of the entity's mesh by name (`clip`), at `speed`, offset by `phase`, cross-fading over `fade` seconds when `clip` changes; the kit advances `time` every tick. `loop = false` plays it once and sets `finished`; `restart` replays it. With `graph` (an `.mcanim`, section 8.9) a state machine picks the clips |
| `AttachToBone` | pins the entity to a node (`bone`: a joint, a socket) of `target`'s animated mesh, plus `offset` / `rotation` in the bone's space; the entity should be a root |
| `IKTarget3D` | gives IK chain `chain` (a `two_bone` or `look_at` chain of `animator`'s graph, section 8.9) this entity's world position as its goal, faded by `weight`; `found` is 1 while the chain resolves |
| `PointLight3D` | a point light at the entity plus `offset`: `color`, `intensity`, `radius`, `enabled`; `cast_shadows` gives it a cube shadow map (granted per frame by screen importance; `r.local_shadows 0` turns them all off) |
| `SpotLight3D` | a cone along `direction` (entity space): full inside `inner_deg`, none past `outer_deg`; `cast_shadows` as for point lights (one atlas tile) |
| `ReflectionProbe3D` | specular reflections for the surfaces inside the world-axis box `half_extents` around the entity, captured from `offset` (box-projected; needs `Environment3D.sky_ibl`); bump `version` to re-capture, `baked` loads the editor's bake instead; at most four per scene |
| `GiVolume3D` | global illumination: a `probes_x` × `probes_y` × `probes_z` grid of irradiance probes over the world-axis box `half_extents` around the entity (centered at `offset`) lights the surfaces inside with the light the scene bounces (sun, lights, emissive surfaces, sky); `probes_per_frame` re-capture every frame and blend in by `hysteresis`, `version` refills them all; `baked` (File > Bake GI volume) starts from the editor's bake, and the Low quality tier draws the bake only. Keep the grid's planes off the walls; the first enabled one is used |
| `Foliage3D` | many copies of `mesh` (grass, pebbles, rocks) as one instanced batch: `count` copies scattered over the world-axis rectangle `half_extents` (x, z) around the entity at its height, each turned by a random yaw and scaled between `scale_min` and `scale_max` (deterministic in `seed`), with `tint` and an optional `material`. They sway in the wind by `wind_amplitude` × h² metres (h: height above the copy's origin) at `wind_frequency` Hz along `wind_direction`; `cull_distance` drops far copies. Culled per copy on the GPU (against the frame's depth too), LOD per copy, shadows; rigid meshes only. Variation and wind shaping (M-VISUAL3 6): `tint_variation` (each copy's colour is `tint` × a mix from white toward `tint_variation` by a hash of its origin; white = none; keep its alpha 1 on alpha-masked materials), `wind_height` (metres where the sway reaches `wind_amplitude`, so a 5 m tree and a 1 m bush both sway by the amplitude at the top; 0 = amplitude × h² as before), `wind_gust` (0..1, slow gusts travelling along `wind_direction`), `wind_flutter` (metres of fast leaf jitter at `wind_height`; needs `wind_amplitude` > 0); `mesh_1`..`mesh_3` with `mesh_weights` (relative shares of `mesh`, `mesh_1`..`mesh_3`) put up to four kinds in one scatter, one instanced batch each (a kind with an unbound mesh or a weight of 0 gets no copies). Heavy scanned plants: cook the glTF with the `lod_levels` / `lod_ratio` / `lod_error` sidecar keys (section 8.4) and every copy picks its level by distance |
| `Decal3D` | a projected decal: `texture` (empty = plain `color`) projected down the entity's −Y onto every opaque surface inside the box of `size` metres; recolors the surface before lighting (`roughness`, `metallic` by `material_weight`; a smooth one also fills the surface's normal-map bumps, so a puddle over paving is a flat mirror), can glow (`emissive`), fades on steep surfaces (`angle_fade`) and toward the box's top and bottom (`edge_fade`). Up to 64 visible decals from 3 distinct textures per frame; past that the extra decals are dropped, `Kit3DExtract::note_built` (the player calls it) logs one warning naming both limits, and `perf_run` / `.mcperf` report `decals_dropped`. The workaround is one atlas texture shared by every decal with each decal's `uv_rect` picking its tile |
| `ParticleEmitter3D` | GPU particles: up to `max_particles` sprites spawned at `rate` per second (bump `burst_version` for `burst` at once) inside `box` around the entity plus `offset`, flying along `direction` within `spread_deg` at `speed_min`..`speed_max`, falling by `gravity`, slowed by `drag`, bouncing off `ground_height` with `collide`, growing and fading from the start to the end size and color over `lifetime_min`..`lifetime_max`; `additive` or alpha blended, optional sprite `texture`. Render-side (not part of the simulation) |
| `Terrain3D` | a heightmap terrain from a `terrain` asset (`.mcterrain`: `resolution` samples per side, 2^n+1; `size` in metres; `height_range`; base64 u16 `heights` or a constant `fill`), centred on the entity. Drawn as a quadtree of chunks that get coarser with distance (CDLOD-style, skirts hide the seams); `pixel_error` is the allowed screen error in pixels (0: the quality tier's, Low 4 / Medium 2 / High 1). `material` (a `.mcmat`) and `tint` color it, `uv_tile` repeats its textures every that many metres (0: once over the terrain), `cast_shadows`, `enabled`. A terrain with splat `layers` (chapter 5.7) draws them instead of `material`, up to 8 blended by their weights, by height on Medium and High, triplanar on cliffs on High |
| `Camera3DComponent` | the view: the first `primary` one, at the entity, `fov_deg`, `near_z`, `far_z`; `mouse_look = 1` asks the player for relative mouse mode. A scene with one plays on the 3D path |
| `CameraView` | where a camera draws (M-VIEWS): `viewport` (x, y, width, height as fractions of the window from its top-left) and `order` (lower first, higher on top). Works on 3D and 2D cameras. Without any `CameraView` the primary camera fills the window as before; with one, every camera that has an enabled `CameraView` is drawn into its rect, and so is each primary camera without one (full window, order 0). Split-screen is two cameras with halves; a picture-in-picture is a second camera with a small rect and a higher order. Each 3D view keeps its own anti-aliasing history and exposure. `target` (a `RenderTexture` entity) draws the camera into that texture instead of the window. `split` 1-4 puts the camera in that player's split-screen cell (`viewport` is then a part of the cell): the cells follow the players present, so a two-player game splits in halves and reflows when one leaves; a per-player minimap or HUD camera shares the player's `split` with a higher `order`. A view covering at most half the window draws two shadow cascades at most, and one at most about a third also skips screen-space reflections, contact shadows, volumetric light, depth of field and motion blur |
| `SplitScreen` | how the window splits between the players (M-VIEWS 4); one per scene: `layout` (0 auto: two side by side, or stacked on a portrait window; three with player 1 on top; four in a 2 x 2 grid. 1 columns, 2 rows, 3 grid) and `divider` (pixels between the cells, the window's clear color) |
| `UiScreenView` | lays a `UiScreen` on the same entity out in `camera`'s view: one HUD per player (chapter 12) |
| `RenderTexture` | a texture a camera draws into (M-VIEWS 3): `width`, `height` (pixels), `update_hz` (redraws a second, 0 = every frame; a security monitor at 10), `enabled`, `ui_image` (a `.mcui` image widget of that name shows it). Point a camera's `CameraView.target` at this entity to fill it. Freed when the component goes, re-made when its size changes. A view never shows the texture it is drawing (that surface falls back to its own look), and two textures that show each other lag a frame. A 3D camera's edges are smoothed in linear color there, so a few edge pixels differ slightly from the same view in the window |
| `ShowRenderTexture` | shows a `RenderTexture` on this entity (M-VIEWS 3): `source` (the `RenderTexture` entity), `slot` (0: a `Sprite`'s texture or a `MeshRef`'s base color; 1: the mesh's emission, so a screen glows in the dark) and `intensity` (slot 1's strength). A minimap is a `Sprite` with one; a monitor is a `MeshRef` with `slot` 1 |
| `CoopGame`, `PlayerSpawn`, `LocalPlayer`, `PlayerCamera`, `SharedCamera2D` | local co-op without code (M-VIEWS 5): join and leave, a prefab per player, a camera per player that fills their split-screen cell; chapter 11.3 describes each, `player_setup` (chapter 14) builds the rig |
| `Environment3D` | the sun (`sun_direction`, `sun_color`, `sun_intensity`, `sun_shadows`), hemisphere ambient, the sky gradient and an optional equirect `sky_texture` (an 8-bit PNG/JPEG or a `.hdr` / `.exr`, chapter 5.2; `sky_texture_intensity` scales it, `sky_rotation_deg` turns it about +Y), fog, exposure, bloom, FXAA and the shadow cascades. `atmosphere` swaps the gradient for a physically based sky that colors the sun and the ambient by the sun's height (`atmosphere_intensity`, `atmosphere_haze`; `sun_color` is then the light above the air), `time_of_day` (0..24, -1 = off) places the sun on a simple day's arc, and `volumetric_fog` replaces the fog with a height fog the sun and the lights shine through (`volumetric_density`, `volumetric_height_falloff`, `volumetric_anisotropy`, `volumetric_albedo`, `volumetric_distance`; off on the Low quality tier). `auto_exposure` follows the frame's brightness (`exposure_compensation`, `auto_exposure_speed_up/down`), `color_grade` grades the picture with a 256 × 16 strip LUT (import it with `"srgb": false`; `color_grade_strength`), `depth_of_field` blurs what lies away from `dof_focus_distance` (`dof_focus_range`, `dof_blur_range`, `dof_max_radius`) and `motion_blur` smears moving things (`motion_blur_intensity`); the last two are off on the Low tier. `ssao` darkens the ambient light in creases and where things meet the ground (`ssao_radius`, `ssao_intensity`, `ssao_power`; `r.ssao 0` turns it off). `ssr` makes smooth surfaces mirror what is on screen over the probes and the sky (`ssr_max_distance`, `ssr_thickness`, `ssr_max_roughness`, `ssr_roughness_fade`, `ssr_intensity`, `ssr_steps`, `ssr_edge_fade`; it reads the previous frame, so it shows from the second frame; off on the Low tier, `r.ssr 0` turns it off). `upscaler` (1, the default: an edge-adaptive spatial upsample; 0: bilinear) is how a frame drawn below the output size reaches the screen, and `sharpen` (0..1) sharpens the final frame; players override both with `r.upscaler` and `r.sharpen`. `contact_shadows` adds short sun shadows the cascades are too coarse for, in creases and under small details (`contact_length`, `contact_thickness`, `contact_intensity`, `contact_steps`; off on the Low tier, `r.contact_shadows 0` turns it off). The camera's look lives in the tonemap pass and is off by default: `vignette` (0..1, with `vignette_smoothness`) darkens the corners in linear light, `chromatic_aberration` (0..1) fringes red and blue toward the edges (1 = 0.5 % of the width), `film_grain` (0..1, `grain_size` 1..4 px) adds animated grain that is strongest in the mid tones, and `lens_dirt` (a mask imported with `"srgb": false`, strength `lens_dirt_intensity` 0..4) shows where bloom is bright. `tonemapper` picks the curve: 0 the ACES fit (the engine's long-standing one), 1 AgX (softer highlights, desaturating toward white), 2 Khronos PBR Neutral (keeps in-gamut colours as authored up to a knee). All tiers keep them; the grain and the fringe follow `access.motion_scale`, and the grain is added after FXAA so FXAA does not chase it (the sharpen pass, when on, does sharpen it). A `Camera3DComponent.focus_distance` above 0 replaces `dof_focus_distance` for that camera (a timeline can animate it for focus pulls). For one-off shots the agent tools take the same look per call: `render_frame` / `render_sheet` accept `camera.focus` (meters, or `"target"` = the distance from `camera.position` to `camera.target`; it turns depth of field on and wins over the environment and the camera component), `camera.exposure` (a multiplier of `exposure`) and an `environment` object of `Environment3D` field overrides (`{"fog_density": 0.04, "sun_color": [1, 0.6, 0.3], "depth_of_field": 1}`; numbers, `true`/`false` for flags, arrays for vectors and colors, checked against the reflected fields). They reach the player as the convars `r.dof_focus`, `r.exposure_scale` and `r.env_override` (`field=value;field=a,b,c`), so a play session can set them too; none of them changes the saved scene |
| `BoxCollider3D` | a static box in the kit's physics world: `half` (scaled by the entity), `offset`, `pitch_deg` (ramps), `friction`, `layer` |
| `MeshCollider3D` | a static triangle mesh from a mesh asset's rest pose (use a simple collision mesh) |
| `TerrainFoliage3D` | grass, flowers or rocks on a terrain: copies of `mesh` standing on the surface of the `Terrain3D` on the same entity or its parent, `density` per square metre where splat layer `layer` is fully painted and thinner where its weight is lower (`layer` 8: everywhere), none in holes or on ground steeper than `max_slope_deg`, at most `max_count`, each turned by a random yaw and scaled between `scale_min` and `scale_max` (deterministic in `seed`). Painting the layer with the Terrain panel paints the foliage; repainting one spot leaves the copies elsewhere where they were. Drawn like `Foliage3D` (one instanced batch, `tint`, `material`, wind, `cull_distance`, `cast_shadows`), with the same `tint_variation`, `wind_height`, `wind_gust` and `wind_flutter`. One kind per entity: put each kind on its own child entity of the terrain |
| `TerrainCollider3D` | a static height field for the entity's `Terrain3D`: the same triangles the terrain draws at full resolution, under its transform (scale included), open where the terrain has `holes`; `friction`, `layer` |
| `StaticMerge3D` | merges the rigid `MeshRef` children (and their children) of this entity into one mesh per material per cell of `cell_size` metres (4..512, default 32) when the scene first draws, so a modular kit of dozens of different pieces sharing a material draws in a few calls per pass (section 8.6, "Static merge"). `cast_shadows` 0 silences the merged meshes (otherwise each child's own flag decides), `enabled` 0 draws the children on their own, and `version` is bumped after moving children at runtime (moving one in the editor re-merges by itself). The editor viewport never merges, so selection and picking stay per entity; the player and `render_frame` do |
| `ChunkLod3D` | on a `SceneChunk` entity of a streamed world (section 4.8): `proxy` names a mesh (an HLOD bake of the area, `.mchlod`) drawn at the chunk entity's transform while the area is not loaded, so a far area keeps its skyline instead of vanishing; `impostor` (optional) replaces the proxy beyond `impostor_distance` metres (camera to the chunk box, default 600), and beyond `max_distance` (0: no limit) nothing draws. A loaded area draws its own entities and hides the proxy; a loading one keeps the proxy up. `cast_shadows`, `enabled`. `Kit3DStats::chunk_proxies` / `chunk_impostors` count the draws; MCP's `hlod_bake` bakes the proxies and, with `impostor: true`, the impostors (M-OPENWORLD A3, A4) |

`Environment3D` exists twice: the scene's component (the first one) is what the editor draws and
what the kit copies at Startup into the **resource** of the same type, which is what the player
draws. A game with a time of day writes the resource every tick (SolsticeReach's `present.cpp`).

2D kit entities draw over the 3D frame: a `Camera2DComponent` with `pixels_per_meter = 1` and
`TextLabel`s positioned from `ViewInfo` make a HUD in pixels (SolsticeReach's `sr_hud`).

## 8.2 Game code on the kit

`kit::install_kit3d(world, &assets)` registers the components and adds these systems; the player
calls it before your module's `install`, and calling it again from the module (so tests and tools
that build a World by hand get it too) is harmless:

| Phase | System | Does |
|---|---|---|
| Startup | `kit3d_environment` | the scene's `Environment3D` into the resource |
| Startup | `kit3d_colliders` | `BoxCollider3D` / `MeshCollider3D` / `TerrainCollider3D` bodies in `Physics3D` |
| FixedUpdate_Physics | `kit3d_physics` | `Physics3D::world.step` when `auto_step` is 1 |
| FixedUpdate_Post | `kit3d_animators` | `Animator` time, cross-fades, graphs, `AnimEvent3D` |
| FixedUpdate_Post | `kit3d_ik` | the goals of the graphs' IK chains: `IKTarget3D` entities, the ground under foot chains |
| FixedUpdate_Post | `kit3d_attach` | `AttachToBone` poses (after the IK) |

Resources your systems use:

- **`kit::Physics3D`**: `world` is the `physics::World3D` the colliders live in (section 8.7).
  Add your characters and bodies to it under entity ids of your choosing. A game that steps
  physics only in some modes sets `auto_step = 0` and calls `world.step(f.dt)` itself. The world
  is not saved: make characters again when `controller_state(e, ...)` says there is none (Room3D's
  `room3d_hero` does, so a loaded save plays on).
- **`kit::StaticInstances3D`**: many static copies of a few meshes (grass, rocks, trees) placed
  at Startup by a seeded scatter, then `build_cells(16, max_extent)`. Each copy can thin out past
  `thin_distance` and vanish past `max_distance`. Not entities and not in the state hash.
- **`kit::Environment3D`**: the light the player draws this frame.

A third-person game in four systems, from Room3D:

```cpp
// FixedUpdate_Pre: the hero walks relative to the camera on the kit's physics world.
MoveResult3D last;
if (!physics->world.controller_state(e, last)) physics->world.create_character(e, feet + offset, params);
const MoveResult3D r = physics->world.move_character(e, {dir.x * speed * dt, vy * dt, dir.z * speed * dt}, params);
t.position = r.position - offset;               // the MeshRef follows (interpolate = 1)
animator.clip.assign(moving ? StringView("walk") : StringView("idle"));

// FixedUpdate_Post: the camera entity orbits the hero, pulled in by camera_sweep.
cam.yaw -= in.look.x * sensitivity;
cam_t.rotation = quat_from_axis_angle({0, 1, 0}, cam.yaw) * quat_from_axis_angle({1, 0, 0}, -cam.pitch);

// Startup: rocks into StaticInstances3D, then cells.
instances.instances.push_back(kit::StaticInstance3D{pos, scale, rot, instances.mesh_slot(rock)});
instances.build_cells(16.0f, 3.0f);
```

Without code, `CameraFollow3D` on the camera entity orbits a `target`: the pivot is the target's
position plus `offset` (1.5 m up by default), smoothed by `smoothing` (1/s, 0 snaps); the camera
sits `distance` metres away at `yaw` (about +Y) and `pitch` (above the horizon, clamped to
`min_pitch`..`max_pitch`) and looks at the pivot. With `orbit_speed` > 0 the Look action turns the
orbit at that many radians per second. These fields are off by default (M-MOVE3D):

- `collide` turns the boom into a spring arm. A sphere of `collision_radius` swept from the pivot
  keeps the camera in front of static and kinematic geometry (characters and dynamic props never
  pull it in). It snaps in when a wall comes between, and grows back at `return_speed` m/s once the
  wall clears. `arm` reads back the boom's length this tick.
- `look_ahead` leads the pivot by that many seconds of the target's ground velocity, so the camera
  shows where the target is going. Vertical motion such as jumps is not led.
- `first_person` puts the camera at the pivot (set `offset` to eye height), looking along `yaw` and
  `pitch`. `distance` and smoothing do not apply.
- `lock_on` names an entity to keep framed. The yaw turns at `lock_on_rate` rad/s (0 means at once)
  until the camera, the target and that entity stand on one line, the target in front. The Look
  action then only tilts. Clearing `lock_on` leaves the orbit where it is.

Everything is tick-driven and deterministic: the player draws moving entities between the last
two ticks, so a camera written once per tick is smooth at any frame rate.

## 8.3 The stock player's 3D path

A scene with a `Camera3DComponent` starts the 3D renderer; the 2D kit, the console and the pause
overlay draw on top. Command-line settings (chapter 16):

| Convar | Default | |
|---|---|---|
| `-r.quality` | auto | the cost tier: `low`, `medium`, `high` (`auto`: medium on phones, high elsewhere). It sets MSAA, the shadow map, render scale, and caps the scene's shadow cascades, bloom and FXAA. Saved: an options screen bound to it takes effect on the next start |
| `-r.msaa` | 4 | at most this many MSAA samples of the 3D frame (1 = off) |
| `-r.upscaler` | scene | how a frame drawn below the output size (Low and Medium tiers, without TAA) reaches it: `spatial` (an edge-adaptive upsample), `bilinear`, or `scene` (the scene's `Environment3D.upscaler`). Saved |
| `-r.sharpen` | -1 | contrast-adaptive sharpening of the final 3D frame, 0..1 (0 = off; -1 = the scene's `Environment3D.sharpen`). Saved |
| `-r.lod` | 1 | draw coarser mesh levels with distance (section 8.4) |
| `-r.lod_pixels` | 1.0 | the largest LOD error allowed on screen, in pixels |
| `-r.lod_force` | -1 | draw every mesh at one level (-1 = by screen size) |
| `-r.preload3d` | 1 | upload every texture and mesh of the pack at start (0 = on first sight) |
| `-r.uniform_ring_mb` | 4 | per-frame uniform memory; raise it if the log reports a uniform ring overflow |
| `-r.stats` | 0 | the stats line: triangles (per LOD level), draws, meshes, instances, lights |
| `-mouse_look` | 1 | let a camera with `mouse_look` capture the mouse (released while paused, in the console and when unfocused) |

At exit a windowed run prints `<game>: 3D last frame N triangles (LOD a/b/c/d), D draws` and
`N frames, M ticks, E GL errors`; tests match on them (Room3D's `room3d_walk_gpu`, `room3d_lod_gpu`).

## 8.4 Models and LODs

Put a `.gltf` or `.glb` under `assets/` and adopt it (section 5.3); a Wavefront `.obj` works too (see below). The glTF importer takes:

- triangle-list primitives with positions, normals (generated if absent) and one UV set,
- the node tree with its transforms,
- metallic-roughness materials, cooked inline into the mesh: factors, base-colour, normal,
  metallic-roughness (ORM, occlusion in R), emissive and occlusion textures, `alphaMode`,
  `doubleSided`, and the extensions `KHR_materials_unlit`, `KHR_materials_emissive_strength`,
  `KHR_texture_transform` (one UV transform per material; every map uses `TEXCOORD_0`) and the factors of
  `KHR_materials_clearcoat`, `_sheen`, `_anisotropy`, `_transmission` and `_ior` (their textures are not
  imported: a warning names them),
- node transform animation clips (translation, rotation, scale), named as in the file,
- skins, when the file has them: joints, inverse bind matrices and 4 influences per vertex
  (`JOINTS_0` / `WEIGHTS_0`, up to 256 joints per skin). A vertex with more influences (`JOINTS_1` /
  `WEIGHTS_1`, up to 8 sets) keeps its 4 largest, renormalized; a warning counts the vertices that lost more than
  1 % of their weight. See "Skinning" below; it is optional,
- sparse accessors (everywhere an accessor is read), `KHR_mesh_quantization` (BYTE / SHORT positions, normals and
  UVs; the node transform carries the scale) and `EXT_meshopt_compression` (the compressed views are decoded at
  cook; the fallback buffer is ignored). `gltfpack -c` and `gltf-transform meshopt` write the latter.

Not imported, each with one warning per file: morph targets (the node's or mesh's default `weights` are baked into
the positions and normals, so the rest pose matches other viewers), `COLOR_0`, `TEXCOORD_1` and up, cameras and
`KHR_lights_punctual` (the mesh asset cannot hold entities; `mce import gltf` writes them as a prefab, see "Lights and
cameras" below, and the cook's warning stays). `KHR_draco_mesh_compression` is not decoded: a file that requires it is a cook error that
says to re-export with meshopt (`gltfpack -c` or `gltf-transform meshopt`); a file that only uses it is read from
its uncompressed fallback. A file that lists any other extension in `extensionsRequired` is a cook error naming it.
Cooking treats the file as untrusted: sparse indices, strides, counts and compressed streams are range-checked
and a bad one is an error naming the JSON pointer (an accessor is capped at 16M elements, a scene at 32M vertices).
Buffers must be inside the file (GLB chunk or `data:` URIs); images must be separate files that
are themselves texture assets, referenced relative to the glTF. Sidecar settings: `"scale"`,
`"max_triangles"` (a budget that makes an over-size mesh a cook error), `"skinning"` (default
`true`; `false` imports a skinned file as a static one) and the LOD settings below.

### Wavefront OBJ

A `.obj` under `assets/` is adopted like a glTF (section 5.3) and cooks to the same mesh asset, so `MeshRef` and the LOD
settings below work on it unchanged. What it reads:

- `v`, `vt`, `vn` and `f` (triangles, quads and larger polygons, which are fan-triangulated, so keep them convex);
  negative indices count back from the last element defined at that line. `o` and `g` start a node (one root node per
  name, created at its first face; faces before any name go on an unnamed node), `usemtl` splits a node into one submesh
  per material, and `mtllib` names the material file. Without `vn` the normals are smooth per position, weighted by the
  corner angle (smoothing groups are not used). V of `vt` is flipped, because OBJ's texture origin is the bottom left.
- The materials of the `.mtl` next to the model (`mtllib`, relative to the `.obj`), cooked inline into the mesh as for
  glTF: `Kd` (base colour), `d` or `Tr` (opacity; below 1 the material is alpha blended), `Ns` (roughness, as the
  perceptual value (2 / (Ns + 2))^(1/4), because the shader squares it; `Ns` 0 is fully rough), `Pr` and `Pm` (roughness
  and metallic, the PBR extension; `Pr` wins over `Ns`), `Ke` (emissive), `map_Kd`, `map_Bump` / `bump` / `norm` (a
  normal map), `map_Ke` (with no `Ke` the emissive colour defaults to white, so the map shows), and `map_Pr` + `map_Pm`
  when both name one packed texture (G = roughness, B = metallic; the shader multiplies it by the factors, so with the
  texture set, roughness and metallic default to 1 unless the `.mtl` gives `Pr` / `Pm`, as in glTF). When both `d` and
  `Tr` appear in one material, `d` wins whatever the order. Textures are separate image files that are texture assets,
  named relative to the `.mtl`; an absolute or drive-letter path (`C:\...\tex.png`, `/abs/tex.png`) is read as its file
  name next to the `.mtl`, with a warning; texture options (`-s`, `-o`, `-bm`, ...) are skipped with a warning.
- The `.mtl` is an asset too: **adopt it** next to the `.obj` (the sidecar says `"importer": "mtl"`; it cooks to a plain
  material of its first `newmtl`, and a change to any material re-cooks the models that read it).

Axes and units: OBJ has neither, so the sidecar says. `"up": "y"` (default) or `"z"` (a +Z-up file is turned into the
engine's +Y-up, right-handed), and `"scale"` multiplies the root nodes (a file in centimetres: `0.01`). `"max_triangles"`,
`"lod_levels"`, `"lod_ratio"` and `"lod_error"` work as for glTF; `"skinning"` and `"ragdoll"` do not exist (no skins).

Warnings, each once per file with the first line and a count: `l` and `p` (lines and points), free-form curves and
surfaces, `s` smoothing groups, other attribute statements, vertex colours after `v x y z`, unsupported MTL keys (`Ka`,
`Ks`, `Ni`, `illum` and `Tf` are skipped without one), a `usemtl` name no `.mtl` defines (the default material is used),
and a `mtllib` that is missing or not adopted (every material falls back to the default: white, rough, non-metallic).
A missing or unregistered texture is an error naming the `.mtl` line. Anything else the parser cannot read (an unknown
statement, a bad number, an index of 0 or out of range, a face with fewer than 3 vertices) is an error naming
`file:line`. The file is untrusted: numbers must be finite and within 1e9, and the cook refuses lines over 16 MiB,
files over 256 MiB, more than 16M vertices, texture coordinates or normals, 32M face corners, polygons over 1M vertices,
1M nodes and 256K submeshes, naming the limit instead of allocating. The caps are generous: the worst-case memory of a maximum-size file can be several GB. DCC exporters differ (3ds Max, ZBrush); if one
writes something this list does not cover, export glTF instead.

### Autodesk FBX

A `.fbx` under `assets/` (binary or ASCII, version 3000 and up) is adopted like a glTF and cooks to the same mesh asset, so
`MeshRef`, animation clips, skins and the LOD settings below work on it unchanged. What it reads:

- Mesh nodes: polygons are triangulated (keep n-gons convex), normals (smooth ones are generated when the file has none),
  UV set 0 (V is flipped, because FBX's texture origin is the bottom left), one submesh per node and material slot.
- Materials, cooked inline as for glTF: diffuse colour times factor, opacity, roughness, metalness, emission, and the base
  colour, normal and emissive maps. Colours are taken as linear, as for glTF (an FBX does not say). Plain (Lambert and
  Phong) materials have no opacity of their own: the `Opacity` property is used when the file has one, otherwise
  opacity is 1 − `TransparencyFactor` × the mean of `TransparentColor` (a factor with no colour is just 1 − factor), so
  Maya's and Max's opaque material (`TransparencyFactor` 1 with a black `TransparentColor`) stays opaque; below 1 the
  material is alpha blended. A plain material with a **base colour map** or an **emissive map** uses the map as the
  colour: `DiffuseColor` / `EmissiveColor` are not multiplied in (Maya writes `EmissiveColor` 0 next to an emission map),
  and only an explicit `DiffuseFactor` / `EmissiveFactor` scales the map (default 1). Emission is capped at 1e6. A roughness map and a metalness map that name one packed image (G = roughness,
  B = metallic) are imported; otherwise they are ignored with a warning. Textures must be **external** image files that
  are texture assets, found as the file names them (relative to the model); an absolute or outside-`assets/` path is read
  as its file name next to the model, with a warning. A texture embedded in the FBX is an error that says to export with
  external textures (Blender: untick "Embed Textures"; Maya and Max: untick "Embed Media").
- The node tree (translation, rotation, scale, pivots and pre/post rotations are folded into one transform per node).
- Skins: each cluster is a joint, up to 256 joints per skin and the 4 largest influences per vertex (renormalized, with a
  warning counting vertices that lost more than 1 % of their weight). Bind poses become inverse binds.
- Animation: every animation stack becomes a clip named after it, sampled at a fixed rate, translation, rotation and
  scale per node, with no curve fitting (a clip is as many keys as frames). Only the stack's **base layer** is evaluated;
  more layers, and a layer weight other than 1, warn. Euler angles are evaluated by the library and written as
  quaternions kept continuous between keys.

Axes and units: an FBX file says which way is up and what its unit is, and the cook converts to the engine's frame (+Y up,
right-handed, metres; a left-handed file is mirrored). A cube modelled in centimetres with +Z up, and Blender's usual pair
(an armature scaled 100 over objects scaled 0.01), come out the same as the metre, +Y-up file. There is no `"up"` key.
Sidecar settings: `"scale"` (default 1.0) multiplies on top of that, `"sample_rate"` (default 30, samples per second; the
key times are exactly `i / rate`, the last key lands on the end of the stack), and `"max_triangles"`, `"lod_levels"`,
`"lod_ratio"` and `"lod_error"` as for glTF. `"skinning"` and `"ragdoll"` are not read for FBX.

Warnings, each once per file with a count: hidden meshes (not imported), points and lines, vertex colours, UV sets beyond
the first, blend shapes, lights and cameras (`mce import fbx` writes them as a prefab, see "Lights and cameras" below), curves and surfaces,
bump maps and opacity maps, extra skin deformers, dual-quaternion skinning (linear blending is used), animation of
anything but node transforms. The file is untrusted: the cook refuses files over 256 MiB, runs the reader under a
memory budget of 1 GiB for its temporary data plus 1 GiB for the loaded scene (so up to about 2 GiB for one load; the
dependency scan and the cook each load the file, one after the other) and a node depth of
4096, and caps the scene at 256K nodes, 16M welded vertices, 32M triangle corners, 64K corners in one polygon, 1K skin
weights on one vertex, 1K clips, 128K keys per channel and 32M key floats, naming the limit in the error instead of
allocating. Work is capped too: 64M animation transform evaluations per file (frames × animated nodes, summed over the
clips; a file with a scale-inheriting node, which needs a helper node, counts every node as animated), and 128M face
visits per file over every node that uses a mesh (a mesh with millions of points and lines on thousands of nodes is
refused). Lower `"sample_rate"` or shorten the stacks to fit. Non-finite numbers are errors. The caps are generous: the
worst-case memory of a maximum-size file is several GB.

Determinism: the same FBX cooks to the same bytes on one machine. Across machines (Linux, Windows, macOS) it can differ
in a quantization step, because the reader evaluates Euler angles and slerp with the platform's `sin`, `cos` and `atan2`
(ADR-0047); glTF and OBJ do not have this. A file the importer refuses, or one from an exporter it misreads, is not worth
fighting: open it in Blender and export glTF (or FBX 7.4 binary), which also bakes anything exotic.

### The import summary

Every cook prints one line per 3D model, so a model that came in far bigger or emptier than expected shows at once:

```
  mesh assets/models/hero.fbx: 1234 tris, 2 submeshes, 3 materials, 24 joints, 2 clips, 3 LODs, 1 warning
```

Triangles are the base level over all submeshes; materials are distinct materials; joints are the skin joints; LODs counts
the levels the most detailed submesh has, the base included (1 = no coarser level). The numbers are read back from the cooked
file, so a model the cook found up to date prints the same ones. The warning count is the exception: it counts the warnings
raised by this run, which a cached model does not repeat (they were printed when it was cooked; `mce cook --force` shows
them again). `--quiet` leaves the lines out. MCP's `cook_run` returns the same fields in `meshes` (chapter 14).

### Lights and cameras (`mce import gltf` / `mce import fbx`)

A mesh asset holds geometry, materials, skins and clips, not entities, so the lights and cameras that come with a model are
written by a converter, as a prefab. Adopt the model first (section 5.3) or let the converter create its sidecar, then:

```
mce import gltf assets/models/room.glb              # -> assets/models/room.mcprefab
mce import fbx  assets/models/stage.fbx [--cook path/to/mce_cook]
    [--out-dir d] [--name n] [--werror]
```

(MCP: `asset_import {"tool": "gltf" | "fbx", "file": "assets/models/room.glb"}`, chapter 14.) The prefab is a root entity
named after the model with a `MeshRef` to it, at the origin, and one child (`ChildOf` the root) per light or camera:
`PointLight3D`, `SpotLight3D` (aimed along the node's local aim axis: -Z for glTF, the light's own axis for FBX) or
`Camera3DComponent` (the first camera `primary`, the rest not, so dropping the prefab in a scene that has its own camera
does not change the view until you pick one). Each child sits at the node's **world** transform in the engine's frame (+Y up,
right-handed, metres; scale is dropped, and the sidecar's `"scale"` multiplies positions as the cook multiplies the root
nodes). glTF is already in that frame; for FBX the cook's own reader converts axes and units (`mce_cook --dump-fbx-scene`
prints the lights, cameras and world transforms as JSON, so there is no second FBX parser; the converter finds `mce_cook`
by `--cook`, `MCE_COOK`, or beside itself in an SDK). glTF nodes are read from the default scene, with `matrix` or
translation / rotation / scale. Put the prefab in a scene as an instance (section 4.6); re-importing rewrites the file and
keeps its GUID, the model's, and the same bytes if nothing changed.

**Light units.** The engine's intensity is reflected radiance, not photometric (ADR-0024): a white Lambert surface 1 m from a
point light of intensity *I* reflects *I*, which is the physical irradiance divided by pi. The converter assumes the usual
683 lm/W and an exposure of 1:

| Source | Engine value |
|---|---|
| glTF `point` / `spot`, candela | `intensity = candela / (683 pi)`, clamped to 0..100 (1 cd is about 0.00047; Blender's 1000 W point light exports about 4300 cd, about 2.0) |
| glTF `directional`, lux | no entity: `Environment3D.sun_intensity` about `lux / (683 pi)`, reported in the warning |
| FBX light `Intensity` | `intensity = Intensity / 100` as read by the library (the DCC's 1.0 is 1.0; FBX has no photometric unit), clamped to 0..100 |
| range / radius | glTF `range` (clamped 0.1..200 m); else, and for FBX, `10 sqrt(intensity)` metres clamped to 1..200, which is where the engine's inverse-square falloff drops to 0.01 |
| colour | taken as linear, clamped to 0..1 |
| spot cone | glTF `outerConeAngle` / `innerConeAngle` and FBX `OuterAngle` / `InnerAngle` (FBX cone angles are full angles, halved) as degrees, outer clamped to 1..89, inner to 0..outer (a warning when the inner angle had to move); an FBX spot with no outer angle gets FBX's default, 45 degrees full angle = 22.5 here |
| switched-off light | an FBX light whose "cast light" is off is imported with `enabled = 0` (its intensity kept) |
| camera | glTF `yfov`, FBX vertical field of view, as `fov_deg` (5..170); `znear` / `zfar` in metres as `near_z` (0.001..100) / `far_z` (1..100000); no `zfar` gives 1000 |

These are conventions, not measurements: if a converted scene looks too dark or bright, scale `intensity` on the
children (or set `Environment3D.exposure`). Directional lights have no entity because the engine has one sun: the warning
names `Environment3D.sun_direction` and the direction found (the way the light travels, in the engine's frame), for
example `set Environment3D.sun_direction to [0.2, -0.9, 0.3]; Environment3D.sun_intensity about 1.2`. Also named warnings:
orthographic cameras and area / volume lights (dropped), a mirrored node (negative scale, dropped; a zero scale is an error), a
colour, angle or plane clamped, a far plane that is not beyond the near plane, an FBX light whose decay is not quadratic
(the engine always falls off with the inverse square, so `none`, linear and cubic are ignored). A model's `.mceasset` that
exists but is unreadable or has no `guid` is an error and nothing is written; a missing one is created. `--werror` turns any warning into a failure and nothing is written, not even the sidecar.

The inputs are untrusted: a glTF file over 128 MiB (64 MiB of JSON in a `.glb`), more than 262,144 nodes or a tree deeper
than 4096 is refused with the limit, as are malformed JSON, a truncated `.glb`, a node reached twice and a light or camera
index that does not exist; an FBX the cook's reader refuses is reported with its message.

**LODs.** A mesh can carry up to four levels per submesh. Generated: `"lod_levels": 1..3` in the
sidecar simplifies each level from the base with meshoptimizer, aiming at `"lod_ratio"` (default
0.35) of the previous level's triangles within `"lod_error"` (default 0.05, relative to the mesh's
size); a level that saves less than 10 % ends the chain, and small submeshes (under 16 triangles)
keep one level. Authored: nodes named `<name>_LOD1`, `<name>_LOD2`, ... become the levels of the
node `<name>` (their error is measured at cook). Skinned submeshes keep one level. At run time the
renderer picks a level per instance from its projected error, with hysteresis so it does not
flicker at a threshold, and shadows use one level coarser. Room3D's rocks
(`assets/models/rock.glb.mceasset`) draw 16× fewer triangles in its wide view.

At cook, meshoptimizer reorders vertices for the GPU cache and positions and normals are quantized.
Textures referenced by the model must be loaded before the mesh is uploaded (the samples load every
texture in the pack first).

## 8.5 Materials

A `.mcmat` asset (section 5.7) holds base colour, metallic, roughness, emissive, alpha mode
(`opaque`, `mask`, `blend`), double-sided and unlit flags plus texture references. glTF materials
travel inline with their mesh; `.mcmat` is for materials you author by hand: set it as a
`MeshRef`'s `material` and it replaces every submesh's material on that entity. The renderer has no
material editor; tint per instance is `MeshInstance::tint` (base color only) and `MeshInstance::emissive_tint`
(the emissive color, for glowing FX recolored per instance; values above 1 feed bloom).

**Clear coat, sheen, anisotropy, transmission.** A material can add a varnish layer (`clearcoat`), a
cloth sheen (`sheen_color`), stretched highlights for brushed metal (`anisotropy`) or let the scene
behind it show through like glass (`transmission`, tinted by the base color, bent a little by `ior`).
Set them in a `.mcmat` (section 5.7) or bring them from glTF. A transmissive material draws after the
opaque ones and sees only them (not other glass or particles); it is thin-walled (no thickness or
absorption), shows the background unblurred whatever its roughness, still casts a full shadow, and
draws opaque when it is part of an instanced batch. The coat reflects the sky but not the reflection
probes or SSR, and sheen has no energy compensation.

**Parallax occlusion mapping.** A `.mcmat` with a `height` texture and a `height_scale` (section 5.7) marches
the view ray through the height map, so cobbles, bricks and tiles show depth and hide behind their own
ridges at a grazing angle, with no extra geometry. Use it on large near surfaces (floors, walls, paths) set as
`MeshRef.material`, `Foliage3D.material` or `TerrainFoliage3D.material`; not on a mesh's own glTF submeshes
(glTF has no height slot) and not on `Terrain3D` (its layers blend by height already). The cost is 8 to 32
extra texture taps per pixel of such a material and nothing for other pixels, so it fades out with distance:

| Tier | Reaches | Max steps |
|---|---|---|
| Low | off | - |
| Medium | 15 m | 16 |
| High | 30 m | 32 |

Limits: the silhouette and the depth buffer stay flat (no clipped edges, no self-shadowing, the shadow maps
and SSAO see the flat surface), and a masked parallax material can differ from the depth prepass by a texel
at its mask edge. The height map must match the normal map's size, mip count and format; otherwise the
material draws without parallax and the log names both maps. The engine composes the two into one texture, so
the map costs the normal map's memory a second time.

**Wet surfaces.** `Scene3D::set_wet_surfaces()` (`render::WetSurfaces`, M-WETSURFACE) makes the frame look rained on:
at `wetness` 1 porous surfaces (rough, non-metal) lose up to `darken` of their albedo, dielectrics' roughness drops
toward `roughness`, and up-facing surfaces (ground, roofs, terrain) collect puddles: `puddles` is the coverage (0.5
covers about a fifth of the ground), `puddle_scale` the size of the noise cell in meters, and crevices in a
material's ambient occlusion fill first. Puddles are darker still, nearly mirror smooth (SSR and probes reflect in
them) and flatten the normal map. Walls darken but never puddle; metals keep their color and roughness; unlit
materials are untouched; the puddles stay in place across a floating-origin rebase. Pass the value through
`render::apply_quality(quality, wet)`: the Low tier keeps the darkening and drops the puddles (their noise is the
cost), and `r.wet_surfaces 0` turns it off. Reflection probes and the irradiance volume capture the dry scene, and
rain does not stop at roofs (interiors get wet too). The kit's `WetSurfaces` component, driven by
`Weather.wetness`, comes with the next step of the milestone.

**Snow cover.** `Scene3D::set_snow_cover()` (`render::SnowCover`, M-SNOWCOVER) lays snow on up-facing surfaces:
ground, roofs, the tops of boxes and rocks, terrain, and the cap of a sphere (metal included: snow on metal is
snow). Slopes up to about 32 degrees take it fully and none past about 57; walls stay bare. `cover` 0..1 is how
far the snowfall has got, `coverage` the share of the ground that whitens at full cover (0.8: four fifths, in
patches), `noise_scale` the size of a patch cell in meters, and `wind_bias` 0..1 how much more snow lee slopes hold
than windward ones. `wind_dir` (an xz direction, default +x) is the way the
wind blows: slopes facing that way (lee) take up to `1 + wind_bias` times the cover and slopes facing into
it (windward) down to `1 - wind_bias`, in proportion to how far they face along it; flat ground is unaffected.
Any length works (the renderer normalizes it, and `apply_quality` does too; NaN or zero length means +x). Under the snow the surface turns 0.9 white, rough (0.8) and non-metal and its normal map flattens;
snow is applied after the wet look, so it covers puddles and the rain's darkening where it lies. The patches stay
in place across a floating-origin rebase. Pass the value through `render::apply_quality(quality, snow)`: the Low
tier keeps the snow and draws it as an even layer of the same mean whiteness instead of patches (the patch noise
is the cost), and `r.snow_cover 0` turns it off. Reflection probes and the irradiance volume capture the snowless
scene, and snow does not stop at roofs. The kit's snow cover component (9.19, weather state) drives it from the
weather.

**Rain ripples.** `Scene3D::set_ripples()` (`render::Ripples`, M-RAINFX) rings the puddles while it rains: drops
land on a world-anchored grid of 0.5 m cells, `density` 0..1 is the share of cells that hold one, and each drop
lands again at a new point of its cell once per period (0.75 to 1.5 s, different per cell) and sends out two
concentric rings that grow to a radius of 0.4 m and fade. `strength` 0..1 is how far the rings tilt the water's
surface (they also roughen it a little at the crests, so the sky's reflection breaks up), `ring_width` 0..1 their
thickness and `speed` 0.1..10 the rate (2 plays the rings twice as fast). The rings exist only inside puddles: they
are scaled by the puddle cover, so without wetness or puddles (or on the Low tier, which drops the puddles) a
frame with ripples is the frame without them, and ground outside the puddles never changes. Snow over a puddle
stills it. The animation follows the scene's clock (`Scene3D::set_time`), so a paused or replayed frame draws the
same rings, and the drops stay in place across a floating-origin rebase. Pass the value through
`render::apply_quality(quality, ripples)`: the Low tier turns the ripples off (strength 0), and `r.rain_ripples 0`
does too; `enabled` false also draws none. Reflection probes and the irradiance volume capture still water. The
kit's rain ripple component, driven by the weather's rain, comes with the next step of the milestone.

## 8.6 Rendering a frame yourself

What the player does for you, for a program that owns its renderer. The pattern from
`samples/04_cubes/main.cpp`:

```cpp
// init
renderer_.init(*device_);
Forward3DDesc fd; fd.msaa = 4;
renderer_.init_3d(fd);                     // HDR, MSAA and shadow targets, created on first use
assets_.set_upload_sink(&sink_);           // cooked textures and meshes go straight to the GPU
assets_.mount(roots, mount_options);
// load textures, then meshes (load_sync<asset::Texture>, load_sync<asset::Mesh>)

// each frame
Scene3D scene(renderer_.meshes(), mem::frame());
scene.begin(camera, viewport);
scene.set_light(light);                    // likewise set_fog, set_sky, set_post, set_shadows,
                                           // set_exposure, set_cluster_grid
MeshInstance inst;
inst.mesh = mesh_key;                      // fields: mesh, world, pose, tint, cast_shadows, skinning, emissive_tint
inst.world = world_matrix;
inst.tint = tint;
scene.add(inst);
scene.add_point_light({...});
scene.build();                             // cull, sort, cascades, light clusters (CPU, deterministic)
rhi::CommandBuffer cmd(mem::frame());
renderer_.record_3d(cmd, scene);
device_->submit(cmd);
```

| Struct | Fields you set |
|---|---|
| `Camera3D` | `position`, `orientation` (looks down −Z, +Y up), `fov_y`, `near_z`, `far_z` |
| `DirectionalLight` | `direction`, `color`, `intensity`, hemisphere `ambient_sky` / `ambient_ground`, `shadows` |
| `Fog` | `color` (also the clear colour), `density` (0 = off) |
| `Sky` | gradient (`zenith`, `horizon`, `ground`), sun disk size and intensity, optional equirect `texture` with `texture_intensity`, `rotation` (radians, yaw about +Y) and `texture_additive` (texture over the gradient), `specular_ambient`, `enabled` |
| `PostFx` | `bloom`, `bloom_intensity`, `bloom_threshold`, `fxaa` |
| `ShadowSettings` | `cascades` (1..`kMaxCascades` = 4), `distance`, `splits` (cascade far ends as fractions of `distance`), `blend`, `resolution` (must equal `Forward3DDesc::shadow_size`) |
| `PointLight` | `position`, `color`, `intensity`, `radius`; a spot when `spot_outer > 0`, pointing along `direction` with full intensity inside `spot_inner` (half-angles in radians) |

Conventions: right-handed, Y up, column vectors, metres. Opaque draws sort front to back, blended
draws back to front. Colour output is tonemapped (ACES) and sRGB-encoded by the renderer.

**Animation.** `AnimationPlayer` plays a mesh's node clips: `u32 clip = mesh->find_clip("spin")`
(a `GpuMesh` method), then `play(clip, fade = 0, speed = 1)` and `advance(dt)`; `pose` (or the
free function `pose_nodes`) turns the result into per-node matrices for submeshes. Pass them as `MeshInstance::pose`. For footsteps and hit frames, keep a list of
`ClipEvent{clip, time, id}` markers and call `player.events(mesh, markers, out)` after each
`advance`: it returns the ids the step passed over, across loops.

**Skinning (optional).** A character can be a rigid node hierarchy (or a single static mesh) or a
skinned mesh with a skeleton; the engine does not require either. Choose per asset and per
instance:

| You want | Do this |
|---|---|
| a skeleton-free character | export without a skin, or keep the skin and set `"skinning": false` in the sidecar. Primitives are placed by their nodes and node clips animate them as before. |
| a skinned character | export the glTF with its skin. The mesh cooks with `GpuMesh::skinned()` true, and its clips animate the joints, which are ordinary nodes. |
| one instance without its skeleton | `MeshInstance::skinning = false` draws the skinned mesh rigidly in its bind pose. |

Drawing a skinned character is the same code as a node-animated one:

```cpp
player.advance(dt);
player.pose(*mesh, pose.span());           // pose has mesh->nodes.size() matrices
MeshInstance hero{mesh_key, world};
hero.pose = pose.span();                   // joints are nodes: the same pose drives the skin
scene.add(hero);
```

`Scene3D` builds each skinned instance's joint palette (`joint_palette()`), moves its culling and
shadow bounds with the pose, and the renderer deforms the vertices on the GPU (4 weighted joints per
vertex), in the shadow pass too. The helpers it uses are public in `render/mesh.h`:
`skin_palette` (joint matrices for a pose), `skin_bounds` (model-space bounds under a palette) and
`pose_nodes_blend` (a weighted blend of several clips, for layered or hand-rolled cross-fades). `mesh->find_node("hand_r")` finds a joint to attach a sword to:
`world * pose[hand]` is its world matrix. Meshes without skins never pay for any of this.
`samples/04_cubes` draws three skinned reeds (`models/reed.glb`, clip `"sway"`): G toggles their
skeletons at run time and `-skin 0` starts with them drawn rigidly.

**Scale (M-VISUAL 9).** Three more inputs for big scenes, all off until used:

```cpp
Array<mat4> grass;                          // keep it alive until the scene is drawn
FoliageScatter sc{.origin = {0, 0, 0}, .half_extents = {40, 40}, .count = 50000, .seed = 7};
scatter_foliage(sc, grass);                 // or your own matrices
InstanceBatch b{.id = 1, .version = 1, .mesh = grass_mesh, .transforms = grass.span()};
b.wind_amplitude = 0.3f;                    // sway: amplitude × h² metres at wind_frequency Hz
b.wind_ext = {5.0f, 0.5f, 0.02f, 0};         // optional: x wind height (sway = amplitude at 5 m: (h/H)²), y gust 0..1, z leaf flutter m
b.tint_variation = {0.6f, 1, 0.5f, 1};      // optional: each copy's colour × a mix from white toward this, by its origin
scene.set_time(seconds, dt);                // the wind's and the particles' clock
scene.add_batch(b);                         // bump version when the matrices change
Decal d;                                    // the unit box under d.world, projected down its -Y
d.world = translation({2, 0, 3}) * scaling({1.5f, 0.5f, 1.5f});
d.texture = splat_key;                      // or ~0u for a plain d.color
scene.add_decal(d);
ParticleEmitter sparks{.id = 1, .position = {0, 2, 0}, .rate = 500};
scene.add_particle_emitter(sparks);         // Forward3D keeps its particles under `id`
```

A batch is culled per instance against the view and every shadow cascade, in compute where the device
has it (`InstanceCulling::gpu`, `-r.gpu_culling 0` to compare) and then also against a depth pyramid of the
frame's other opaque draws (`InstanceCulling::occlusion`, `-r.occlusion 0`); without compute the CPU culls it
against the frustum and draws the same frame. Each instance picks its LOD level as a `MeshInstance` would
(without hysteresis). Batches draw in the main pass, the SSAO prepass, the cascades and the velocity
prepass, not in local-light shadows, reflection probes or the GI volume. Decals change the base color,
roughness and metallic of what they cover before lighting, in submission order; particles draw after the
blended draws, unsorted and unlit. `samples/04_cubes -foliage 20000 -decals 1 -particles 1` shows all three.
`samples/06_showcase` puts every high-end feature in one scene and compares the quality tiers with the
M5a frame side by side (`-sheet sheet.ppm`).

**Automatic instancing (M-VISUAL3 5a).** Copies of one mesh need no batch: `MeshInstance`s (and
`StaticInstances3D` copies) that draw the same rigid submesh at the same LOD level with the same material
(`MeshInstance::material` pointer, or none) and a bitwise-equal `tint` and `emissive_tint` become one instanced
draw per pass: in the main pass, the SSAO prepass, the velocity prepass, every sun cascade and every local-light
shadow tile. Twenty crates of one model cost one draw per submesh in each of those passes instead of twenty.
Nothing in the scene data changes, and the frame is the same pixel for pixel.

- `Scene3D::build()` groups the copies inside each material group of the opaque sort, at the place of the
  nearest one, so the list stays front to back between groups. `scene.opaque_runs()` lists the runs
  (`DrawRun{first, count}` over `draws()`); shadow passes group their caster lists the same way with
  `scene.caster_runs()`, on mesh, submesh and level only (tint does not matter in a depth pass); alpha-masked
  casters stay single there. A group longer than `InstancingSettings::max_count` (`kMaxInstancesPerRun`, 65536)
  splits into several runs.
- Not instanced (drawn singly, as before): skinned meshes, alpha-blended and transmissive materials, terrain
  nodes, copies that moved since last frame while TAA or motion blur reads their previous transform, reflection
  probe and GI captures. Batches (`InstanceBatch`) keep their own GPU-culled path.
- `scene.set_instancing({.enabled = false})` keeps the sort's order and single draws; `-r.instancing 0` (or
  `Forward3DDesc::instancing = false`) creates no instanced pipelines and draws every run singly.
  `InstancingSettings::min_count` (2) is the shortest group that is drawn instanced.
- The per-copy matrices go up each frame as transient storage data: 128 bytes per copy in the main pass and the
  SSAO prepass, 64 in the velocity prepass and per shadow pass. They share the device's geometry ring with the
  rest of the frame, so a frame's total, over all views, is capped at `min(instancing_budget,
  geometry_ring_bytes / 4)` (1 MiB and 1 MiB by default, about 8,000 main-pass copies; keep
  `Forward3DDesc::geometry_ring_bytes` equal to the device's). Each view reserves its share for the main pass first,
  then the SSAO prepass, then the velocity prepass; the shadow passes take what is left. A run past its pass's
  share draws the copies that still fit as one instanced draw and the rest singly (`instancing_overflow`), with
  the same pixels, so a long run of one mesh degrades gradually instead of losing instancing altogether. The
  showcase uses about 17 KiB; a city with 10,000 visible copies wants a 4 MiB budget and a 16 MiB ring.

The counters say why each draw is or is not instanced; they are stable names for reports:

| Counter | Where | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `runs_instanced` | `Scene3D::Stats` | opaque runs of the main view drawn as one instanced draw |
| `draws_instanced` | `Scene3D::Stats` | the main view's draws inside those runs |
| `not_instanced_blended` | `Scene3D::Stats` | drawn after the opaques (alpha blended or transmissive) |
| `not_instanced_terrain` | `Scene3D::Stats` | terrain nodes |
| `not_instanced_skinned` | `Scene3D::Stats` | submeshes of skinned meshes |
| `not_instanced_moving` | `Scene3D::Stats` | moved since last frame while the velocity prepass reads it |
| `not_instanced_single` | `Scene3D::Stats` | eligible, but no copy to share a run with (or fewer than `min_count`) |
| `not_instanced_disabled` | `Scene3D::Stats` | every draw, when `InstancingSettings::enabled` is off |
| `runs_instanced`, `draws_instanced` | `Forward3DStats` | the same, as the main pass drew them (after the budget) |
| `shadow_runs_instanced`, `shadow_draws_instanced` | `Forward3DStats` | instanced draws over every cascade and shadow tile, and the caster draws they replaced |
| `instancing_overflow` | `Forward3DStats` | runs of any pass drawn singly because the pass's share of the budget ran out |
| `instancing_bytes` | `Forward3DStats` | per-copy data uploaded this frame |

`not_instanced_single` is split, when `Scene3D::set_collect_draw_causes(true)` (off by default; the player turns it on under `-perf_capture`), by `not_instanced_unique_mesh` (no other instancing-eligible draw has the mesh), `not_instanced_short` (identical copies
exist, fewer than `min_count`) and the rest, whose mesh is shared but whose material, tint or emissive tint is not.
`Forward3DStats::draw_causes` (`mce/draw_cause.h`) regroups them for reports, adds `budget` and the renderer's own
`instancing_off`, and is what `perf_run` and `mce perf` print (chapter 25.1).

`draws_instanced` and the six `not_instanced_*` counters add up to `draws().size()`. `Forward3DStats::main_draws`
counts an instanced run once, so it falls by `draws_instanced - runs_instanced`.

**Static merge (M-VISUAL3 5b).** Instancing folds copies of the *same* mesh. A modular kit (Kenney's walls, floors,
pillars and props share one colormap) is many *different* small meshes with one material; put them under one entity
with a `StaticMerge3D` and the extract merges them at runtime into one mesh per material per cell, drawn in their
place:

- A child qualifies when it is visible, rigid (no skin, no posed nodes), has no `Animator`, `interpolate` 0, an opaque
  or alpha-masked material (no alpha blend, no transmission, no parallax) and no `ShowRenderTexture`. The others draw as
  before. A child's children merge too, whatever the child's own parent does.
- Cells are `cell_size` metres on a grid anchored at the origin; a child belongs to the cell its mesh's bounds centre is
  in. Each cell is culled as one sphere and picks its LOD level as one mesh (level k is the union of each child's level
  k, so a far cell is cheap). Vertices are re-quantized to the merged bounds (0.5 mm at 32 m), so a merged frame matches
  the unmerged one to within that, not bit for bit.
- Submeshes of one material share a merged submesh: equal `MaterialChunk` and `MaterialExt` bytes after the child's
  `tint` and `emissive_tint` are folded in, so different tints, a `MeshRef.material` override, double-sided and
  different texture sets each get their own submesh. Draws per pass: materials x cells (x 2 for a cell that holds both
  shadow casters and non-casters).
- It merges on the first extract and again when the children's (entity, mesh, material, transform, tint) hash or
  `version` changes, so editing moves are picked up and a game that moves children at runtime bumps `version`. Do not
  merge things that move: leave them as separate `MeshRef`s (an entity that moves every tick would re-merge its whole
  group every tick).
- Cost: the CPU merge is about 10 ms per 100k merged vertices (release build) on first draw and on change, and the merged
  vertices duplicate the sources' in memory (the sources stay resident for other users). `Kit3DExtract::stats()`
  reports `merged_groups`, `merged_children`, `merged_cells` and `merged_rebuilt` (groups merged this frame: 0 on an
  unchanged frame), and `perf_run` and `.mcperf` captures carry
  `merged_groups` and `merged_children`; the draw count falls by about children x submeshes
  per pass minus materials x cells. An unchanged group costs a walk over its children and a hash each frame (about
  3 ms for 24k children in a release build), nothing else; for a whole city, put each area's buildings under one
  `StaticMerge3D` and the far areas behind a `ChunkLod3D` proxy (section 4.8). Measured for M-OPENWORLD (release
  build, 24k pieces): drawn one by one they cost 15 ms of `Scene3D::build` per frame; merged, the scene sees a few
  hundred cells and builds in 0.6 ms.
- An area that streams in merges all its cells in its first frame (about 75 ms for 24k pieces, release build): a
  hitch. `r.merge_budget` (or `Kit3DExtract::set_merge_budget`) caps the cells merged per frame; the waiting cells'
  children draw on their own meanwhile and `Kit3DStats::merge_pending` counts the cells still waiting. 0, the
  default, merges at once as before; an open world wants a few dozen.
- Merging needs the mounted asset (the merge reads the cooked vertices) and a writable mesh store, which the player has.
  A mesh given to `Kit3DExtract::bind_mesh` or a read-only store draws its children one by one. A frame you build by hand
  can call `render::build_merged_mesh(sources, blob)` itself and upload the blob under `MeshStore::allocate_key()`.

Which one: **`StaticMerge3D`** for many *different* static meshes sharing materials (buildings from modular pieces,
dressed rooms, a dungeon's walls and floors). **Automatic instancing** (5a, nothing to set up) for many copies of the
*same* mesh, moving or static, and it also folds identical merged cells; **`StaticInstances3D`** for thousands of
static copies of a few meshes placed by code, with distance thinning; **`Foliage3D` / `TerrainFoliage3D`** for scattered
vegetation, which wants per-copy GPU culling, wind and LOD. Merging many copies of one mesh works but costs memory the
instanced draw does not.

**Conventions that are easy to guess wrong.**

- **Sky texture.** The equirect `texture` of `Sky` / `Environment3D.sky_texture` is a 2:1 image seen from the inside, not
  mirrored: the top row is straight up (+Y), the bottom row straight down, the middle column faces +X, and the picture
  runs left to right toward +Z. So the left and right edges meet at −X, and −Z (the default camera's forward) is a
  quarter of the width from the left edge. To turn the sky (put an HDRI's sun where your light is) set
  `Environment3D.sky_rotation_deg`: a positive angle turns the sky counter-clockwise seen from above (a feature that was
  at +X moves toward −Z), in the sky, the image-based light, the reflections and the probes alike; the gradient and the
  atmosphere ignore it, and 0 draws exactly the frame the engine drew before the setting existed.
- **HDR sky.** A `.hdr` or `.exr` (chapter 5.2) holds light, not a picture: the sun in a Poly Haven HDRI is thousands of
  times brighter than its sky, and the image-based light (`sky_ibl`: the SH9 irradiance and the pre-filtered reflections)
  lights the scene with it as is. `sky_texture_intensity` is the exposure of the texture (an HDRI that looks too bright
  wants 0.2, not a darker file), `exposure` and the tonemapper handle the rest. An HDR sky does not need a separate
  `ibl_texture`. A one-texel sun 80,000 times the sky leaves no sparkles on rough surfaces in the engine's own test, so
  there is no clamp to tune; the sun disk of the texture still lights through the image-based light only, so keep
  `sun_direction` and `sun_intensity` for the shadow-casting light.
- **Decals.** A decal projects along its entity's local −Y, so an entity with no rotation paints the floor from above. To
  paint a wall that faces +Z, turn the entity +90° about X (its −Y then points along −Z); the picture's u runs along the
  entity's +X and v along +Z. `size` is `x`, the projection depth `y`, `z`, centred on the entity. A frame draws at
  most 64 visible decals from 3 distinct textures (decals past either are dropped, the later ones first; plain-color
  decals use no texture slot). The renderer logs `visible decal(s) dropped` once when that happens; `Scene3D::stats()`
  has the count (`decals_dropped`). Share one atlas texture between decals (`uv_rect` picks the region) to stay under the
  texture limit.
- **Point and spot light shadows.** The shadow lookup is moved off the surface by one to three shadow-map texels along the
  normal, plus one along the light. With the default 512-texel tile a cube face texel is about `distance / 256` metres
  (4 cm at 10 m), so a floor, wall or sheet thinner than a few texels lets that light through to what lies behind it,
  and more so farther from the light and where the light grazes the surface. Make shadow-casting slabs at least a few
  centimetres thick for every 10 m they are from the light, or close the gap with a second thick piece behind them.

**Headless.** With no device, `MeshStore` works on the CPU so a headless run can still build the
scene and fold its statistics into the determinism hash, as the samples do under `-headless 1`.

## 8.7 3D physics

`physics::World3D` wraps Jolt behind an API that mirrors the 2D world's spelling:

```cpp
physics::World3D world;                      // PhysicsConfig3D: gravity, fixed_dt 1/60
physics::BodyDesc3D d;
d.type = physics::BodyType3D::Static;
d.shape = physics::Shape3D::box({10, 0.5f, 10});   // also sphere(r), capsule(half_h, r), convex(points), mesh(vertices, indices)
d.position = {0, -0.5f, 0};
world.create_body(floor_entity, d);
world.step(dt);                              // asserts dt == fixed_dt
world.body_state(e, state);                  // position, rotation, velocities
```

- **Characters** are not rigid bodies: `create_character(e, position, params)` (a capsule
  centred at `position`; `params` defaults to `{}`) then, each tick,
  `move_character(e, delta, params)` with gravity, jumps and input already folded into `delta`.
  The result reports ground, steep ground, wall, ceiling and moving-body flags. Characters climb
  steps up to `step_height`, respect `max_slope_deg`, push dynamic props and ride platforms.
- **Camera boom.** `camera_sweep(from, to, radius)` sweeps a sphere from the character toward the
  desired camera position against static and kinematic geometry only, so the third-person camera
  never clips a wall and is never pulled in by props or other characters.
- **Queries.** `raycast` and sphere `shape_cast`, filtered by layer mask and an exclude list.
- **Layers.** The same 16-layer `layer` / `mask` model as 2D; a pair collides only when each is in
  the other's mask.
- **Determinism.** Single-threaded, same binary gives the same `hash_state`, Debug and Release
  agree.
- **A `BoxCollider3D` is an oriented box.** It follows its entity's full world rotation (yaw, pitch and roll, through a
  parent too) and scale, around the entity's position plus `offset`; `pitch_deg` tilts it further about its local X (ramps).
  Moving or turning the entity rebuilds the body the next tick; a scale change alone does not, so change the scale
  before the first tick or move the entity too.

On the kit, the `World3D` is `kit::Physics3D::world` and the static colliders come from
`BoxCollider3D` / `MeshCollider3D` / `TerrainCollider3D` (section 8.2). Moving bodies, characters and triggers are
components too (`mce/kit/bodies3d.h`), so a scene authored in the editor or over MCP moves with no
code:

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| `RigidBody3D` | `type` `Dynamic` or `Kinematic`; `shape` `Box` (`half`), `Sphere` (`radius`), `Capsule` (`radius`, `half_height`) or `ConvexMesh` (the hull of the `hull` mesh's vertices), scaled by the entity; `friction`, `restitution`, `density`, `gravity_scale`, `linear_damping`, `layer`, `mask`. A kinematic body moves by `velocity` every tick; a dynamic one takes `velocity` once when `set_velocity` is set, and `impulse` once. `current_velocity` reads back |
| `CharacterController3D` | a walking capsule (`radius`, `half_height`, `max_slope_deg`, `step_height`, `stick_to_floor`). It walks at `move` (m/s), or with `use_input` by the Move action at `speed` relative to the primary 3D camera, and jumps at `jump_speed` on `jump` (or the Jump action) when `grounded`; gravity is the world's times `gravity_scale`. Feel fields, all off by default: below the table |
| `Trigger3D` | a `Box` (`half`, axis-aligned) or `Sphere` (`radius`) volume at `offset`: `TriggerEnter3D` / `TriggerExit3D` events for the bodies and characters whose bounding spheres overlap it; `inside`, `enters` and `last_other` read back |
| `Raycast3D` | a ray from `offset` along `direction` (entity space, forward is -Z) up to `distance`, or a sphere cast with `radius` > 0; `mask`, `dynamic` and `characters` choose what it hits. `hit`, `point`, `normal`, `hit_distance` and `hits` read back (`mce/kit/sensors3d.h`) |
| `Overlap3D` | a `Sphere` (`radius`) or `Box` (`half`, turned and scaled with the entity) at `offset` that sees every body, static collider and character capsule touching it; `count`, `enters` and `last_other` read back |

The entity's position is the body's centre (a character's capsule centre). Bodies are created the
first tick their component is there, scene-placed or spawned later, and destroyed with it; after
each step the kit writes the body's pose into `LocalTransform`. Moving the entity from outside (the
editor, MCP, a sequence, a teleport in game code) moves the body there; a moved or late-spawned
static collider is rebuilt. Game code teleports a body by writing its `LocalTransform` in
`FixedUpdate_Post` (the next tick's `kit3d_bodies` picks the move up) or with
`World3D::teleport`: a write in `FixedUpdate_Pre` lands after `kit3d_bodies` has looked, and the
step's write-back puts the body back. Simple meshes to block a level out come from `gen_mesh`. Put bodies on root entities: the pose is world space. `SfxTrigger`,
`ParticleBurst` and `ShakeTrigger` with `on: TriggerEnter` and `PhysicsEvents` see 3D triggers too
(chapter 7), and the sensors: `Raycast3D` and `Overlap3D` push the same `TriggerEnter3D` /
`TriggerExit3D` events (the sensor is `trigger`) when something comes into the ray or the shape and
when it leaves, is destroyed, or the sensor is switched off. Unlike `Trigger3D` they test real
shapes, and they never see their own entity or the entities they hang under, so an `Overlap3D` on a
child of the player senses what is around the player. Section 9.5 has the queries behind them. Code that wants its own bodies still creates them on `Physics3D::world` directly.
`samples/05_ashen_court/game/court.cpp` is a complete code-driven example with an FSM-driven enemy
roster, melee and a swept camera; `samples/04_cubes` adds a capsule walk under `-walk 1`.

**Character feel (M-MOVE3D).** `CharacterController3D`'s feel fields are off by default, so a
character without them moves exactly as before:

- `acceleration` and `deceleration` (m/s²) ease the ground speed towards the wanted one, and to a
  stop when there is no Move. `air_acceleration` replaces both in the air. 0 means at once.
- `coyote_time` (s) still allows a jump just after walking off a ledge. `jump_buffer` (s) keeps a
  jump pressed just before landing until the feet touch.
- `jump_cut` scales the rising speed when Jump is let go (or `release_jump` is set), so a tap jumps
  lower than a hold. `air_jumps` allows that many extra jumps before landing (`air_jumps_used`
  reads back).
- `sprint` (or the Sprint action held, with `use_input`) multiplies the speed by
  `sprint_multiplier`. `crouch` (or the Crouch action held) shrinks the capsule to
  `crouch_half_height` with the feet kept down, and slows the speed by `crouch_multiplier`. The
  character stands up again only when there is room overhead; `crouched` reads back.
- Touching down raises a `Landed3D` event (`entity`, `impact_speed` in m/s). `SfxTrigger`,
  `ParticleBurst` and `ShakeTrigger` with `on: Landed` play on the character's landing.

The windows are in seconds and round to whole ticks: at 60 Hz, `coyote_time: 0.1` is 6 ticks.

## 8.8 Starting a 3D game

1. `mce example Room3D` (or `mce new` and add a `Camera3DComponent`): the host is a `PlayerDesc`,
   the module only the game.
2. Author the scene in the editor or over MCP: models with `MeshRef`, lights, `BoxCollider3D`s for
   what the character walks on, an `Environment3D`, a camera. The viewport draws it (the orbit view
   frames the scene's camera) and play-in-editor runs it.
3. Write systems for what moves (section 8.2) and a headless ctest that plays the cooked scene
   with scripted input (Room3D's `tests/test_room3d.cpp`).

Much of a 3D game needs no systems at all. Enemies that chase the player around walls are
`NavAgent3D`s with `follow` on a baked navmesh (9.7); doors, drawbridges and ropes are `Joint3D`s
(9.6); a character that falls when hit is a `Ragdoll3D` with `on_trigger` (9.8); a car is a
`Vehicle3D` (9.9); what an entity sees is a `Raycast3D` or an `Overlap3D` (8.7). `samples/07_gameplay3d`
puts them all in one scene, and it was made over MCP alone: `gen_mesh` for the models (its
`humanoid` is a skinned, animated figure), `entity_create` for the scene, `navmesh_bake` for the
navmesh, `run_headless` with `inspect` to check it (chapter 14). `samples/08_movement` is the same for the
movement kit: an obstacle course for a humanoid (a lift, a coyote-time jump, a crouch tunnel, a camera that
stays out of the walls) and a race against two AI karts.

Mods can replace a model or texture by reusing its GUID (chapter 19).

Particles in 3D are the `ParticleEmitter3D` component (8.1 lists its fields): add it to an entity and the kit draws
GPU sprites at the entity's position plus `offset` (rotated with the entity, as `direction` is). A pickup's sparkle is
an emitter with `rate` 0 and a `burst`: it bursts once when the entity first appears and again each time you bump
`burst_version` (from code, a sequence or `component_set`); `emitting` 0 stops new particles and lets the live ones play
out. The 2D `ParticleEmitter`, `ParticleBurst` and `.mcpart` presets still draw in 2D scenes only. Particles are
render-side state: they are not part of the simulation or its state hash, and they are unlit and unsorted (`additive`
0 alpha-blends them). `ground_height` is a world-space y for `collide`; there is no collision with meshes.

Reference: `docs/engine/render.md`, `docs/engine/physics3d.md`, `docs/adr/0024-pbr-forward-plus-csm-m5a.md`.

## 8.9 Animation graphs

An **animation graph** (`.mcanim`, a JSON source asset; MCP `asset_create` makes one) is a state
machine over a mesh's clips. Set it as an `Animator`'s `graph` and write the parameters into
`Animator.params` (in the order the graph lists them) from game code, a sequence or MCP:

```json
{ "format": "mce-animgraph", "version": 1, "guid": "<uuid>", "name": "hero",
  "params": ["speed", "attack"],
  "start": "locomotion",
  "states": [
    { "name": "locomotion",
      "blend": { "param": "speed", "points": [ { "clip": "Idle", "at": 0 }, { "clip": "Walk", "at": 2 }, { "clip": "Run", "at": 6 } ] },
      "events": [ { "time": 0.25, "name": "footstep" } ] },
    { "name": "slash", "clip": "Slash", "loop": false, "speed": 1.2, "events": [ { "time": 0.3, "name": "hit" } ] } ],
  "transitions": [
    { "from": "*", "to": "slash", "when": [ { "param": "attack", "op": ">", "value": 0.5 } ], "fade": 0.1 },
    { "from": "slash", "to": "locomotion", "on_finish": true, "fade": 0.2 } ] }
```

- Up to 4 parameters. Clip names are the mesh's (glTF animation names), matched when the graph
  plays, so one graph serves every mesh with the same clip names.
- A **state** plays a `clip` or a 1D `blend`: the two points around the parameter's value mix by
  distance, sampled at the same phase (a walk and a run keep their feet in step). `loop: false`
  plays once and holds the last pose; the Animator's `finished` turns on.
- **Transitions** are checked in order every tick after the state advances: `from` a state or
  `"*"` (any other state), every `when` condition true (`>`, `>=`, `<`, `<=`, `==`, `!=`), and with
  `on_finish` the one-shot done. The first that holds cross-fades over its `fade`.
- **Events** raise `kit::AnimEvent3D { entity, id }` (`id` is the StringId of the name) as the
  playhead crosses their time, wrapping with the loop; the Animator counts them in
  `events_fired` and keeps the last name in `last_event`. Read them from FixedUpdate_Pre of the
  next tick or later: `world.events().read<kit::AnimEvent3D>()` (`mce/kit/anim3d.h`).
- `state` shows the state playing; `restart` returns to `start`.

Clip lengths (for one-shots and loop wrapping) and bone poses come from the mesh through the
asset system `install_kit3d` was given; without it clips loop forever and graphs do not run.

### Blend spaces, layers, root motion and IK

Version 2 of the format adds four things a character needs, all optional:

```json
{ "format": "mce-animgraph", "version": 2, "guid": "<uuid>", "name": "soldier",
  "params": ["move_x", "move_y", "aiming", "look"],
  "states": [
    { "name": "move", "blend2d": { "x": "move_x", "y": "move_y", "points": [
        { "clip": "Idle", "at": [0, 0] }, { "clip": "Forward", "at": [0, 1] }, { "clip": "Back", "at": [0, -1] },
        { "clip": "Left", "at": [-1, 0] }, { "clip": "Right", "at": [1, 0] } ] } } ],
  "root_motion": "hips",
  "layers": [
    { "name": "aim", "clip": "Aim", "mask": ["spine"], "param": "aiming" },
    { "name": "breathe", "clip": "Breathe", "additive": true, "weight": 0.5 } ],
  "ik": [
    { "name": "head", "type": "look_at", "bone": "head", "axis": [0, 0, 1], "limit": 70, "param": "look" },
    { "name": "hand", "type": "two_bone", "bone": "hand_r", "pole": [0, 0, -1] },
    { "name": "foot_l", "type": "foot", "bone": "foot_l", "pelvis": "hips", "reach": 0.4 },
    { "name": "foot_r", "type": "foot", "bone": "foot_r", "pelvis": "hips", "reach": 0.4 } ] }
```

- **2D blend spaces.** A `blend2d` state places 3 to 8 clips on a plane of two parameters (`x`,
  `y`; a strafe set, a lean set). The clips near the parameters' point mix (gradient-band
  weights: a clip has full weight on its own point and none past a neighbour), all at the same
  phase. The Animator's `playing` is the heaviest clip, `blend_clip` the next.
- **Layers** play over the state machine, in order, each on its own playhead (`layer_time`).
  `mask` lists bones; each takes its whole subtree (no mask: every bone), and only the bones the
  layer's clip animates change. An override layer pulls them to its clip by `weight`; an
  `additive` one adds its clip's motion away from the clip's first frame (a breathing loop, a
  flinch on top of any pose). `param` multiplies the weight by a parameter (0..1), so an aim layer
  fades in with `aiming`. `loop: false` holds the layer's last frame; `speed` scales its playhead.
- **Root motion.** With `"root_motion": "<bone>"`, the bone's ground motion (x, z) and turn about
  +Y leave the pose and move the entity instead: its `LocalTransform`, or the `move` of its
  `CharacterController3D` (one with `use_input` off), so collisions still apply. The Animator
  reports it in `root_velocity` (m/s, world space) and `root_turn` (rad/s); `root_motion = 2` only
  reports it, `0` leaves the motion in the pose.
- **IK** runs after the layers, chain by chain. A `two_bone` chain bends `bone`'s parent and
  grandparent (elbow and shoulder, knee and hip) so `bone` reaches its goal, bending toward
  `pole` (a model-space direction) when given; `bone` keeps its animated rotation. A `look_at`
  chain turns `bone` so its bone-space `axis` points at the goal, at most `limit` degrees from the
  animation. Their goals come from `IKTarget3D` entities (a ledge's handhold, the player for a
  head to follow). A `foot` chain is a two-bone chain whose goal is the ground under the animated
  foot, found by a ray down through the 3D physics world from `reach` metres above to `reach`
  below the entity's feet; the foot tilts onto the ground's slope, and its `pelvis` goes down so
  the lower foot reaches a step below. Every chain has `weight` and `param` like a layer.

`AttachToBone` follows the final pose, IK included, so a sword stays in a hand that IK moved.

### Animation from movement (Locomotion3D)

A `Locomotion3D` on a moving entity fills its `Animator`'s graph parameters every tick, so idle,
walk, run, jump and crouch states switch with no code (M-MOVE3D). Name the graph's parameters after
what it should receive:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| `speed` | ground speed, m/s |
| `move_x`, `move_y` | ground velocity in the entity's own frame: right, forward (−Z) |
| `vertical_speed` | m/s, up positive |
| `grounded` / `airborne` | 1 / 0 on the ground, the reverse in the air |
| `crouch` | 1 while crouched |

The motion is the entity's `CharacterController3D` (its velocity, `grounded` and `crouched`). An
entity without a controller uses its `NavAgent3D` velocity, and anything else uses the change of
its world position, so a body or an entity moved by a sequence works too.
- `smoothing` (seconds) eases the speeds.
- `animator` names the entity with the `Animator` when the model is a separate entity, such as a
  child hanging below the capsule.
- With `stride_speed` (the m/s the walk clip was authored at), the Animator plays at
  speed ÷ `stride_speed` while moving, between `min_rate` and `max_rate`, and at 1 when still, so
  the feet keep pace with the ground.

`found` reports how many of the graph's parameters it recognised. Foot chains (above) keep the feet
on slopes and stairs while the character walks.

### Clips from another character or an animation library (`mce retarget`)

A clip plays on the nodes of the model it was cooked with (section 8.4), so motion from a library such as Quaternius'
Universal Animation Library, a Mixamo export or another character is copied into your character's own `.glb`:

```sh
mce retarget Library.glb Mixamo_Run.glb --onto hero.glb --out hero_anim.glb
mce retarget Library.glb --onto hero.glb --inplace --clips Idle,Walk,Run --prefix lib_ --in-place-motion
```

The result is the character with the retargeted clips added; mesh, skin, materials and texture links are copied
unchanged, a `.mceasset` sidecar with the character's importer settings is written beside a new output, and the
animation graph (8.9) then uses the clips by name. Bones are matched through a humanoid map (hips, spine chain,
neck, head, shoulders, arms, hands, legs, feet, toes and fingers) found from the bone names: Mixamo, Unreal,
Rigify, VRM, Biped and the engine's own `hips` / `upper_arm_l` / `thigh_r` names. A skeleton the names do not
explain is refused with its bone list; give the pairs in `--map pairs.json` (`{"SourceBone": "TargetBone"}`).

The motion is carried in world space: the rotation each source bone made away from its rest pose is applied to the
target bone's rest pose, so an A-pose library drives a T-pose character and a character that faces the other way
is turned to match. The hips' translation is scaled by the ratio of the two hips' heights above the feet
(`--scale N` forces one; `--no-translation` keeps the hips at rest). `--in-place-motion` removes the steady
horizontal travel (a straight line from the first key to the last) for cycles that the game moves by code. A
target with fewer spine bones takes the top of the source's spine chain; a missing chest follows the spine.
Bones without a counterpart stay at rest or follow their parent and are named in a warning (`--strict` makes any
warning a failure). Scale and morph-weight channels are not carried, cubic-spline keys are read as their values, and
every mapped bone gets a rotation channel in every clip, so blending a clip with another does not leave a bone at
the last pose. The output is a pure function of the inputs.

Write the output beside the character: its textures are linked by relative path, and the tool warns when the output is
in another folder. A character that came from `mce content add` lives under `assets/third_party/`, where
`mce content verify` allows only recorded files, so copy the character and its `Textures` folder to your own
folder before retargeting onto it.

A rig animated through plain nodes (no skin, e.g. Kenney's Blocky Characters) has no skeleton to recognise and is
refused with that explanation. Pair the nodes yourself and add `--map-only`: `--map pairs.json --map-only` carries
exactly the named pairs as world-space rotation deltas (no hips translation or scaling, no facing correction), which is
enough to share clips between two characters built on the same node layout.

Limits: Y-up glTF only, no IK or foot-slide correction, fingers matched by name only.

### One shared clip library for every character with the same skeleton (`animations_from`)

When your characters share a skeleton (the same bone names), keep the clips in one glTF/GLB and name it in each
character's sidecar instead of merging it into every `.glb`:

```json
{ "importer": "gltf", "guid": "...", "settings": { "animations_from": ["animations/ual.glb"] } }
```

The paths are relative to `assets/` and name `.glb` / `.gltf` files of the project. At cook the library's clips are
bound to the character's nodes by bone name and cooked into the character, so the Animator and the animation graphs
(8.9) use them by clip name exactly like the character's own: nothing changes at run time, on any platform. Only the
library's active scene counts (the nodes under its scene roots), the same nodes its own cook reads. A bone the
library has and the character lacks (IK targets, controls) is skipped, and the cook says how many channels it
dropped; a clip none of whose bones match is not added, and a library whose bones match nothing is an error. A clip
the character already has wins over a library clip of the same name, and with several libraries the first listed
that has the name wins (a name repeated inside one library keeps its first). The library is an ordinary asset (a
glTF with the skeleton and the animations and no mesh is fine, though its own cook then warns "no mesh primitives")
and a dependency of the character, so editing it re-cooks every character that names it. A library does not pass on
its own `animations_from`, and a mod cannot name a library of the base pack.

Names are not enough on their own: the bones must have the same parents and rest proportions, because the keys are
local transforms. Translation and scale keys of the character's root node take the character's `"scale"` (the
library's own is ignored). The clips are copied into each character that names the library, so the pack grows by the
library's animation data per character, as it would with a manual merge. For a different skeleton (Mixamo, another
character) use `mce retarget` above.

### Editing graphs and previewing clips in the editor

The **Animation** panel edits an `.mcanim` (double-click one in Assets, or **New...**). The graph
is drawn as boxes and arrows: the start state has a blue edge, a one-shot says "(once)", and
transitions from any state come from an "any state" box.

- **States:** drag a box to move it (the position is saved in the file as `pos`, which the cook
  ignores); **+ State** or right-click on empty space adds one; right-click a state to start a
  transition from it, make it the start state or remove it. Delete removes the selection.
- **Transitions:** "Transition to..." then click the target. A transition from a looping state
  starts with the condition "first parameter > 0.5", one from a one-shot waits for its end; edit
  the conditions, fade and "when the one-shot ends" on the right. Click an arrow to select it.
- **Inspector:** the graph's name and parameters (up to four); the selected state's clip, or its
  blend parameter and points, loop, speed and events. Clip fields offer the clips of the previewed
  entity's mesh.
- **Preview:** pick an entity with an `Animator` (the selected one, or the first that uses the
  graph) and tick "Preview in the viewport": the scene plays paused. Pick one of its mesh's clips
  and drag the time slider or press Play to see it; **Run the graph** plays the entity's graph
  with the parameter values typed above it, and the state playing is outlined in green. The scene
  document never changes; untick Preview (or Stop) to go back to editing.

Every change is checked by the cook before it is kept, saved as soon as you let go and
hot-reloaded. The panel has its own Undo and Redo. A change written by MCP or a text editor is read
in while the panel has nothing unsaved.

Clips themselves come from the model (glTF); MCE does not edit a mesh's keyframes. Keyframed
movement of entities and their properties (a door swinging, a light pulsing, a platform's path) is
a timeline (section 13.5), which can loop.
