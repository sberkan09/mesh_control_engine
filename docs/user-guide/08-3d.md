# 8. 3D games

A 3D game is a scene of **kit3d components** plus a game module, played by the stock player, the
same way a 2D game is a scene of 2D kit components (chapter 7). The scene places models, lights, a
camera, the sky and static colliders; the module's systems move things and drive the camera; the
player draws it all with the physically based forward renderer (cascaded shadows, Forward+ point
and spot lights, sky, fog, bloom, tonemap, FXAA) and runs it headless, with `-capture_tick`,
`-input` scripts and `-save` slots like any other game. The editor viewport shows the same scene.

`projects/Room3D` is the small reference (`mce example Room3D` copies it out of the SDK) and
`projects/SolsticeReach` the large one: an island with a time of day, scatter, beams and a HUD,
all from a module on the kit. A program that owns its renderer (`samples/04_cubes`,
`05_ashen_court`) still works and is covered at the end of the chapter.

## 8.1 A 3D scene on the kit

The components live in `mce/kit/kit3d.h`; the editor's inspector, the cook and the MCP server know
them. Conventions: right-handed, Y up, metres; an entity's `LocalTransform` places it (its
`WorldTransform` when it has a parent), and cameras look down their local −Z.

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| `MeshRef` | draws `mesh` at the entity: `tint`, `emissive_tint` (> 1 feeds bloom), `cull_radius`, `cast_shadows`, `visible`; `interpolate = 1` draws it between the last two ticks (anything the game moves); `skinning = 0` draws a skinned mesh rigidly; `material` (a `.mcmat`, section 8.5) replaces every submesh's own material |
| `Animator` | plays a clip of the entity's mesh by name (`clip`), at `speed`, offset by `phase`, cross-fading over `fade` seconds when `clip` changes; the kit advances `time` every tick. `loop = false` plays it once and sets `finished`; `restart` replays it. With `graph` (an `.mcanim`, section 8.9) a state machine picks the clips |
| `AttachToBone` | pins the entity to a node (`bone`: a joint, a socket) of `target`'s animated mesh, plus `offset` / `rotation` in the bone's space; the entity should be a root |
| `IKTarget3D` | gives IK chain `chain` (a `two_bone` or `look_at` chain of `animator`'s graph, section 8.9) this entity's world position as its goal, faded by `weight`; `found` is 1 while the chain resolves |
| `PointLight3D` | a point light at the entity plus `offset`: `color`, `intensity`, `radius`, `enabled`; `cast_shadows` gives it a cube shadow map (granted per frame by screen importance; `r.local_shadows 0` turns them all off) |
| `SpotLight3D` | a cone along `direction` (entity space): full inside `inner_deg`, none past `outer_deg`; `cast_shadows` as for point lights (one atlas tile) |
| `ReflectionProbe3D` | specular reflections for the surfaces inside the world-axis box `half_extents` around the entity, captured from `offset` (box-projected; needs `Environment3D.sky_ibl`); bump `version` to re-capture, `baked` loads the editor's bake instead; at most four per scene |
| `GiVolume3D` | global illumination: a `probes_x` × `probes_y` × `probes_z` grid of irradiance probes over the world-axis box `half_extents` around the entity (centered at `offset`) lights the surfaces inside with the light the scene bounces (sun, lights, emissive surfaces, sky); `probes_per_frame` re-capture every frame and blend in by `hysteresis`, `version` refills them all; `baked` (File > Bake GI volume) starts from the editor's bake, and the Low quality tier draws the bake only. Keep the grid's planes off the walls; the first enabled one is used |
| `Foliage3D` | many copies of `mesh` (grass, pebbles, rocks) as one instanced batch: `count` copies scattered over the world-axis rectangle `half_extents` (x, z) around the entity at its height, each turned by a random yaw and scaled between `scale_min` and `scale_max` (deterministic in `seed`), with `tint` and an optional `material`. They sway in the wind by `wind_amplitude` × h² metres (h: height above the copy's origin) at `wind_frequency` Hz along `wind_direction`; `cull_distance` drops far copies. Culled per copy on the GPU (against the frame's depth too), LOD per copy, shadows; rigid meshes only |
| `Decal3D` | a projected decal: `texture` (empty = plain `color`) projected down the entity's −Y onto every opaque surface inside the box of `size` metres; recolors the surface before lighting (`roughness`, `metallic` by `material_weight`; a smooth one also fills the surface's normal-map bumps, so a puddle over paving is a flat mirror), can glow (`emissive`), fades on steep surfaces (`angle_fade`) and toward the box's top and bottom (`edge_fade`). Up to 64 visible decals from 3 distinct textures per frame |
| `ParticleEmitter3D` | GPU particles: up to `max_particles` sprites spawned at `rate` per second (bump `burst_version` for `burst` at once) inside `box` around the entity plus `offset`, flying along `direction` within `spread_deg` at `speed_min`..`speed_max`, falling by `gravity`, slowed by `drag`, bouncing off `ground_height` with `collide`, growing and fading from the start to the end size and color over `lifetime_min`..`lifetime_max`; `additive` or alpha blended, optional sprite `texture`. Render-side (not part of the simulation) |
| `Terrain3D` | a heightmap terrain from a `terrain` asset (`.mcterrain`: `resolution` samples per side, 2^n+1; `size` in metres; `height_range`; base64 u16 `heights` or a constant `fill`), centred on the entity. Drawn as a quadtree of chunks that get coarser with distance (CDLOD-style, skirts hide the seams); `pixel_error` is the allowed screen error in pixels (0: the quality tier's, Low 4 / Medium 2 / High 1). `material` (a `.mcmat`) and `tint` color it, `uv_tile` repeats its textures every that many metres (0: once over the terrain), `cast_shadows`, `enabled`. A terrain with splat `layers` (chapter 5.7) draws them instead of `material`, up to 8 blended by their weights, by height on Medium and High, triplanar on cliffs on High |
| `Camera3DComponent` | the view: the first `primary` one, at the entity, `fov_deg`, `near_z`, `far_z`; `mouse_look = 1` asks the player for relative mouse mode. A scene with one plays on the 3D path |
| `CameraView` | where a camera draws (M-VIEWS): `viewport` (x, y, width, height as fractions of the window from its top-left) and `order` (lower first, higher on top). Works on 3D and 2D cameras. Without any `CameraView` the primary camera fills the window as before; with one, every camera that has an enabled `CameraView` is drawn into its rect, and so is each primary camera without one (full window, order 0). Split-screen is two cameras with halves; a picture-in-picture is a second camera with a small rect and a higher order. Each 3D view keeps its own anti-aliasing history and exposure. `target` (a `RenderTexture` entity) draws the camera into that texture instead of the window. `split` 1-4 puts the camera in that player's split-screen cell (`viewport` is then a part of the cell): the cells follow the players present, so a two-player game splits in halves and reflows when one leaves; a per-player minimap or HUD camera shares the player's `split` with a higher `order`. A view covering at most half the window draws two shadow cascades at most, and one at most about a third also skips screen-space reflections, contact shadows, volumetric light, depth of field and motion blur |
| `SplitScreen` | how the window splits between the players (M-VIEWS 4); one per scene: `layout` (0 auto: two side by side, or stacked on a portrait window; three with player 1 on top; four in a 2 x 2 grid. 1 columns, 2 rows, 3 grid) and `divider` (pixels between the cells, the window's clear color) |
| `UiScreenView` | lays a `UiScreen` on the same entity out in `camera`'s view: one HUD per player (chapter 12) |
| `RenderTexture` | a texture a camera draws into (M-VIEWS 3): `width`, `height` (pixels), `update_hz` (redraws a second, 0 = every frame; a security monitor at 10), `enabled`, `ui_image` (a `.mcui` image widget of that name shows it). Point a camera's `CameraView.target` at this entity to fill it. Freed when the component goes, re-made when its size changes. A view never shows the texture it is drawing (that surface falls back to its own look), and two textures that show each other lag a frame. A 3D camera's edges are smoothed in linear color there, so a few edge pixels differ slightly from the same view in the window |
| `ShowRenderTexture` | shows a `RenderTexture` on this entity (M-VIEWS 3): `source` (the `RenderTexture` entity), `slot` (0: a `Sprite`'s texture or a `MeshRef`'s base color; 1: the mesh's emission, so a screen glows in the dark) and `intensity` (slot 1's strength). A minimap is a `Sprite` with one; a monitor is a `MeshRef` with `slot` 1 |
| `Environment3D` | the sun (`sun_direction`, `sun_color`, `sun_intensity`, `sun_shadows`), hemisphere ambient, the sky gradient and an optional equirect `sky_texture`, fog, exposure, bloom, FXAA and the shadow cascades. `atmosphere` swaps the gradient for a physically based sky that colors the sun and the ambient by the sun's height (`atmosphere_intensity`, `atmosphere_haze`; `sun_color` is then the light above the air), `time_of_day` (0..24, -1 = off) places the sun on a simple day's arc, and `volumetric_fog` replaces the fog with a height fog the sun and the lights shine through (`volumetric_density`, `volumetric_height_falloff`, `volumetric_anisotropy`, `volumetric_albedo`, `volumetric_distance`; off on the Low quality tier). `auto_exposure` follows the frame's brightness (`exposure_compensation`, `auto_exposure_speed_up/down`), `color_grade` grades the picture with a 256 × 16 strip LUT (import it with `"srgb": false`; `color_grade_strength`), `depth_of_field` blurs what lies away from `dof_focus_distance` (`dof_focus_range`, `dof_blur_range`, `dof_max_radius`) and `motion_blur` smears moving things (`motion_blur_intensity`); the last two are off on the Low tier. `ssao` darkens the ambient light in creases and where things meet the ground (`ssao_radius`, `ssao_intensity`, `ssao_power`; `r.ssao 0` turns it off). `ssr` makes smooth surfaces mirror what is on screen over the probes and the sky (`ssr_max_distance`, `ssr_thickness`, `ssr_max_roughness`, `ssr_roughness_fade`, `ssr_intensity`, `ssr_steps`, `ssr_edge_fade`; it reads the previous frame, so it shows from the second frame; off on the Low tier, `r.ssr 0` turns it off). `upscaler` (1, the default: an edge-adaptive spatial upsample; 0: bilinear) is how a frame drawn below the output size reaches the screen, and `sharpen` (0..1) sharpens the final frame; players override both with `r.upscaler` and `r.sharpen`. `contact_shadows` adds short sun shadows the cascades are too coarse for, in creases and under small details (`contact_length`, `contact_thickness`, `contact_intensity`, `contact_steps`; off on the Low tier, `r.contact_shadows 0` turns it off) |
| `BoxCollider3D` | a static box in the kit's physics world: `half` (scaled by the entity), `offset`, `pitch_deg` (ramps), `friction`, `layer` |
| `MeshCollider3D` | a static triangle mesh from a mesh asset's rest pose (use a simple collision mesh) |
| `TerrainFoliage3D` | grass, flowers or rocks on a terrain: copies of `mesh` standing on the surface of the `Terrain3D` on the same entity or its parent, `density` per square metre where splat layer `layer` is fully painted and thinner where its weight is lower (`layer` 8: everywhere), none in holes or on ground steeper than `max_slope_deg`, at most `max_count`, each turned by a random yaw and scaled between `scale_min` and `scale_max` (deterministic in `seed`). Painting the layer with the Terrain panel paints the foliage; repainting one spot leaves the copies elsewhere where they were. Drawn like `Foliage3D` (one instanced batch, `tint`, `material`, wind, `cull_distance`, `cast_shadows`). One kind per entity: put each kind on its own child entity of the terrain |
| `TerrainCollider3D` | a static height field for the entity's `Terrain3D`: the same triangles the terrain draws at full resolution, under its transform (scale included), open where the terrain has `holes`; `friction`, `layer` |

`Environment3D` exists twice: the scene's component (the first one) is what the editor draws and
what the kit copies at Startup into the **resource** of the same type, which is what the player
draws. A game with a time of day writes the resource every tick (SolsticeReach's `present.cpp`).

2D kit entities draw over the 3D frame: a `Camera2DComponent` with `pixels_per_meter = 1` and
`TextLabel`s positioned from `ViewInfo` make a HUD in pixels (SolsticeReach's `sr_hud`).

## 8.2 Game code on the kit

`kit::install_kit3d(world, &assets)` registers the components and adds these systems; the player
calls it before your module's `install`, and calling it again from the module (so tests and tools
that build a World by hand get it too) is harmless:

| Phase | System | Does |
|---|---|---|
| Startup | `kit3d_environment` | the scene's `Environment3D` into the resource |
| Startup | `kit3d_colliders` | `BoxCollider3D` / `MeshCollider3D` / `TerrainCollider3D` bodies in `Physics3D` |
| FixedUpdate_Physics | `kit3d_physics` | `Physics3D::world.step` when `auto_step` is 1 |
| FixedUpdate_Post | `kit3d_animators` | `Animator` time, cross-fades, graphs, `AnimEvent3D` |
| FixedUpdate_Post | `kit3d_ik` | the goals of the graphs' IK chains: `IKTarget3D` entities, the ground under foot chains |
| FixedUpdate_Post | `kit3d_attach` | `AttachToBone` poses (after the IK) |

Resources your systems use:

- **`kit::Physics3D`**: `world` is the `physics::World3D` the colliders live in (section 8.7).
  Add your characters and bodies to it under entity ids of your choosing. A game that steps
  physics only in some modes sets `auto_step = 0` and calls `world.step(f.dt)` itself. The world
  is not saved: make characters again when `controller_state(e, ...)` says there is none (Room3D's
  `room3d_hero` does, so a loaded save plays on).
- **`kit::StaticInstances3D`**: many static copies of a few meshes (grass, rocks, trees) placed
  at Startup by a seeded scatter, then `build_cells(16, max_extent)`. Each copy can thin out past
  `thin_distance` and vanish past `max_distance`. Not entities and not in the state hash.
- **`kit::Environment3D`**: the light the player draws this frame.

A third-person game in four systems, from Room3D:

```cpp
// FixedUpdate_Pre: the hero walks relative to the camera on the kit's physics world.
MoveResult3D last;
if (!physics->world.controller_state(e, last)) physics->world.create_character(e, feet + offset, params);
const MoveResult3D r = physics->world.move_character(e, {dir.x * speed * dt, vy * dt, dir.z * speed * dt}, params);
t.position = r.position - offset;               // the MeshRef follows (interpolate = 1)
animator.clip.assign(moving ? StringView("walk") : StringView("idle"));

// FixedUpdate_Post: the camera entity orbits the hero, pulled in by camera_sweep.
cam.yaw -= in.look.x * sensitivity;
cam_t.rotation = quat_from_axis_angle({0, 1, 0}, cam.yaw) * quat_from_axis_angle({1, 0, 0}, -cam.pitch);

// Startup: rocks into StaticInstances3D, then cells.
instances.instances.push_back(kit::StaticInstance3D{pos, scale, rot, instances.mesh_slot(rock)});
instances.build_cells(16.0f, 3.0f);
```

Without code, `CameraFollow3D` on the camera entity orbits a `target`: the pivot is the target's
position plus `offset` (1.5 m up by default), smoothed by `smoothing` (1/s, 0 snaps); the camera
sits `distance` metres away at `yaw` (about +Y) and `pitch` (above the horizon, clamped to
`min_pitch`..`max_pitch`) and looks at the pivot. With `orbit_speed` > 0 the Look action turns the
orbit at that many radians per second. These fields are off by default (M-MOVE3D):

- `collide` turns the boom into a spring arm. A sphere of `collision_radius` swept from the pivot
  keeps the camera in front of static and kinematic geometry (characters and dynamic props never
  pull it in). It snaps in when a wall comes between, and grows back at `return_speed` m/s once the
  wall clears. `arm` reads back the boom's length this tick.
- `look_ahead` leads the pivot by that many seconds of the target's ground velocity, so the camera
  shows where the target is going. Vertical motion such as jumps is not led.
- `first_person` puts the camera at the pivot (set `offset` to eye height), looking along `yaw` and
  `pitch`. `distance` and smoothing do not apply.
- `lock_on` names an entity to keep framed. The yaw turns at `lock_on_rate` rad/s (0 means at once)
  until the camera, the target and that entity stand on one line, the target in front. The Look
  action then only tilts. Clearing `lock_on` leaves the orbit where it is.

Everything is tick-driven and deterministic: the player draws moving entities between the last
two ticks, so a camera written once per tick is smooth at any frame rate.

## 8.3 The stock player's 3D path

A scene with a `Camera3DComponent` starts the 3D renderer; the 2D kit, the console and the pause
overlay draw on top. Command-line settings (chapter 16):

| Convar | Default | |
|---|---|---|
| `-r.quality` | auto | the cost tier: `low`, `medium`, `high` (`auto`: medium on phones, high elsewhere). It sets MSAA, the shadow map, render scale, and caps the scene's shadow cascades, bloom and FXAA. Saved: an options screen bound to it takes effect on the next start |
| `-r.msaa` | 4 | at most this many MSAA samples of the 3D frame (1 = off) |
| `-r.upscaler` | scene | how a frame drawn below the output size (Low and Medium tiers, without TAA) reaches it: `spatial` (an edge-adaptive upsample), `bilinear`, or `scene` (the scene's `Environment3D.upscaler`). Saved |
| `-r.sharpen` | -1 | contrast-adaptive sharpening of the final 3D frame, 0..1 (0 = off; -1 = the scene's `Environment3D.sharpen`). Saved |
| `-r.lod` | 1 | draw coarser mesh levels with distance (section 8.4) |
| `-r.lod_pixels` | 1.0 | the largest LOD error allowed on screen, in pixels |
| `-r.lod_force` | -1 | draw every mesh at one level (-1 = by screen size) |
| `-r.preload3d` | 1 | upload every texture and mesh of the pack at start (0 = on first sight) |
| `-r.uniform_ring_mb` | 4 | per-frame uniform memory; raise it if the log reports a uniform ring overflow |
| `-r.stats` | 0 | the stats line: triangles (per LOD level), draws, meshes, instances, lights |
| `-mouse_look` | 1 | let a camera with `mouse_look` capture the mouse (released while paused, in the console and when unfocused) |

At exit a windowed run prints `<game>: 3D last frame N triangles (LOD a/b/c/d), D draws` and
`N frames, M ticks, E GL errors`; tests match on them (Room3D's `room3d_walk_gpu`, `room3d_lod_gpu`).

## 8.4 Models and LODs

Put a `.gltf` or `.glb` under `assets/` and adopt it (section 5.3). The importer takes:

- triangle-list primitives with positions, normals (generated if absent) and one UV set,
- the node tree with its transforms,
- metallic-roughness materials, cooked inline into the mesh: factors, base-colour, normal,
  metallic-roughness (ORM, occlusion in R), emissive and occlusion textures, `alphaMode`,
  `doubleSided`, and the extensions `KHR_materials_unlit`, `KHR_materials_emissive_strength`,
  `KHR_texture_transform` (one UV transform per material; every map uses `TEXCOORD_0`) and the factors of
  `KHR_materials_clearcoat`, `_sheen`, `_anisotropy`, `_transmission` and `_ior` (their textures are not
  imported: a warning names them),
- node transform animation clips (translation, rotation, scale), named as in the file,
- skins, when the file has them: joints, inverse bind matrices and 4 influences per vertex
  (`JOINTS_0` / `WEIGHTS_0`, up to 256 joints per skin). See "Skinning" below; it is optional.

Not imported: morph targets, `JOINTS_1`/`WEIGHTS_1`, cameras, lights (ignored with a warning). A
file that lists any other extension in `extensionsRequired` is a cook error naming it.
Buffers must be inside the file (GLB chunk or `data:` URIs); images must be separate files that
are themselves texture assets, referenced relative to the glTF. Sidecar settings: `"scale"`,
`"max_triangles"` (a budget that makes an over-size mesh a cook error), `"skinning"` (default
`true`; `false` imports a skinned file as a static one) and the LOD settings below.

**LODs.** A mesh can carry up to four levels per submesh. Generated: `"lod_levels": 1..3` in the
sidecar simplifies each level from the base with meshoptimizer, aiming at `"lod_ratio"` (default
0.35) of the previous level's triangles within `"lod_error"` (default 0.05, relative to the mesh's
size); a level that saves less than 10 % ends the chain, and small submeshes (under 16 triangles)
keep one level. Authored: nodes named `<name>_LOD1`, `<name>_LOD2`, ... become the levels of the
node `<name>` (their error is measured at cook). Skinned submeshes keep one level. At run time the
renderer picks a level per instance from its projected error, with hysteresis so it does not
flicker at a threshold, and shadows use one level coarser. Room3D's rocks
(`assets/models/rock.glb.mceasset`) draw 16× fewer triangles in its wide view.

At cook, meshoptimizer reorders vertices for the GPU cache and positions and normals are quantized.
Textures referenced by the model must be loaded before the mesh is uploaded (the samples load every
texture in the pack first).

## 8.5 Materials

A `.mcmat` asset (section 5.7) holds base colour, metallic, roughness, emissive, alpha mode
(`opaque`, `mask`, `blend`), double-sided and unlit flags plus texture references. glTF materials
travel inline with their mesh; `.mcmat` is for materials you author by hand: set it as a
`MeshRef`'s `material` and it replaces every submesh's material on that entity. The renderer has no
material editor; tint per instance is `MeshInstance::tint` (base color only) and `MeshInstance::emissive_tint`
(the emissive color, for glowing FX recolored per instance; values above 1 feed bloom).

**Clear coat, sheen, anisotropy, transmission.** A material can add a varnish layer (`clearcoat`), a
cloth sheen (`sheen_color`), stretched highlights for brushed metal (`anisotropy`) or let the scene
behind it show through like glass (`transmission`, tinted by the base color, bent a little by `ior`).
Set them in a `.mcmat` (section 5.7) or bring them from glTF. A transmissive material draws after the
opaque ones and sees only them (not other glass or particles); it is thin-walled (no thickness or
absorption), shows the background unblurred whatever its roughness, still casts a full shadow, and
draws opaque when it is part of an instanced batch. The coat reflects the sky but not the reflection
probes or SSR, and sheen has no energy compensation.

## 8.6 Rendering a frame yourself

What the player does for you, for a program that owns its renderer. The pattern from
`samples/04_cubes/main.cpp`:

```cpp
// init
renderer_.init(*device_);
Forward3DDesc fd; fd.msaa = 4;
renderer_.init_3d(fd);                     // HDR, MSAA and shadow targets, created on first use
assets_.set_upload_sink(&sink_);           // cooked textures and meshes go straight to the GPU
assets_.mount(roots, mount_options);
// load textures, then meshes (load_sync<asset::Texture>, load_sync<asset::Mesh>)

// each frame
Scene3D scene(renderer_.meshes(), mem::frame());
scene.begin(camera, viewport);
scene.set_light(light);                    // likewise set_fog, set_sky, set_post, set_shadows,
                                           // set_exposure, set_cluster_grid
MeshInstance inst;
inst.mesh = mesh_key;                      // fields: mesh, world, pose, tint, cast_shadows, skinning, emissive_tint
inst.world = world_matrix;
inst.tint = tint;
scene.add(inst);
scene.add_point_light({...});
scene.build();                             // cull, sort, cascades, light clusters (CPU, deterministic)
rhi::CommandBuffer cmd(mem::frame());
renderer_.record_3d(cmd, scene);
device_->submit(cmd);
```

| Struct | Fields you set |
|---|---|
| `Camera3D` | `position`, `orientation` (looks down −Z, +Y up), `fov_y`, `near_z`, `far_z` |
| `DirectionalLight` | `direction`, `color`, `intensity`, hemisphere `ambient_sky` / `ambient_ground`, `shadows` |
| `Fog` | `color` (also the clear colour), `density` (0 = off) |
| `Sky` | gradient (`zenith`, `horizon`, `ground`), sun disk size and intensity, optional equirect `texture` with `texture_intensity` and `texture_additive` (texture over the gradient), `specular_ambient`, `enabled` |
| `PostFx` | `bloom`, `bloom_intensity`, `bloom_threshold`, `fxaa` |
| `ShadowSettings` | `cascades` (1..`kMaxCascades` = 4), `distance`, `splits` (cascade far ends as fractions of `distance`), `blend`, `resolution` (must equal `Forward3DDesc::shadow_size`) |
| `PointLight` | `position`, `color`, `intensity`, `radius`; a spot when `spot_outer > 0`, pointing along `direction` with full intensity inside `spot_inner` (half-angles in radians) |

Conventions: right-handed, Y up, column vectors, metres. Opaque draws sort front to back, blended
draws back to front. Colour output is tonemapped (ACES) and sRGB-encoded by the renderer.

**Animation.** `AnimationPlayer` plays a mesh's node clips: `u32 clip = mesh->find_clip("spin")`
(a `GpuMesh` method), then `play(clip, fade = 0, speed = 1)` and `advance(dt)`; `pose` (or the
free function `pose_nodes`) turns the result into per-node matrices for submeshes. Pass them as `MeshInstance::pose`. For footsteps and hit frames, keep a list of
`ClipEvent{clip, time, id}` markers and call `player.events(mesh, markers, out)` after each
`advance`: it returns the ids the step passed over, across loops.

**Skinning (optional).** A character can be a rigid node hierarchy (or a single static mesh) or a
skinned mesh with a skeleton; the engine does not require either. Choose per asset and per
instance:

| You want | Do this |
|---|---|
| a skeleton-free character | export without a skin, or keep the skin and set `"skinning": false` in the sidecar. Primitives are placed by their nodes and node clips animate them as before. |
| a skinned character | export the glTF with its skin. The mesh cooks with `GpuMesh::skinned()` true, and its clips animate the joints, which are ordinary nodes. |
| one instance without its skeleton | `MeshInstance::skinning = false` draws the skinned mesh rigidly in its bind pose. |

Drawing a skinned character is the same code as a node-animated one:

```cpp
player.advance(dt);
player.pose(*mesh, pose.span());           // pose has mesh->nodes.size() matrices
MeshInstance hero{mesh_key, world};
hero.pose = pose.span();                   // joints are nodes: the same pose drives the skin
scene.add(hero);
```

`Scene3D` builds each skinned instance's joint palette (`joint_palette()`), moves its culling and
shadow bounds with the pose, and the renderer deforms the vertices on the GPU (4 weighted joints per
vertex), in the shadow pass too. The helpers it uses are public in `render/mesh.h`:
`skin_palette` (joint matrices for a pose), `skin_bounds` (model-space bounds under a palette) and
`pose_nodes_blend` (a weighted blend of several clips, for layered or hand-rolled cross-fades). `mesh->find_node("hand_r")` finds a joint to attach a sword to:
`world * pose[hand]` is its world matrix. Meshes without skins never pay for any of this.
`samples/04_cubes` draws three skinned reeds (`models/reed.glb`, clip `"sway"`): G toggles their
skeletons at run time and `-skin 0` starts with them drawn rigidly.

**Scale (M-VISUAL 9).** Three more inputs for big scenes, all off until used:

```cpp
Array<mat4> grass;                          // keep it alive until the scene is drawn
FoliageScatter sc{.origin = {0, 0, 0}, .half_extents = {40, 40}, .count = 50000, .seed = 7};
scatter_foliage(sc, grass);                 // or your own matrices
InstanceBatch b{.id = 1, .version = 1, .mesh = grass_mesh, .transforms = grass.span()};
b.wind_amplitude = 0.3f;                    // sway: amplitude × h² metres at wind_frequency Hz
scene.set_time(seconds, dt);                // the wind's and the particles' clock
scene.add_batch(b);                         // bump version when the matrices change
Decal d;                                    // the unit box under d.world, projected down its -Y
d.world = translation({2, 0, 3}) * scaling({1.5f, 0.5f, 1.5f});
d.texture = splat_key;                      // or ~0u for a plain d.color
scene.add_decal(d);
ParticleEmitter sparks{.id = 1, .position = {0, 2, 0}, .rate = 500};
scene.add_particle_emitter(sparks);         // Forward3D keeps its particles under `id`
```

A batch is culled per instance against the view and every shadow cascade, in compute where the device
has it (`InstanceCulling::gpu`, `-r.gpu_culling 0` to compare) and then also against a depth pyramid of the
frame's other opaque draws (`InstanceCulling::occlusion`, `-r.occlusion 0`); without compute the CPU culls it
against the frustum and draws the same frame. Each instance picks its LOD level as a `MeshInstance` would
(without hysteresis). Batches draw in the main pass, the SSAO prepass, the cascades and the velocity
prepass, not in local-light shadows, reflection probes or the GI volume. Decals change the base color,
roughness and metallic of what they cover before lighting, in submission order; particles draw after the
blended draws, unsorted and unlit. `samples/04_cubes -foliage 20000 -decals 1 -particles 1` shows all three.
`samples/06_showcase` puts every high-end feature in one scene and compares the quality tiers with the
M5a frame side by side (`-sheet sheet.ppm`).

**Conventions that are easy to guess wrong.**

- **Sky texture.** The equirect `texture` of `Sky` / `Environment3D.sky_texture` is a 2:1 image seen from the inside, not
  mirrored: the top row is straight up (+Y), the bottom row straight down, the middle column faces +X, and the picture
  runs left to right toward +Z. So the left and right edges meet at −X, and −Z (the default camera's forward) is a
  quarter of the width from the left edge. There is no rotation setting: turn the picture (shift it sideways) to turn
  the sky.
- **Decals.** A decal projects along its entity's local −Y, so an entity with no rotation paints the floor from above. To
  paint a wall that faces +Z, turn the entity +90° about X (its −Y then points along −Z); the picture's u runs along the
  entity's +X and v along +Z. `size` is `x`, the projection depth `y`, `z`, centred on the entity. A frame draws at
  most 64 visible decals from 3 distinct textures (decals past either are dropped, the later ones first; plain-color
  decals use no texture slot). The renderer logs `visible decal(s) dropped` once when that happens; `Scene3D::stats()`
  has the count (`decals_dropped`). Share one atlas texture between decals (`uv_rect` picks the region) to stay under the
  texture limit.
- **Point and spot light shadows.** The shadow lookup is moved off the surface by one to three shadow-map texels along the
  normal, plus one along the light. With the default 512-texel tile a cube face texel is about `distance / 256` metres
  (4 cm at 10 m), so a floor, wall or sheet thinner than a few texels lets that light through to what lies behind it,
  and more so farther from the light and where the light grazes the surface. Make shadow-casting slabs at least a few
  centimetres thick for every 10 m they are from the light, or close the gap with a second thick piece behind them.

**Headless.** With no device, `MeshStore` works on the CPU so a headless run can still build the
scene and fold its statistics into the determinism hash, as the samples do under `-headless 1`.

## 8.7 3D physics

`physics::World3D` wraps Jolt behind an API that mirrors the 2D world's spelling:

```cpp
physics::World3D world;                      // PhysicsConfig3D: gravity, fixed_dt 1/60
physics::BodyDesc3D d;
d.type = physics::BodyType3D::Static;
d.shape = physics::Shape3D::box({10, 0.5f, 10});   // also sphere(r), capsule(half_h, r), convex(points), mesh(vertices, indices)
d.position = {0, -0.5f, 0};
world.create_body(floor_entity, d);
world.step(dt);                              // asserts dt == fixed_dt
world.body_state(e, state);                  // position, rotation, velocities
```

- **Characters** are not rigid bodies: `create_character(e, position, params)` (a capsule
  centred at `position`; `params` defaults to `{}`) then, each tick,
  `move_character(e, delta, params)` with gravity, jumps and input already folded into `delta`.
  The result reports ground, steep ground, wall, ceiling and moving-body flags. Characters climb
  steps up to `step_height`, respect `max_slope_deg`, push dynamic props and ride platforms.
- **Camera boom.** `camera_sweep(from, to, radius)` sweeps a sphere from the character toward the
  desired camera position against static and kinematic geometry only, so the third-person camera
  never clips a wall and is never pulled in by props or other characters.
- **Queries.** `raycast` and sphere `shape_cast`, filtered by layer mask and an exclude list.
- **Layers.** The same 16-layer `layer` / `mask` model as 2D; a pair collides only when each is in
  the other's mask.
- **Determinism.** Single-threaded, same binary gives the same `hash_state`, Debug and Release
  agree.
- **A `BoxCollider3D` is an oriented box.** It follows its entity's full world rotation (yaw, pitch and roll, through a
  parent too) and scale, around the entity's position plus `offset`; `pitch_deg` tilts it further about its local X (ramps).
  Moving or turning the entity rebuilds the body the next tick; a scale change alone does not, so change the scale
  before the first tick or move the entity too.

On the kit, the `World3D` is `kit::Physics3D::world` and the static colliders come from
`BoxCollider3D` / `MeshCollider3D` / `TerrainCollider3D` (section 8.2). Moving bodies, characters and triggers are
components too (`mce/kit/bodies3d.h`), so a scene authored in the editor or over MCP moves with no
code:

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| `RigidBody3D` | `type` `Dynamic` or `Kinematic`; `shape` `Box` (`half`), `Sphere` (`radius`), `Capsule` (`radius`, `half_height`) or `ConvexMesh` (the hull of the `hull` mesh's vertices), scaled by the entity; `friction`, `restitution`, `density`, `gravity_scale`, `linear_damping`, `layer`, `mask`. A kinematic body moves by `velocity` every tick; a dynamic one takes `velocity` once when `set_velocity` is set, and `impulse` once. `current_velocity` reads back |
| `CharacterController3D` | a walking capsule (`radius`, `half_height`, `max_slope_deg`, `step_height`, `stick_to_floor`). It walks at `move` (m/s), or with `use_input` by the Move action at `speed` relative to the primary 3D camera, and jumps at `jump_speed` on `jump` (or the Jump action) when `grounded`; gravity is the world's times `gravity_scale`. Feel fields, all off by default: below the table |
| `Trigger3D` | a `Box` (`half`, axis-aligned) or `Sphere` (`radius`) volume at `offset`: `TriggerEnter3D` / `TriggerExit3D` events for the bodies and characters whose bounding spheres overlap it; `inside`, `enters` and `last_other` read back |
| `Raycast3D` | a ray from `offset` along `direction` (entity space, forward is -Z) up to `distance`, or a sphere cast with `radius` > 0; `mask`, `dynamic` and `characters` choose what it hits. `hit`, `point`, `normal`, `hit_distance` and `hits` read back (`mce/kit/sensors3d.h`) |
| `Overlap3D` | a `Sphere` (`radius`) or `Box` (`half`, turned and scaled with the entity) at `offset` that sees every body, static collider and character capsule touching it; `count`, `enters` and `last_other` read back |

The entity's position is the body's centre (a character's capsule centre). Bodies are created the
first tick their component is there, scene-placed or spawned later, and destroyed with it; after
each step the kit writes the body's pose into `LocalTransform`. Moving the entity from outside (the
editor, MCP, a sequence, a teleport in game code) moves the body there; a moved or late-spawned
static collider is rebuilt. Game code teleports a body by writing its `LocalTransform` in
`FixedUpdate_Post` (the next tick's `kit3d_bodies` picks the move up) or with
`World3D::teleport`: a write in `FixedUpdate_Pre` lands after `kit3d_bodies` has looked, and the
step's write-back puts the body back. Simple meshes to block a level out come from `gen_mesh`. Put bodies on root entities: the pose is world space. `SfxTrigger`,
`ParticleBurst` and `ShakeTrigger` with `on: TriggerEnter` and `PhysicsEvents` see 3D triggers too
(chapter 7), and the sensors: `Raycast3D` and `Overlap3D` push the same `TriggerEnter3D` /
`TriggerExit3D` events (the sensor is `trigger`) when something comes into the ray or the shape and
when it leaves, is destroyed, or the sensor is switched off. Unlike `Trigger3D` they test real
shapes, and they never see their own entity or the entities they hang under, so an `Overlap3D` on a
child of the player senses what is around the player. Section 9.5 has the queries behind them. Code that wants its own bodies still creates them on `Physics3D::world` directly.
`samples/05_ashen_court/game/court.cpp` is a complete code-driven example with an FSM-driven enemy
roster, melee and a swept camera; `samples/04_cubes` adds a capsule walk under `-walk 1`.

**Character feel (M-MOVE3D).** `CharacterController3D`'s feel fields are off by default, so a
character without them moves exactly as before:

- `acceleration` and `deceleration` (m/s²) ease the ground speed towards the wanted one, and to a
  stop when there is no Move. `air_acceleration` replaces both in the air. 0 means at once.
- `coyote_time` (s) still allows a jump just after walking off a ledge. `jump_buffer` (s) keeps a
  jump pressed just before landing until the feet touch.
- `jump_cut` scales the rising speed when Jump is let go (or `release_jump` is set), so a tap jumps
  lower than a hold. `air_jumps` allows that many extra jumps before landing (`air_jumps_used`
  reads back).
- `sprint` (or the Sprint action held, with `use_input`) multiplies the speed by
  `sprint_multiplier`. `crouch` (or the Crouch action held) shrinks the capsule to
  `crouch_half_height` with the feet kept down, and slows the speed by `crouch_multiplier`. The
  character stands up again only when there is room overhead; `crouched` reads back.
- Touching down raises a `Landed3D` event (`entity`, `impact_speed` in m/s). `SfxTrigger`,
  `ParticleBurst` and `ShakeTrigger` with `on: Landed` play on the character's landing.

The windows are in seconds and round to whole ticks: at 60 Hz, `coyote_time: 0.1` is 6 ticks.

## 8.8 Starting a 3D game

1. `mce example Room3D` (or `mce new` and add a `Camera3DComponent`): the host is a `PlayerDesc`,
   the module only the game.
2. Author the scene in the editor or over MCP: models with `MeshRef`, lights, `BoxCollider3D`s for
   what the character walks on, an `Environment3D`, a camera. The viewport draws it (the orbit view
   frames the scene's camera) and play-in-editor runs it.
3. Write systems for what moves (section 8.2) and a headless ctest that plays the cooked scene
   with scripted input (Room3D's `tests/test_room3d.cpp`).

Much of a 3D game needs no systems at all. Enemies that chase the player around walls are
`NavAgent3D`s with `follow` on a baked navmesh (9.7); doors, drawbridges and ropes are `Joint3D`s
(9.6); a character that falls when hit is a `Ragdoll3D` with `on_trigger` (9.8); a car is a
`Vehicle3D` (9.9); what an entity sees is a `Raycast3D` or an `Overlap3D` (8.7). `samples/07_gameplay3d`
puts them all in one scene, and it was made over MCP alone: `gen_mesh` for the models (its
`humanoid` is a skinned, animated figure), `entity_create` for the scene, `navmesh_bake` for the
navmesh, `run_headless` with `inspect` to check it (chapter 14).

Mods can replace a model or texture by reusing its GUID (chapter 19).

Particles in 3D are the `ParticleEmitter3D` component (8.1 lists its fields): add it to an entity and the kit draws
GPU sprites at the entity's position plus `offset` (rotated with the entity, as `direction` is). A pickup's sparkle is
an emitter with `rate` 0 and a `burst`: it bursts once when the entity first appears and again each time you bump
`burst_version` (from code, a sequence or `component_set`); `emitting` 0 stops new particles and lets the live ones play
out. The 2D `ParticleEmitter`, `ParticleBurst` and `.mcpart` presets still draw in 2D scenes only. Particles are
render-side state: they are not part of the simulation or its state hash, and they are unlit and unsorted (`additive`
0 alpha-blends them). `ground_height` is a world-space y for `collide`; there is no collision with meshes.

Reference: `docs/engine/render.md`, `docs/engine/physics3d.md`, `docs/adr/0024-pbr-forward-plus-csm-m5a.md`.

## 8.9 Animation graphs

An **animation graph** (`.mcanim`, a JSON source asset; MCP `asset_create` makes one) is a state
machine over a mesh's clips. Set it as an `Animator`'s `graph` and write the parameters into
`Animator.params` (in the order the graph lists them) from game code, a sequence or MCP:

```json
{ "format": "mce-animgraph", "version": 1, "guid": "<uuid>", "name": "hero",
  "params": ["speed", "attack"],
  "start": "locomotion",
  "states": [
    { "name": "locomotion",
      "blend": { "param": "speed", "points": [ { "clip": "Idle", "at": 0 }, { "clip": "Walk", "at": 2 }, { "clip": "Run", "at": 6 } ] },
      "events": [ { "time": 0.25, "name": "footstep" } ] },
    { "name": "slash", "clip": "Slash", "loop": false, "speed": 1.2, "events": [ { "time": 0.3, "name": "hit" } ] } ],
  "transitions": [
    { "from": "*", "to": "slash", "when": [ { "param": "attack", "op": ">", "value": 0.5 } ], "fade": 0.1 },
    { "from": "slash", "to": "locomotion", "on_finish": true, "fade": 0.2 } ] }
```

- Up to 4 parameters. Clip names are the mesh's (glTF animation names), matched when the graph
  plays, so one graph serves every mesh with the same clip names.
- A **state** plays a `clip` or a 1D `blend`: the two points around the parameter's value mix by
  distance, sampled at the same phase (a walk and a run keep their feet in step). `loop: false`
  plays once and holds the last pose; the Animator's `finished` turns on.
- **Transitions** are checked in order every tick after the state advances: `from` a state or
  `"*"` (any other state), every `when` condition true (`>`, `>=`, `<`, `<=`, `==`, `!=`), and with
  `on_finish` the one-shot done. The first that holds cross-fades over its `fade`.
- **Events** raise `kit::AnimEvent3D { entity, id }` (`id` is the StringId of the name) as the
  playhead crosses their time, wrapping with the loop; the Animator counts them in
  `events_fired` and keeps the last name in `last_event`. Read them from FixedUpdate_Pre of the
  next tick or later: `world.events().read<kit::AnimEvent3D>()` (`mce/kit/anim3d.h`).
- `state` shows the state playing; `restart` returns to `start`.

Clip lengths (for one-shots and loop wrapping) and bone poses come from the mesh through the
asset system `install_kit3d` was given; without it clips loop forever and graphs do not run.

### Blend spaces, layers, root motion and IK

Version 2 of the format adds four things a character needs, all optional:

```json
{ "format": "mce-animgraph", "version": 2, "guid": "<uuid>", "name": "soldier",
  "params": ["move_x", "move_y", "aiming", "look"],
  "states": [
    { "name": "move", "blend2d": { "x": "move_x", "y": "move_y", "points": [
        { "clip": "Idle", "at": [0, 0] }, { "clip": "Forward", "at": [0, 1] }, { "clip": "Back", "at": [0, -1] },
        { "clip": "Left", "at": [-1, 0] }, { "clip": "Right", "at": [1, 0] } ] } } ],
  "root_motion": "hips",
  "layers": [
    { "name": "aim", "clip": "Aim", "mask": ["spine"], "param": "aiming" },
    { "name": "breathe", "clip": "Breathe", "additive": true, "weight": 0.5 } ],
  "ik": [
    { "name": "head", "type": "look_at", "bone": "head", "axis": [0, 0, 1], "limit": 70, "param": "look" },
    { "name": "hand", "type": "two_bone", "bone": "hand_r", "pole": [0, 0, -1] },
    { "name": "foot_l", "type": "foot", "bone": "foot_l", "pelvis": "hips", "reach": 0.4 },
    { "name": "foot_r", "type": "foot", "bone": "foot_r", "pelvis": "hips", "reach": 0.4 } ] }
```

- **2D blend spaces.** A `blend2d` state places 3 to 8 clips on a plane of two parameters (`x`,
  `y`; a strafe set, a lean set). The clips near the parameters' point mix (gradient-band
  weights: a clip has full weight on its own point and none past a neighbour), all at the same
  phase. The Animator's `playing` is the heaviest clip, `blend_clip` the next.
- **Layers** play over the state machine, in order, each on its own playhead (`layer_time`).
  `mask` lists bones; each takes its whole subtree (no mask: every bone), and only the bones the
  layer's clip animates change. An override layer pulls them to its clip by `weight`; an
  `additive` one adds its clip's motion away from the clip's first frame (a breathing loop, a
  flinch on top of any pose). `param` multiplies the weight by a parameter (0..1), so an aim layer
  fades in with `aiming`. `loop: false` holds the layer's last frame; `speed` scales its playhead.
- **Root motion.** With `"root_motion": "<bone>"`, the bone's ground motion (x, z) and turn about
  +Y leave the pose and move the entity instead: its `LocalTransform`, or the `move` of its
  `CharacterController3D` (one with `use_input` off), so collisions still apply. The Animator
  reports it in `root_velocity` (m/s, world space) and `root_turn` (rad/s); `root_motion = 2` only
  reports it, `0` leaves the motion in the pose.
- **IK** runs after the layers, chain by chain. A `two_bone` chain bends `bone`'s parent and
  grandparent (elbow and shoulder, knee and hip) so `bone` reaches its goal, bending toward
  `pole` (a model-space direction) when given; `bone` keeps its animated rotation. A `look_at`
  chain turns `bone` so its bone-space `axis` points at the goal, at most `limit` degrees from the
  animation. Their goals come from `IKTarget3D` entities (a ledge's handhold, the player for a
  head to follow). A `foot` chain is a two-bone chain whose goal is the ground under the animated
  foot, found by a ray down through the 3D physics world from `reach` metres above to `reach`
  below the entity's feet; the foot tilts onto the ground's slope, and its `pelvis` goes down so
  the lower foot reaches a step below. Every chain has `weight` and `param` like a layer.

`AttachToBone` follows the final pose, IK included, so a sword stays in a hand that IK moved.

### Animation from movement (Locomotion3D)

A `Locomotion3D` on a moving entity fills its `Animator`'s graph parameters every tick, so idle,
walk, run, jump and crouch states switch with no code (M-MOVE3D). Name the graph's parameters after
what it should receive:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| `speed` | ground speed, m/s |
| `move_x`, `move_y` | ground velocity in the entity's own frame: right, forward (−Z) |
| `vertical_speed` | m/s, up positive |
| `grounded` / `airborne` | 1 / 0 on the ground, the reverse in the air |
| `crouch` | 1 while crouched |

The motion is the entity's `CharacterController3D` (its velocity, `grounded` and `crouched`). An
entity without a controller uses its `NavAgent3D` velocity, and anything else uses the change of
its world position, so a body or an entity moved by a sequence works too.
- `smoothing` (seconds) eases the speeds.
- `animator` names the entity with the `Animator` when the model is a separate entity, such as a
  child hanging below the capsule.
- With `stride_speed` (the m/s the walk clip was authored at), the Animator plays at
  speed ÷ `stride_speed` while moving, between `min_rate` and `max_rate`, and at 1 when still, so
  the feet keep pace with the ground.

`found` reports how many of the graph's parameters it recognised. Foot chains (above) keep the feet
on slopes and stairs while the character walks.

### Editing graphs and previewing clips in the editor

The **Animation** panel edits an `.mcanim` (double-click one in Assets, or **New...**). The graph
is drawn as boxes and arrows: the start state has a blue edge, a one-shot says "(once)", and
transitions from any state come from an "any state" box.

- **States:** drag a box to move it (the position is saved in the file as `pos`, which the cook
  ignores); **+ State** or right-click on empty space adds one; right-click a state to start a
  transition from it, make it the start state or remove it. Delete removes the selection.
- **Transitions:** "Transition to..." then click the target. A transition from a looping state
  starts with the condition "first parameter > 0.5", one from a one-shot waits for its end; edit
  the conditions, fade and "when the one-shot ends" on the right. Click an arrow to select it.
- **Inspector:** the graph's name and parameters (up to four); the selected state's clip, or its
  blend parameter and points, loop, speed and events. Clip fields offer the clips of the previewed
  entity's mesh.
- **Preview:** pick an entity with an `Animator` (the selected one, or the first that uses the
  graph) and tick "Preview in the viewport": the scene plays paused. Pick one of its mesh's clips
  and drag the time slider or press Play to see it; **Run the graph** plays the entity's graph
  with the parameter values typed above it, and the state playing is outlined in green. The scene
  document never changes; untick Preview (or Stop) to go back to editing.

Every change is checked by the cook before it is kept, saved as soon as you let go and
hot-reloaded. The panel has its own Undo and Redo. A change written by MCP or a text editor is read
in while the panel has nothing unsaved.

Clips themselves come from the model (glTF); MCE does not edit a mesh's keyframes. Keyframed
movement of entities and their properties (a door swinging, a light pulsing, a platform's path) is
a timeline (section 13.5), which can loop.
