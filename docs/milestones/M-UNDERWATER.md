# M-UNDERWATER — The camera under water: absorption, fog, a wobbling picture and the surface seen from below (milestone record)

**Status:** planned (2026-11-06), steps 44a to 44f, nothing built yet. **Owner: routine 2 thread (the M-VISUAL line).** Follow-up of M-WATER (its "Not in scope" names "underwater post-process and camera-under-water look"), M-HEATHAZE and M-LENSRAIN (the same post-pass pattern), M-LENSFLARE (the prepass depth read) and the 3D visual assessment (`assessments/3d-gorsellik-degerlendirmesi.md`, item 8, post-process). Today the water plane is drawn double-sided (`cull = None`, "seen from above or below"), but a camera that dips below it sees the same picture as above, with a mirror-bright lid on top: no tint, no fog, no wobble. `water.submerged(x, y, z)` (Luau) and the audio bus effects (a low-pass "while the player is underwater") already exist for gameplay and sound; the picture is the missing half. No Linear issues (free issue limit); this record is the tracker.

**Naming:** no type, file, cvar or milestone called `Underwater`, `underwater` or `M-UNDERWATER` exists (`src/`, `docs/`, `tests/`, `samples/` checked 2026-11-06; the only hits are one comment in `mce/audio/components.h` ("a low-pass that closes while the player is underwater"), one sentence in `docs/user-guide/10-audio.md`, and the M-WATER "Not in scope" line this milestone answers; Luau `water.submerged` is a different name and stays). The kit component that M-WATER ships is called `Water` (`mce/kit/water.h`), not `Water3D`; this plan reads its render carrier `render::WaterSurface` and adds no kit component. The render setting is `render::Underwater` (in `PostFx::underwater`, look tweaks only), the pure helpers live in `mce/render/underwater.h`, the cvar is `r.underwater`.

**Goal.** When the camera is below the water plane, one post pass turns the frame into an underwater picture: what is seen is attenuated per colour channel with distance by a Beer-Lambert law (red dies first, so far things go blue-green), the farther the thing the more the water's own scatter colour replaces it (underwater fog), the whole picture wobbles gently on the screen, and looking up the surface is a lid: a bright "Snell window" straight above and a dark, fog-coloured mirror beyond the critical angle. Crossing the surface blends in and out over a short band, with no pop. The pass runs on the HDR frame **before bloom** (after the flare, before the lens rain and the heat haze), so bloom, the tonemapper and the grade act on the underwater picture. **Render-side only: nothing is written to a component and nothing is hashed.** With no water in the scene, the camera above the surface (plus the blend band), the Low tier's look aside (see tiers), `r.underwater 0` or `PostFx::underwater.enabled` false, the pass is not recorded and the frame is bit-identical to today.

**Not in scope:** sun shafts and god rays (light shafts through the surface), caustics on the seabed, bubbles, silt and particles, a separate underwater sky or fog medium (the volumetric `Fog` stays as it is), the camera-in-the-air-above-waves "waterline split" (a camera half in and half out of the surface blends over a band instead; there is no per-pixel waterline), refraction of the above-water world seen through the surface from below beyond the Snell window disc (the lid shows no real sky and no scene above it), water audio (the bus-effects component and `water.submerged` already carry that), a camera inside water volumes that are not the one `Water` plane (swimming pools, lakes at several levels: one plane, as M-WATER), a kit component for authoring the look (a follow-up if the water carrier's colours are not enough), and a Luau API beyond the existing `water.*` getters.

## Design

- **How the renderer knows the camera is below the water (no new data from the kit).** The renderer already holds the plane: `Scene3D::water()` (`render::WaterSurface`: `enabled`, `level_y` absolute, `amplitude`, `shallow_color`, `deep_color`, `depth_scale_m`; the extract fills it from the first `Water`, passes it through `apply_quality`, and `r.water 0` or a non-finite level clear `enabled`, so water that is not drawn gives no underwater look either). `View3D::position` is camera-relative to the floating origin, exactly as `water_params` turns the level into `level_y - origin_[1]` before it reaches the shader. The pass computes `cam_depth = (level_y - origin_[1]) - view.position.y` in f32 (positive below the mean surface). **The test is wave-free: the surface height at the camera's xz is the mean level**, not `water_height_at`. The waves (amplitude 0.4 m by default, up to ~2.7 of that summed) would make the exact test flicker the whole screen on and off at every swell, and the CPU does not know the drawn waves without the kit; instead the blend band (below) is wider than the waves, so the error is a slightly early or late dip that the band hides. Recorded as a limit.
- **Render setting (seam, 44a).** A POD `render::Underwater` in `scene3d.h` just above `PostFx`, and `Underwater underwater;` in `PostFx` after `haze`: `bool enabled = true; f32 strength = 1.0 (0..1: scales the whole look, 0 is off); f32 wobble = 0.5 (0..2, screen wobble amplitude multiplier); f32 fog_scale = 1.0 (0.25..4, multiplies the water's `depth_scale_m` to get the absorption length: the underwater fog distance); f32 band = 0 (0 = automatic, else 0.05..2 m, the crossing blend half-width); u8 pad_[3]` with explicit zeroed pad; ranges clamped by `apply_quality`, NaN to the default. It is `enabled` by default because it only acts when a `Water` plane exists and the camera is below it (no existing scene without water changes; the gate below audits scenes that have one). The kit does not set it (defaults), so authors tune the look through the water's own `shallow_color`, `deep_color` and `depth_scale_m`; a tool or test may set it. `scene3d.h` and `forward3d.h` are over the 150 warning, so 44a is the seam PR for both, data only, merged first, with the `include_fanout.py` numbers: `Forward3DStats::underwater` (bool) and `underwater_submersion` (f32), and the private members 44c needs (`underwater_pipe_`, `record_underwater`), the handle in the destroy list.
- **Pure CPU helpers (render, 44b).** Narrow header `mce/render/underwater.h` (math only, forward-declares `struct WaterSurface; struct Underwater; struct DirectionalLight;`), bodies in `src/underwater.cpp`; f32, fixed evaluation order, no `rand`:
  - `f32 underwater_band(f32 amplitude, f32 band_setting)`: the crossing half-width in metres, `clamp(1.5 * amplitude, 0.15, 1.0)` when the setting is 0, else the setting. Wider than the swell, so the wave-free test cannot flicker.
  - `f32 underwater_submersion(f32 cam_depth, f32 band)`: `smoothstep(-band, band, cam_depth)`; exactly 0 at or above `-band`, exactly 1 at or below `+band`, monotone and C1. This is the **surface-crossing transition**: the whole look (absorption strength, fog, lid, wobble) is multiplied by it, so the picture eases in over about two band widths of camera travel and never pops. There is no state and no hysteresis, so a replay, a pause or a teleport give the same frame; the blend is a pure function of the camera height.
  - `vec3 underwater_sigma(vec3 shallow, f32 depth_scale_m, f32 fog_scale)`: per-channel absorption per metre, `-ln(clamp(shallow_c, 0.02, 1)) / (depth_scale_m * fog_scale)`: the shallow tint is the fraction of each channel that survives one absorption length, which with the default colours (0.10, 0.38, 0.40 over 8 m) kills red in about 14 m and leaves blue-green to about 40 m, the look the surface's depth tint already promises from above. `depth_scale_m` and `fog_scale` clamped to 0.5..200 and 0.25..4; NaN to the defaults.
  - `vec3 underwater_transmittance(vec3 sigma, f32 dist)`: `exp(-sigma * dist)`, `dist` clamped to 0..1e4. (`exp` is evaluated by the CPU test over a fixed table to a tolerance, not bit-pinned: GLSL `exp` differs by ULPs per driver.)
  - `vec3 underwater_scatter(const WaterSurface&, f32 cam_depth, f32 light)`: the colour far things fade to: `mix(deep, shallow, exp(-max(cam_depth, 0) / depth_scale_m)) * light`, so it is lighter near the surface and darker with depth.
  - `f32 underwater_light(const DirectionalLight&)`: `clamp(luma(color) * intensity * max(0, -direction.y) / 2 + luma(ambient_sky), 0.04, 1)`, so water at night is dark and at noon bright (the sun's colour and the scene's ambient, nothing new to author).
  - `f32 underwater_window(f32 dir_y, f32 softness)`: the **Snell window** for a world ray direction looking up: `smoothstep(c - softness, c + softness, dir_y)` with `c = cos(48.6 deg) = 0.6614` (the critical angle of water, index 1.333); 1 straight up, 0 beyond the cone.
  - `f32 underwater_lid_distance(f32 cam_depth, f32 dir_y)`: the distance along an up-going ray to the mean surface, `cam_depth / max(dir_y, 1e-3)`, clamped to 1e4.
  - `bool underwater_frame(const WaterSurface&, const Underwater&, const View3D&, const f64 origin[3], const DirectionalLight&, f32 time_s, f32 aspect, u32 detail, UnderwaterFrame& out)`: false (the pass records nothing) when the water is not on, the settings are off or `strength` 0, the input is not finite, or the submersion is 0; otherwise fills the whole uniform content: submersion, aspect, the ray slopes `1 / proj[0][0]`, `1 / proj[1][1]` (0 for orthographic views, which are unsupported: the shader needs a perspective camera for the ray lengths), the camera's right, up and forward vectors (the world ray of a pixel for the lid test), sigma, scatter, lid colour, the wobble amplitude and phases. The wobble phases are whole-cycle: two octaves, each x and y, at 3, 5, 7 and 11 whole cycles per 64 s (the water's `kWaterPeriod`, so a wrapped f64 clock repeats exactly), turned into radians in f64 and wrapped to 0..2 pi, so the wrapped clock never pops.
- **Quality tiers (render, 44b).** `QualitySettings::underwater` (true on all tiers: a camera under the surface must not look like the sky) and `underwater_detail` (u32: Low 0, Medium 1, High 2), cvar `r.underwater` (bool, Saved, "Camera under water look (PostFx::underwater; off = the frame without it)") next to `r.heat_haze` in `quality.cpp`; `apply_quality` clamps the ranges. **Low (detail 0) is the cheap look**: a flat, uniform tint of the whole frame (`transmittance` and scatter evaluated once at the camera's depth for a fixed 6 m distance; no depth read, no lid window, no wobble), one texture tap per pixel and no prepass. **Medium (1)**: per-pixel distance from the prepass, Beer-Lambert absorption, fog, the lid and one wobble octave (a 2-tap look-up pair). **High (2)**: two wobble octaves. `r.underwater 0` removes the look entirely (the frame as today, a diver sees the plain water plane from inside).
- **Kit component: none.** The look is a function of the camera and of the water the author already placed (`Water.shallow_color`, `deep_color`, `depth_scale`), which M-WATER's extract hands over, and of the scene's sun. No `Water3D` exists (the component is `Water`), and a second component would only duplicate `Water`'s colours. **`kit3d.h` and `water.h` are untouched.** 44d is therefore the kit-side check, not a component: tests that the extract's existing feed reaches the pass inputs unchanged (see the steps). If the look proves too rigid, a separate `UnderwaterLook3D` (own header, like `HeatHaze3D`) is a follow-up.
- **Determinism.** The pass is a pure function of the camera, the water carrier, the sun, the settings, this frame's depth and the scene clock; it never feeds the simulation and nothing in it is stored. A scene without `Water` is byte-identical.

## Pass design (44c)

- **Where it sits.** `record_underwater(cmd, scene)` runs right after `record_flare` and before `record_lens_rain`: the flare is already in the picture (the flare is above water, so it is attenuated like everything else), the lens drops and the haze (a lens and air effect) act on the underwater picture, all before `record_post`. Same ping-pong as the haze: reads `post_source_`, writes `post_fx_target(post_size_)`, then `post_source_` = that target (never in place: TAA history). Skipped for render-to-texture views (`out_.linear_output`: a monitor in the scene does not wear the player's diving mask). `underwater_frame` is checked first (water off, camera above the band: return before anything is created, drawn or touched, so the off frame is bit-identical and the pipeline is only compiled on the first frame that needs it).
- **Depth.** The main depth buffer is not sampled, so detail 1 and 2 read the half-resolution opaque prepass `ao_gbuf_` (`a` = view depth, 0 for sky), through `record_prepass` (a no-op when SSAO, SSR, contact shadows, the water, the flare, the outline or the haze drew it this frame; the water already draws it for an animated surface). The prepass holds opaques only: the water plane is not in it, which the shader makes use of (below). Without the prepass pipeline (SSAO, SSR, contact shadows and the outline all off at startup) the pass still exists, binds `ao_off_` and takes the detail-0 path (flat tint), with one `MCE_LOG_WARN` per process, because a camera under the surface that shows the unchanged picture is worse than a flat one. Detail 0 never records the prepass.
- **Shader (`kUnderwaterFS`, GLSL 450).** `UnderwaterU` (bank `kPass`, a C++ mirror `struct UnderwaterU` in `forward3d.cpp` with `static_assert`s on `sizeof` and every `offsetof`, std140: only `vec4`, as `HazeU`): `frame` (submersion `s`, aspect, ray slope x, ray slope y), `cam_right`, `cam_up`, `cam_fwd` (xyz; `cam_right.w` = camera depth below the mean surface in metres), `sigma` (rgb per metre, w wobble amplitude in view heights), `scatter` (rgb, w detail tier), `lid` (rgb the window colour, w the window softness), `phase` (xy octave 1, zw octave 2, radians) = 9 `vec4`, 144 bytes (final count as built). Per pixel:
  1. `uv2 = uv + s * wobble_amp * (sin(uv.y * 38 + ph1.x) * 0.7 + sin(uv.y * 71 + ph2.x) * 0.3 (High only) , sin(uv.x * 43 + ph1.y) * 0.7 + ...)` in view heights (x divided by aspect); the arguments are small (phases wrapped to 0..2 pi on the CPU), no hash, no noise lattice, no derivatives. The frequencies are fixed in the shader, the whole-cycle clock is in the phases. Then `uv2` is clamped to the frame (`textureLod` with `linear_clamp_`), so the edge does not pull in the border colour.
  2. The pixel's world ray `dir = normalize(right * ndc.x * slope_x + up * ndc.y * slope_y + fwd)` at `uv2`; the prepass distance `d_op = a * length(vec3(ndc.x * slope_x, ndc.y * slope_y, 1))` (view depth times the ray length from the slopes, as the haze), or infinity where `a == 0` (sky or nothing drawn; one point `texelFetch`-style tap with `textureLod(…, 0)`).
  3. **The surface seen from below (decision).** For an up-going ray (`dir.y > 0`), the distance to the mean surface is `t_s = cam_depth / dir.y`. If `t_s < d_op` (including the sky), the pixel is the **lid** and the scene colour is **discarded** (the plane's own double-sided mirror shading is not used, so the water shader and its goldens stay untouched): `lid_colour = mix(scatter_at_t_s, window_colour, window(dir.y))` where `scatter_at_t_s` is the scatter colour faded by the transmittance to `t_s`, and `window_colour` is a bright shallow tint scaled by the sun's `light`, so you see a bright disc straight up and a dark fog-coloured mirror outside the cone, with a soft edge. No real sky, no refraction of what is above, no waves on the lid beyond the wobble: stated limit. Otherwise the pixel is an opaque thing below the surface (or an up-going ray hitting an object before the surface): `d = d_op`.
  4. **Absorption and fog.** `T = exp(-sigma * d)`; `colour = src(uv2) * T + scatter * (1 - T_avg)` where `T_avg` is the mean of the three channels of `T`, so the fog colour is one colour (the scatter colour) that grows with distance while each channel of the thing itself fades at its own rate (red first). Down-going and horizontal rays into nothing (no depth) take `d` = the fog's far cap (`1e4`: pure scatter colour).
  5. `out = mix(src_unwobbled, colour, s)`: the submersion blends the whole look over the band, so the crossing has no pop. At `s == 1` there is no `src` contribution from the unwobbled picture.
- **Detail 0 (Low).** One tap `src(uv)`; `colour = src * T6 + scatter * (1 - mean(T6))` with `T6 = exp(-sigma * 6)` evaluated on the CPU and uploaded as `sigma` (the shader multiplies by a constant distance of 1 when `scatter.w == 0`); `out = mix(src, colour, s)`. No prepass, no wobble, no lid.
- **Per backend.** Plain GLSL 450 like every pass; the banks the post passes use (`kAlbedo` for the source, `kNormal` for the prepass), one uniform block, no SSBO, no compute, no derivatives in non-uniform flow, explicit `textureLod`, constant loop bounds (there are no loops), no `sin` of a large argument, `exp` only of non-positive numbers, `max(x, 1e-3)` before every division, no `pow` of a negative base, no integer ops. All of that is what lets the SPIR-V (lavapipe) and the Metal and WGSL cross paths take it unchanged.

## Risks

- **Look.** The absorption lengths, the fog colour, the wobble amplitude and frequency and the window brightness are look choices only a real display can judge; llvmpipe pixels prove placement, the lid decision, the crossing blend, determinism and the off checks. Goes to the GPU list (row A17).
- **Wave-free test.** With a swell of up to roughly one metre peak to peak the camera can be a little below the true surface while the test says above (or the reverse); the band is wider than the swell by construction (`1.5 * amplitude`, at least 0.15 m) so the blend hides it, but a camera bobbing on a large swell sees the look breathe slightly. A per-pixel waterline is a follow-up.
- **No waterline.** A camera that straddles the surface shows a blended whole-frame look, not a split picture with the line of the surface across it. Recorded as a limit.
- **The lid is a stand-in.** No sky, no refraction and no waves on the lid beyond the wobble; the Snell window is a soft disc. A scene that wants a real look from below needs the water shader changed (a separate render milestone, its goldens would move).
- **Transparent things.** Particles and glass write no depth, so a transparent surface in front of far geometry is attenuated by the distance of what is behind it, and a bubble-like particle near the camera looks too clear. Recorded as a limit.
- **Existing distance fog.** The main pass fog (`Fog`, the sky pass) is already in the HDR frame when the pass runs; under water it adds to the scatter and is mostly hidden by it. A scene with heavy fog gets a greyer underwater picture. Recorded.
- **Prepass cost and availability.** Medium and High read the half-resolution opaque prepass, drawn once a frame if no other pass drew it (the water pass already does when its amplitude or foam is above 0). Without the prepass pipeline the pass takes the flat detail-0 path and says so once (a refused shader likewise).
- **Depth taps at silhouettes.** The prepass is half resolution and read with one point tap, so a thin pole against a far background can show a one-pixel fog mismatch at its edge. Recorded.
- **Orthographic views** are unsupported: the ray lengths assume a perspective camera.

## GPU verification

Headless (llvmpipe, lavapipe): off equals the plain frame exactly (water off, camera above the band, the cvar and the setting off); with the camera below, red falls faster than blue with distance along a row of equal grey spheres; the far pixels converge to the scatter colour; an up-going pixel is the lid and its colour does not depend on what is drawn above the surface; the window is brighter straight up than at 60 degrees; the same time gives the same frame, a different time another; the crossing sweep has no step above the bound; the TAA history stays free of the look; the Low tier is a uniform tint and records no prepass. Real-GPU look and cost: row A17 of `/mnt/project-files/assessments/gpu-gereken-isler.md`.
