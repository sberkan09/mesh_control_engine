# M-AURORA — An opt-in aurora for the night sky: latitude-gated, animated curtains in the sky pass (milestone record)

**Status:** planned (2026-10-23), steps 16a to 16e. **Owner: routine 2 thread (the M-VISUAL line).** Follow-up of M-NIGHTSKY (which listed the aurora as not in scope and recorded it under "Aurora deferred": "needs its own noise and animation budget and a latitude concept"), M-WATER (the water reflects the sky layers through the shared `MCE_GLSL_SKY_LAYERS` string) and M-CLOUDS (the sky pass shader and its quality switch). Today the night sky is the HDR gradient plus the M-NIGHTSKY moon and star field, and `grep -ri aurora src tests samples` finds nothing. The scene has no latitude concept at all: `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Moon` and `Environment3D` carry time, not place. No Linear issues (free issue limit); this record is the tracker. The shader step is Opus-written and merged after an independent Opus review plus a green `--task` (as M-CLOUDS 9b, M-NIGHTSKY 14d and M-WATER 15c).

**Goal.** An opt-in kit component `Aurora` describes a curtain aurora: a latitude (how far toward the pole the scene sits), a strength, a travel direction and a colour pair. Pure helpers turn the authoring, the night fade and the time into a curtain height, an intensity along the curtain and a colour by height band, and gate the whole effect by latitude, so a scene near the equator never shows it. `Kit3DExtract` hands the result to the renderer as a small `render::AuroraSky` carrier, and one shader step adds the curtains to the sky pass (`kSkyFS`) as additive, noise-driven, animated ribbons in the shared `MCE_GLSL_SKY_LAYERS`, so the water reflects them with no second copy. Luau reads and tunes it. Deterministic, saved with the scene, opt-in; a scene without `Aurora` hashes, simulates and renders exactly as today.

**Not in scope:** a real geomagnetic model, Kp index or solar-wind data (the "storm" is an authored strength and a slow deterministic pulse), aurora lighting the ground, the water or fog (the layer is sky emission only; the ground does not receive light from it, the water gets the reflection through the sky function), aurora in the IBL bake, reflection probes or GI captures (the main sky pass and the water reflection only, like clouds and the night sky), aurora under an equirect `sky_texture` (the texture wins, as for clouds and the night sky), several simultaneous curtains or per-curtain authoring (one curtain system, a fixed set of ribbons from a seed table), volumetric rays or true 3D curtains with parallax (the layer is a function of the view direction, see Risks), a southern or northern hemisphere distinction beyond the sign of latitude (the curtains sit toward +z of the camera's local north, rotated by `azimuth_deg`), sound, changing `Weather`, `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Moon`, `Water` or `Environment3D` bytes (the last is a wide header), and the sample goldens of any existing sample except the one 16e picks.

## Design

- **Opt-in component, no system, no state.** New component `Aurora` (`mce/kit/aurora.h`, one per scene, the first in ECS order is read, registered by `install_weather` next to `GroundMist`, `Moon` and `Water`; 16a confirms the registration spot by reading `install_weather`). Pure data: the gate, level, height and colour are pure functions of authoring, the night fade and the wrapped time, evaluated at extract and in the Luau getters, so nothing is carried between ticks and nothing is hashed beyond the authored fields. Authoring (all `Save`, with a `Range`): `enabled` 1; `latitude_deg` 68 (-90..90, the scene's magnetic latitude; the sign only mirrors the curtain side, the gate reads `abs`); `gate_low_deg` 55 and `gate_high_deg` 65 (0..90, the aurora is off below `gate_low_deg` and fully on above `gate_high_deg`, smoothstep between; inverted pairs fall back to the defaults); `strength` 1 (0..4, brightness gain); `curtain_count` 3 (1..6, ribbons from a fixed seed table); `base_height` 0.18 (0.02..0.6, the lower edge of the curtains as the sine of the elevation); `height_span` 0.35 (0.05..0.8, how far up they reach, same unit); `azimuth_deg` 0 (0..360, where the arc centres, 0 = +z of the world, counter-clockwise from above like `WindDirection`); `width_deg` 70 (10..180, the arc's angular width); `speed` 1 (0..4, scales the sway and the shimmer rates); `low_color` (default 0.10 1.00 0.35, the green base, linear), `high_color` (default 0.55 0.15 0.90, the violet top, linear); `night_only` 1 (0..1, 1 = scaled by the night fade, so it never shows by day; 0 lets a scene force it, e.g. for a screenshot). No engine-written fields, so the struct stays naturally packed with an explicit zeroed pad.
- **Pure helpers (kit, 16a).** In `aurora.h`, all inline, f32 with a fixed evaluation order, no `rand`, no hash of pointers, no `sin()` of an unbounded argument:
  - `aurora_latitude_gate(const Aurora&) -> f32`: `smoothstep(gate_low, gate_high, abs(latitude_deg))` through a fixed polynomial (as `moon_illumination` avoids `cos`), so it is bit-stable across platforms; 0 below the gate.
  - `aurora_level(const Aurora&, f32 night_fade, f32 cloud_coverage) -> f32`: `enabled * gate * strength_clamped * (night_only ? night_fade : 1) * (1 - 0.85 * coverage)`; the extract passes the same night fade and cloud coverage it passes the night sky (14e), so the helper itself has no scene dependency and is testable alone.
  - `aurora_wrap_time(f64 t) -> f32`: the time wrapped in f64 to `kAuroraPeriodSeconds` (every animation rate is a whole multiple of one base frequency, so the wrap is seamless, as the cloud drift, the star time and the water time are).
  - `aurora_curtain_height(const Aurora&, f32 u, f32 t) -> vec2`: the lower and upper edge (sines of the elevation) of the curtain at arc parameter `u` in [0, 1] and wrapped time `t`: a base plus two slow sine sways at fixed table frequencies, `base_height` and `height_span` clamped so lower < upper always.
  - `aurora_curtain_intensity(const Aurora&, f32 u, f32 h01, f32 t) -> f32`: the brightness at arc parameter `u` and relative height `h01` in the curtain: a sharp lower edge, an exponential fall toward the top, vertical ray striations from a lattice hash of the integer ray cell (the pcg2d family `cloud_hash` uses, mirrored on the CPU as a pure integer function) and a slow brightness pulse; an end fade at the arc ends from `width_deg`.
  - `aurora_band_color(const Aurora&, f32 h01) -> vec3`: `mix(low_color, high_color, smoothstep(0.55, 1.0, h01))` with the bright lower fringe pushed toward `low_color` (the colour bands by height: green base, violet top).
  NaN, inverted and zero ranges fall back to the defaults; a zero `strength` or a closed gate returns exactly 0 (not a small number), which is what the render off-check relies on.
- **Latitude concept.** The engine has no place on the globe, so the gate is authored on the component, not derived: `latitude_deg` is an explicit scene value. A scene that wants an aurora to appear when the player travels sets `Aurora.latitude_deg` from a script (`weather.set_aurora`), which is also the only way latitude can change at runtime; 16d's sample moves it across the gate to show the fade. No `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Moon` or `Environment3D` field is added or read for latitude. Records the choice so a later geography milestone can replace the authored value with a position-derived one without changing the helpers.
- **Render carrier (seam, 16b).** `render::AuroraSky {level, base_height, height_span, azimuth (xz unit vector), half_width_cos, curtain_count, time, speed, low_color, high_color, enabled, pad_}` in `scene3d.h` next to `Clouds`, `NightSky` and `WaterSurface`, `set_aurora()` / `aurora()`, `QualitySettings::aurora` and cvar `r.aurora` (Low is off; `apply_quality` by-value overload clamps ranges, normalises `azimuth`, NaN to 0, and zeroes `level` for `r.aurora 0` or the Low tier), exactly the shape of 9a, 14c and 15b. `scene3d.h` is a wide header (`include_fanout.py` reported 139 of 828 TUs at M-WATER 15b, under the 150 warning but a rebuild of every consumer), so this ships as its own small seam PR merged first, with the reason and the current `include_fanout.py` number in the description. `level` 0 or `enabled` false means off: the frame is the one without it. The gate and the night fade are already folded into `level` by the kit, so the shader never sees a latitude.
- **Extract feed (kit, 16b).** `Kit3DExtract::apply_environment` fills `render::AuroraSky` from the first `Aurora` (`.cpp` only; no header changed beyond the carrier): `level` from `aurora_level` with the night fade and cloud coverage the night-sky feed already computes (`1 - smoothstep(-0.1, 0.1, sun_elevation)` and the `CloudLayer` coverage), `time` is the value `set_time` gets later in the same extract (as 14e's `star_time` and 15b's water `time`, because `Scene3D::time()` still holds last frame's value), wrapped with `aurora_wrap_time`; the arc centre is `azimuth_deg`; the result goes through `apply_quality`. Nothing is set with no `Aurora`, `enabled` 0, a closed gate or an equirect `sky_texture`. The feed does not need a `Moon`: a scene with no `Moon` still has a night fade from the sun elevation (a scene with no clock has `time_of_day` -1, no night fade, and shows no aurora unless `night_only` is 0, which the guide says).
- **Sky shader (Opus, 16c).** A new `aurora_layer(vec3 c, vec3 d, float pix)` in the shared `MCE_GLSL_SKY_LAYERS` string (next to `apply_night_at`), behind `p_aurora.x > 0.0` so a frame without an aurora is bit-identical. For an up-going ray `d` (`d.y > 0`): the arc parameter `u` from the azimuth of `d` against `p_aurora2` (the arc centre and its half-width cosine), the curtain's lower and upper edge from the same sway sines as `aurora_curtain_height` (the constants shared with the kit as a copied block, with a pixel test that compares them at probe points, as 15c does for the wave table), then 3 to 4 sampled layers of the curtain along the view elevation (a short fixed march in elevation, not in distance: the curtain is a function of the view direction, so there is no ray-marched depth) with the ray striations from an integer lattice hash (pcg2d, no `sin()` of a large argument), the vertical fall-off, the height colour band mixed from `p_aurora3` (low) and `p_aurora4` (high), and the brightness pulse from the wrapped time `p_aurora.w`. The whole layer is scaled by `p_aurora.x` (level, which already holds the gate and night fade), fades out toward the horizon by the same haze the stars use (`smoothstep(0, 0.25, d.y)`) and is sized by `pix` (the angular size of a pixel, as `night_stars` does) so the striations do not alias or crawl; additive emission only. It is applied in `kSkyFS` after `apply_night` and before the cloud composite and the fog (`apply_clouds`, `vol_fog`, the horizon haze), so a cloud deck in front dims it and fog hides it, the same line that dims the stars. The water's `water_sky(r, pix, lod)` calls the same `aurora_layer` on the mirrored ray, after `apply_night_at`, so the sea reflects the curtains with no second copy (16c reads `kWaterFS` first and records how; the reflected `pix` is the reflected ray's screen derivative, as for the stars). Nothing here reads the IBL path (`sky_radiance()` is not touched). The Low tier draws no aurora.
- **PassU budget (16c checks this first).** `Forward3DPassU` is exactly 80 `vec4` = 1280 bytes (`static_assert(sizeof(Forward3DPassU) == 1280)` in `forward3d.cpp`, `MCE_GLSL_PASS_U` in `shaders3d.h`): M-WATER's `water` .. `water5` and `wave[8]` filled the last 256-byte slot on purpose, so the uniform ring does not grow per upload. The aurora needs `p_aurora` (x level, y base height, z height span, w time mod period), `p_aurora2` (xy arc centre on xz, z half-width cos, w curtain count) and `p_aurora3` / `p_aurora4` (rgb low / high colour, w speed and spare), four `vec4`, or three if the colours share a `vec4` pair with the speed and curtain count packed into the `w` components. There is exactly one spare component in the block (`water5.w`, always 0) and it is the water's, so the aurora cannot fit in the 1280 bytes. 16c therefore (1) measures how the device aligns the PassU ring range and what the 1280 comment protects, (2) grows the block by one 256-byte slot to 1536 bytes (96 `vec4`, 12 of them free afterwards), updating the three places that must agree (`MCE_GLSL_PASS_U`, `Forward3DPassU`, both `static_assert`s and their message, the offset chain) in one PR, (3) records the new size, the per-frame ring cost and the free count in the step status. Aliasing the aurora onto `p_wave[8]` is rejected: water and aurora can both be on, and a shared slot would make the frame depend on which one fills last. If the measurement shows the ring grows per upload or a backend limit is near (a UBO max range, the SDL GPU uniform push size), 16c stops and splits a "PassU repack" seam PR before the shader, instead of squeezing.
- **Why no system.** The scene time is an exact tick-quantized counter and the night fade a smooth function of the sun elevation; the curtains are smooth pure functions of both. An aurora system would only add state to hash and a way for the CPU values and the drawn curtains to drift. `Weather`, `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Moon`, `Water` and `Environment3D` bytes are unchanged by construction.
- **Luau (16d).** `weather.set_aurora{latitude=, gate_low=, gate_high=, strength=, curtain_count=, base_height=, height_span=, azimuth=, width=, speed=, low_color={r,g,b}, high_color={r,g,b}, night_only=, enabled=}`, the authoring getters, and read-only `weather.aurora_gate()` and `weather.aurora_level()` from the same helpers (the level uses the night fade of the current clock and the cloud coverage, and 0 with no clock unless `night_only` is 0); named errors, values clamped to the Range, nothing written on error, a missing `Aurora` raises (the setter never adds one), typed in `definitions.cpp`, same rules as `weather.set_moon`. The read-only queries and the authoring live in one place: the `weather` table, next to `set_moon`. 16d records whether a separate `aurora` table would fit better (as 15d chose `water` because the weather table was full of unrelated follow-ups); the default is the `weather` table because the aurora is a night-sky layer like the moon, and the sample's script needs both the clock and the latitude. The sample: `samples/12_weather` already carries a `Moon` and a clock, so it gets an `Aurora` on the Weather entity (entity count unchanged) and a script step that moves `latitude` across the gate, logging `aurora:` every 150 ticks, if 16d finds its night frames already in a golden and the golden move is small; otherwise a night capture of `samples/13_water` (the sea reflects the curtains) carries it. 16d decides and records, and only the chosen sample's goldens and log expectations move.
- **Determinism and hash.** A scene without `Aurora` is byte-identical (one empty query in the extract; the extract is not part of the state hash). A scene with it differs only in the saved component and in frames. The gate, level, height and colour depend only on authoring, the night fade, the cloud coverage and the wrapped time, so two runs agree, a save/load gives the same values, and a test evaluates the helpers at fixed ticks in one go and in two halves. The GPU pattern is stable over long runs (time wrapped in f64 on the CPU to a period every rate divides).

## Gates
- A scene without `Aurora` (or with `enabled` 0, a closed latitude gate, no night or a zero level) has an unchanged state hash and unchanged frames; every existing sample hash and golden stays byte-identical until 16d changes the chosen sample, and only that sample's expectations move. Existing goldens under `tests/render_gl/*` stay byte-identical through 16b and 16c on both backends.
- `Weather`, `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Moon`, `Water`, `CloudLayer` and `Environment3D` are never written by aurora code: the extract changes only its local `render::AuroraSky`; a test hashes the resources and the `Weather` column with and without `Aurora`.
- Gate, level, curtain height and colour are pure functions of authoring, night fade, cloud coverage and time; no stored float, no wall clock, no `rand`. The shader and the kit helper agree (pixel test of 16c).
- PassU: the size after 16c is recorded, the five places agree (`MCE_GLSL_PASS_U`, `Forward3DPassU`, the offset chain, both `static_assert`s), it stays a whole number of 256-byte slots, and no other pass's block changes.
- New goldens `aurora_sky` and `aurora_water` with pixel tests as in 16c; a night at coverage 1 (overcast) shows a dimmer aurora than a clear one.
- Include fan-out: the only wide header touched is `scene3d.h`, in the 16b seam PR (checked with `scripts/ci/include_fanout.py`, 139 of 828 TUs at M-WATER 15b); `aurora.h` is included only by the extract, the install, the script binding and the tests; `kit3d.h`, `weather.h`, `world_clock.h`, `day_cycle.h` and `moon.h` are not edited.
- Slow tests: an aurora GPU test over 60 s ships a smoke variant (one frame, one scene) that `--task` runs and the long-time sweep is labelled `slow` with `mce_mark_slow(<test>)`; the sample's added `aurora:` run does not lengthen its headless test.
- Luau: setters clamp, bad types raise named errors and write nothing.

## Risks
- **Main shader risk: the look and the cost.** Curtains are a noise-driven layer drawn over every up-going sky pixel; 3 to 4 elevation samples with an integer hash each is cheap next to the cloud layer but it runs on the whole sky, so 16c measures with `perf_run` at 1080p and caps the sample count by tier (Low is off). Because the layer is a function of the view direction there is no parallax and no depth: moving the camera does not move the curtains (like the stars and the moon). The guide says so; a true 3D curtain is a separate follow-up.
- **PassU is full.** Any new `vec4` breaks the 1280-byte alignment that M-WATER 15c chose; the plan grows the block by a whole 256-byte slot and measures the ring, with a repack PR as the fallback. The risk is a backend limit or a per-upload ring growth nobody has measured; step one of 16c is the measurement, and it records the answer even if nothing needs to change.
- **Aliasing and crawl.** Thin ray striations minified below a pixel crawl when the camera turns. 16c sizes the striation frequency by the pixel footprint (`pix`), fades it out when it drops under a pixel, and includes the one-degree yaw frame-difference test; if it still crawls the striation contrast is capped and recorded.
- **Latitude is authored, not derived.** There is no globe in the engine, so a "gated by latitude" aurora can be wrong for a scene whose geography says otherwise; the guide says the value is the author's, and a later geography milestone can feed it. The gate has a smooth ramp so a script that moves the latitude never pops.
- **Interaction with the moon, stars, clouds and fog.** The curtains must sit in front of the stars and behind the cloud composite and the fog; a mis-ordered line would show them through a deck or through fog, or hide the stars under a bright ribbon that should be additive. Tests cover clouds and fog, and the ordering is part of the Opus review checklist. A full moon near the arc washes the curtains out slightly (the moon halo adds to the same pixels); this is physical enough and not special-cased.
- **Water reflection and the draw order.** The water shader calls `aurora_layer`, so a change to the function changes `kWaterFS` too. Every `water_*` golden must stay byte-identical with the aurora off, which the off-check in `water_sky` guarantees by construction; the reviewer checks the call is behind it.
- **Dependence on the night fade and the clock.** A scene with no clock has `time_of_day` -1 and no night fade, so with `night_only` 1 it shows no aurora; the guide says to add a clock or set `night_only` 0, and the Luau setter's help text repeats it.
