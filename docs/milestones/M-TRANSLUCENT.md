# M-TRANSLUCENT — Thin-surface translucency: leaves, grass, paper, wax and ears lit from behind glow with their own colour, plus wrap lighting for skin-like soft terminators (milestone record)

**Status:** planned (2026-11-05), steps 41a to 41h. Real-GPU checks (look, cost, Metal, WebGPU) go to the GPU backlog as row A14 of `/mnt/project-files/assessments/gpu-gereken-isler.md`. **Owner: routine 2 thread (the M-VISUAL line).** Follow-up of M-VISUAL2 4 (the material lobes: clear coat, sheen, anisotropy, transmission and ior, and the way they reached the GPU), M-VISUAL3 (whose "Not in scope" names "subsurface scattering" as not taken, `docs/milestones/M-VISUAL3.md` lines 27 and 644, and `docs/ROADMAP.md` line 781), M-LIGHTCOOKIE (the same opt-in, render-side pattern and the same lit-shader loop) and the 3D visual assessment (`assessments/3d-gorsellik-degerlendirmesi.md`: the engine's strength is stylized and indie 3D, where foliage is most of the frame). Today a leaf card, a blade of grass, a paper screen, a wax candle or an ear lit from behind is black on its shadowed side apart from the ambient, because every lit term in `MCE_GLSL_MESH_FS` is gated by `n·l > 0` (`direct`, `direct_ext`) and the sun's shadow is not even looked up where `n·l <= 0`; the terminator of a character's face is the hard Lambert edge. No Linear issues: free limit; this record is the tracker. The shader steps (41d, 41e) are Opus-written and merged after an independent Opus review plus a green `--task`, as M-LIGHTCOOKIE 40c to 40f, M-OUTLINE 39c and M-LENSFLARE 38c were.

**Why this one (2026-11-05 survey).** Checked against `src/`, `docs/`, `tests/` and `samples/` with grep before choosing:

- No `translucen`, `subsurface`, `diffuse_transmission`, `wrap_diffuse`, `backlit` or `thin_surface` in the render, asset or kit code; the only hit of `translucent` in `src/` is a 2D veil comment in `src/player/src/player.cpp`. Subsurface scattering is the one lighting gap the visual milestones list as "not taken".
- The material already has four lobes and transmission (`asset::MaterialExt` in `mce/asset/formats.h`, 64 bytes, `static_assert`ed, every byte in use: clear coat 2, sheen 4, anisotropy 2, transmission 1, ior 1, the parallax height GUID 4 and scale and steps 2), cooked from `.mcmat` keys (`material_importer.cpp`) and from glTF `KHR_materials_clearcoat` / `_sheen` / `_anisotropy` / `_transmission` / `_ior` (`gltf_importer.cpp`, factors only), carried per submesh (`GpuSubmesh::material_ext`, `mesh.h`) or by a `.mcmat` override (`render::MaterialOverride::ext`, `scene3d.h`), and turned into `ObjectU::coat` / `sheen` / `ext` by `material_ext_uniforms` (`forward3d.cpp`). `KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission` and `KHR_materials_volume` are not in `kSupportedExtensions`.
- Transmission (`o_ext.z`) is the refractive glass lobe: it replaces the diffuse with the blurred opaque frame behind and draws after the opaques. It does not light a leaf from the sun behind it, and an opaque or alpha-masked leaf never takes that path.
- The foliage paths that would show it most (`Foliage3D`, `TerrainFoliage3D`, the instanced batches with wind sway, gust and flutter, M-VISUAL 9, M-VISUAL3 6, M-WINDSWAY, M-WINDGUST) take their material from the mesh or from a `.mcmat` (`Foliage3D::material`), so a material property reaches them with no kit change. M-SEASONS tints foliage through `tint`, M-SNOWCOVER whitens up-facing surfaces in the shader (`snow_mask`), M-WETSURFACE darkens the albedo: all three change `base` before lighting, so a translucent term built from `base` follows them (the snow also needs to damp it, see Design).
- Of the remaining candidates the survey of M-OUTLINE named (water caustics, heat haze, bokeh shapes, planar mirrors), translucency has the widest visible effect per unit of risk: it changes every back-lit leaf of every outdoor scene, it needs **no new texture, no new bank and no new pass** (one free float of the per-draw record carries it, see Design), and the per-pixel work sits behind a per-draw uniform branch.

**Naming:** no type, file, shader symbol, cvar or milestone called `Translucency`, `translucency`, `Translucent`, `wrap_diffuse`, `thin_surface` or `M-TRANSLUCENT` exists (`src/`, `docs/`, `tests/`, `samples/` checked with grep 2026-11-05; the `player.cpp` veil comment is unrelated). The cooked bits are `MaterialFlags::TranslucencyMask` / `WrapMask` (in `formats.h`), the asset accessors live in `mce/asset/material_translucency.h`, the pure shading helpers in `mce/render/translucency.h`, the `.mcmat` keys are `translucency` and `wrap`, the cvar is `r.translucency`.

**Goal.** Two opt-in material properties, both 0 (off) by default:

- **`translucency` 0..1 (thin-surface diffuse transmission).** A share of the diffuse goes through the surface: light arriving at the back face leaves the front face tinted by the surface's own colour (the base colour after tint, seasons, wetness and snow), as a Lambert lobe around the inverted normal plus a small forward-scatter lobe toward the light (the glow when looking into the sun through a leaf). The reflected diffuse is reduced by the same share, as glTF `KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission` mixes the two, so a translucent leaf lit from the front is not brighter than before. It acts for the sun (with its own shadow lookup from the light's side, so a leaf behind another leaf stays dark), for point and spot lights in the Forward+ loop (a paper lantern glows from the lamp inside), and on Medium and High for the ambient seen through the back.
- **`wrap` 0..1 (wrap lighting).** The diffuse of the sun and the local lights wraps past the terminator with Filament's energy-conserving form, `max(n·l + w, 0) / (1 + w)²`, so skin, wax, cloth and fruit get a soft terminator. Specular and the clear coat are unchanged.

Both are "thin" approximations: no thickness, no scattering radius, no blur. They are cheap and they look right on surfaces that are thin or seen as thin (leaves, grass, petals, paper, lamp shades, ears, fingers, a candle's rim); a thick object with `translucency` glows as if it were a shell.

**Render-side only: nothing is written to a component and nothing is hashed.** With no material carrying either property, `r.translucency 0`, the tier switch off or `Forward3DDesc::translucency` false, every draw's record keeps the value 0 it has today, the shader takes the not-taken branch (the expressions of today) and every frame and golden is bit-identical.

**Not in scope:** real subsurface scattering (screen-space diffusion, a scattering profile, a thickness pre-pass or baked thickness maps; see Follow-ups), a transmission colour separate from the base colour (`KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission`'s `diffuseTransmissionColorFactor`: one colour field does not fit, see Design), the extension's textures (`diffuseTransmissionTexture` and the colour texture: textures of the other lobes are not cooked either), translucency of terrain splat layers (the terrain has its own `TerrainU`; grass on a terrain is a `TerrainFoliage3D` batch, which gets it), of particles, decals and water, translucency in the volumetric light injection (the fog has no surface), a per-entity override component (Design, Kit), the FBX and OBJ importers (glTF and `.mcmat` only), a Luau API, the MCP `render_frame` `environment` override and the editor viewport's own preview path.

## Design

- **Where the two values live (the load-bearing decision; checked 2026-11-05).**

  *The cooked material.* `asset::MaterialExt` has no spare byte (64 bytes, all named, `static_assert`), and growing it changes the stride of the per-submesh array in every cooked mesh (`MeshSubmesh::reserved2[0]` offsets, the reader's `span_at(..., sizeof(MaterialExt))` check in `formats.cpp`) and the `.mcmat` chunk layout, which means a chunk minor bump, a versioned reader and a migration (M-FORMATVER, M-SAVECOMPAT territory) for two scalars. `asset::MaterialChunk::flags` is a `u32` of which bits 0 to 5 are used (`AlphaBlend` to `OcclusionOnly`), no reader rejects unknown bits (`MaterialChunk::read` checks the size only; `grep` of `flags & ~` in `src/engine/asset` and `render` finds none for materials), and every material cooked so far has bits 8 to 31 zero. So the two values are **quantized into the existing flags word**: bits 8..15 `translucency` (0..255 for 0..1), bits 16..23 `wrap` (0..255 for 0..1); bits 6, 7 and 24..31 stay free. Every existing asset reads as 0, 0 (off), the chunk sizes, the mesh layout and the cook format version are unchanged, and the bits travel wherever a `MaterialChunk` already travels: the per-submesh material of a cooked mesh, a `.mcmat` override (`MaterialOverride::params`, copied whole by the kit extract), `StaticMerge3D` (which groups by material, so a translucent submesh stays its own group: 41c checks the key compares `flags`) and the impostor bake (which writes its own material: Risks). Eight bits are plenty for an art-direction factor (steps of 0.4 percent).

  *The GPU record.* The per-draw material record is the std140 `ObjectU` (bank `kObject` = 3; `forward3d.cpp` and `MCE_GLSL_OBJECT_U` in `src/engine/render/src/shaders3d.h`): 23 `vec4` = 368 bytes, in a ring of `kUniformAlignment` = 256-byte slots (512 per draw). Its members are all in use except one float: `o_ext.w` is the transmission pass's flag (set at `forward3d.cpp` 2937), `o_pom` is full, and **`o_skin.w` is free** (`o_skin` = first palette joint, first visible instance slot, time; every fill writes `w` = 0: `forward3d.cpp` 2929 and 3175, checked). 41d puts the resolved pair there as one exact small integer, `o_skin.w = t8 + 256 * w8` (both 0..255 after the tier and the cvar; integers up to 65535 are exact in f32, and the shader decodes them with `mod` and `floor` of a power of two, exactly). 0 means off. No new bank, no new uniform block, no new sampler, no `ObjectU` size change. *Fallback, if 41d finds a fill site that writes `o_skin.w`:* a 24th `vec4 o_thin` in `ObjectU` (384 bytes, still one 512-byte ring slot, so no extra memory per draw), recorded in Step status.

  *The per-frame switches* ride in one of `PassU`'s six free `vec4` (`p_free[6]` after M-LIGHTCOOKIE 40e): `p_translucency` = {back-side ambient on (tier), scatter lobe on (tier), 0, 0}, added to the GLSL block and the C++ mirror with the existing `static_assert`s (`PassU` stays 96 `vec4`); neither file is a wide header (`shaders3d.h` is included by 4 TUs).

  Alternatives rejected (41d re-verifies the first before writing anything): (1) a second extension struct after `MaterialExt` in a new chunk minor: a format change for two bytes; (2) a per-entity kit component that writes a per-instance value into `MeshInstance` (`scene3d.h` has two bytes of padding after `skinning`, so it would fit), which applies to every submesh of the entity: wrong for a character whose skin, clothes and eyes are submeshes of one mesh, and for a tree whose bark and leaves are; it stays a follow-up with a submesh mask (see Kit and Follow-ups); (3) a scene-wide setting: translucency is a material property.

- **Seam (asset and render, 41a).** `formats.h` (498 of 999 TUs, `scripts/ci/include_fanout.py` 2026-11-05) gains only the reservation in `MaterialFlags`: `TranslucencyShift = 8`, `TranslucencyMask = 0xFFu << 8`, `WrapShift = 16`, `WrapMask = 0xFFu << 16`, with a comment that the bits are quantized factors (`mce/asset/material_translucency.h`) and that 6, 7 and 24..31 stay free. `forward3d.h` (167 TUs) gains `bool translucency = true;` in `Forward3DDesc` (false: every draw's value is forced to 0 for the renderer's life) and `u32 translucent_draws = 0;` in `Forward3DStats` (draws this frame whose resolved value is not 0). No private member is needed (no pipeline, no target, no buffer). The new narrow header `mce/asset/material_translucency.h` (includes `mce/core` types only, forward-declares `struct MaterialChunk;`, inline bodies, no state) holds the accessors every later step uses:
  - `u32 quantize_unit(f32 v)`: `clamp(v, 0, 1)`, NaN to 0, then `u32(v * 255 + 0.5)`; `f32 dequantize_unit(u32 q)` = `q / 255.0f`.
  - `f32 material_translucency(const MaterialChunk&)`, `f32 material_wrap(const MaterialChunk&)` (the bits, dequantized), `void set_material_translucency(MaterialChunk&, f32 translucency, f32 wrap)` (writes both fields, leaves every other bit).
  - `u32 translucency_bits(u32 flags)` = `flags & (TranslucencyMask | WrapMask)`, 0 = off.

  Data only: nothing reads it yet. `scene3d.h`, `mesh.h`, `clusters.h`, `kit3d.h`, `weather.h` and `rhi/types.h` are **not** touched by any step (`scene3d.h` 189, `clusters.h` 189, `kit3d.h` 247, `mesh.h` 218, `rhi/types.h` 285, `weather.h` 80 TUs, 2026-11-05). The seam PR re-measures `formats.h` and `forward3d.h` in its description; `formats.h` is wide enough that the reservation goes in alone, before any feature code.

- **Pure CPU helpers (render, 41b).** New narrow header `mce/render/translucency.h` (includes `mce/math` only, forward-declares `struct QualitySettings;`), bodies in `src/translucency.cpp`; f32, fixed evaluation order, no `rand`, no state. The shader mirrors each lighting helper literally (one expression each, pinned by the CPU tests, checked line by line in the 41d and 41e reviews):
  - `inline constexpr f32 kTranslucencyDistortion = 0.2f;` (how far the forward-scatter direction bends toward the normal), `kTranslucencyPower = 4.0f;` (the lobe's tightness), `kTranslucencyScatter = 0.5f;` (its weight against the Lambert lobe). Constants, not material fields: there are no bits left for them, and they are look choices to settle once on a real display (GPU list).
  - `f32 wrap_diffuse(f32 n_dot_l, f32 wrap)` = `max(n_dot_l + wrap, 0) / ((1 + wrap) * (1 + wrap))`; `wrap_diffuse(x, 0)` = `max(x, 0)` exactly.
  - `f32 wrap_extra(f32 n_dot_l, f32 wrap)` = `wrap_diffuse(n_dot_l, wrap) - max(n_dot_l, 0)`: what the lit pass adds to the plain clamped Lambert of `shade` (negative on the lit side, positive in the band past the terminator); exactly 0 for `wrap` 0.
  - `f32 translucency_back(f32 n_dot_l, f32 v_dot_t, bool scatter)` = `max(-n_dot_l, 0) + (scatter ? kTranslucencyScatter * pow(max(v_dot_t, 0), kTranslucencyPower) : 0)`, with `v_dot_t = dot(v, -normalize(l + n * kTranslucencyDistortion))` computed by the caller (the shader has `n`, `v`, `l`); 0 when the light is on the front side and the lobe is off.
  - `f32 translucency_object(u32 flags, bool unlit, const QualitySettings&, bool renderer_on)`: the value `o_skin.w` receives, `t8 + 256 * w8`, or exactly 0 for an unlit material, both fields 0, `!q.translucency`, `r.translucency 0` or `renderer_on` false.
  - `struct TranslucencyFrame { bool ambient; bool scatter; }` and `TranslucencyFrame translucency_frame(const QualitySettings&)` for `p_translucency`.
- **Quality tiers (render, 41b).** Translucency is art direction, so no tier removes it; the tier bounds the cost only: `QualitySettings::translucency` (true on every tier), `translucency_ambient` (Low false: the back side's ambient is not evaluated and the ambient keeps the full diffuse, as today) and `translucency_scatter` (Low false: the Lambert lobe only). Wrap is on every tier (a few ALU). Cvar `r.translucency` (bool, Saved, "Thin-surface translucency and wrap lighting (material translucency / wrap; off = plain diffuse)") next to `r.light_cookies` in `quality.cpp`; `quality.h` (125 TUs, under the warning) only gains the three fields.
- **Material path (asset cook, 41c).** `.mcmat` gains two optional keys, `"translucency": 0..1` and `"wrap": 0..1` (default 0; added to the known-keys list and read with the existing `ranged` helper of `material_importer.cpp`, so an out-of-range value is a cook error naming file, line and column), written through `set_material_translucency`. glTF (`gltf_importer.cpp`): `KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission` is added to `kSupportedExtensions`; `diffuseTransmissionFactor` becomes `translucency`; a `diffuseTransmissionColorFactor` other than white, or either of the extension's textures, gives one warning per material ("the transmitted light takes the base colour; the colour factor and the textures are not cooked"); `KHR_materials_volume` with `thicknessFactor` > 0 is accepted and only noted (the surface is still treated as thin). glTF has no wrap extension, so the material's `extras` (where Blender writes a material's custom properties) may carry `"mce_translucency"` and `"mce_wrap"` (numbers 0..1; they win over the extension), so a character's skin submesh gets its wrap in Blender without a `.mcmat` that would replace every submesh. *Spec details here are from memory and 41c re-reads the ratified `KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission` text before coding* (factor names, defaults, and that the colour factor defaults to white and is not multiplied by the base colour, which is the one deliberate deviation: MCE tints the transmitted light by the base colour, the "glow with its own colour" this milestone is for). 41c also checks `StaticMerge3D`'s grouping key and the impostor bake (Risks).
- **Lit pass: the sun (render, 41d).** In `MCE_GLSL_MESH_FS`, after `diffuse` and `f0` are known and before the sun term. Everything new sits behind `o_skin.w > 0.0`, a per-draw value, so the branch is uniform across the draw and costs an off draw one compare per pixel:

  ```
  bool thin = o_skin.w > 0.0;
  float tr = 0.0, wr = 0.0;
  if (thin) {
      tr = mod(o_skin.w, 256.0) / 255.0 * (1.0 - metallic) * (1.0 - snow);   // snow: this pixel's snow cover
      wr = floor(o_skin.w * (1.0 / 256.0)) / 255.0;
  }
  vec3 diffuse_f = thin ? diffuse * (1.0 - tr) : diffuse;                   // KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission's mix
  vec3 tcol = base.rgb * (1.0 - metallic) * tr;                             // what the back side transmits
  ```

  The sun, for a thin draw (`else`: today's statements unchanged, so an off draw is the same code path):
  - `nl = dot(n, l)`; the front shadow `sh` is looked up where `nl > -wr` (today `nl > 0`; the wrap band needs it), with the contact shadows as today where `nl > 0`.
  - The back shadow `shb` is looked up where `tr > 0 && nl < 0`, with the **flipped** geometric normal for the normal offset (`shadow_term(v_world, -ng, l, depth)`, `ng` the face-oriented `v_normal`), so the receiver point moves to the lit side of the leaf: the leaf itself does not shadow its own back, a second leaf between it and the sun does. No contact shadow on the back (the screen ray would hit the leaf itself). A pixel pays one cascade lookup, front or back, as today; only a translucent pixel inside the wrap band pays two.
  - `color = shade(n, v, l, diffuse_f, f0, a, Lc) * sh + diffuse_f * wrap_extra(nl, wr) * Lc * sh + tcol * translucency_back(nl, dot(v, -normalize(l + n * kTranslucencyDistortion)), p_translucency.y > 0.5) * Lc * shb`, with `Lc = p_light_color.rgb`. The wrap term ignores `shade`'s `(1 - F)` factor on the extra share (documented approximation; the CPU tests pin the expression, not a BRDF).
  - The sun cookie (M-LIGHTCOOKIE 40e) multiplies all three terms (it is the sun's light, whichever side it arrives on): where `p_sun_cookie.w` is set, `sc` is read where `sh > 0 || shb > 0`.
  - `snow`: the snow mask at this pixel (0 without snow cover); a snowed-on leaf is a white, opaque leaf. Today `apply_snow` (`shaders3d.h`) keeps the mask in a local and only raises `fill` to it (`fill` is also raised by smooth decals and by puddles, `apply_decals` and `apply_wet`), so 41d hands the mask out of `apply_snow` (a new `inout float` initialised to 0, called only behind `p_snow.x > 0` as today) rather than reading `fill`. Wetness changes `base` and roughness only, so nothing to add.
- **Lit pass: local lights and ambient (render, 41e).** In the Forward+ loop, after the attenuation, the cone and the cookie (`lc`), for a thin draw: `nl = dot(n, ld)`; the local shadow (when the light has a tile) is looked up with the flipped geometric normal where `tr > 0 && nl < 0` (one lookup per light as today, only its offset direction changes, so the back term costs ALU only); then `color += shade(n, v, ld, diffuse_f, f0, a, E) + diffuse_f * wrap_extra(nl, wr) * E + tcol * translucency_back(nl, ..., scatter) * E` with `E` the light's colour times `att` (and `lc`). A paper lantern (a closed shade around a point light) glows: each outer face has the lamp behind it. The **ambient** on Medium and High (`p_translucency.x`): the front's diffuse ambient uses `diffuse_f`, and the back adds `tcol * irradiance(-n)` (the SH9 sky irradiance, or the hemisphere gradient without IBL; SH9's odd band flips sign under `-n`, so 41e evaluates both sides from the same nine `texelFetch` and pays ALU only), both darkened by SSAO as today. On Low the ambient keeps the full `diffuse` (the back side's ambient is taken as the front's). The irradiance volume (GI probes) follows the same rule where it replaces the sky irradiance; 41e records the reading.
- **Kit (no new component; decided).** The values are per material, and the existing material path is not a wide header: `.mcmat` keys are parsed in `material_importer.cpp`, glTF in `gltf_importer.cpp`, and the kit extract already copies a `.mcmat`'s whole `MaterialChunk` into `MaterialOverride::params` (flags included) for `MeshRef::material`, `Foliage3D::material` and `TerrainFoliage3D`'s material. So every kit path that draws a material gets translucency with **no kit code**: no change to `kit3d.h` (247 TUs), no `engine_components.cpp` includes, no `capabilities` component row, no `sample_coverage.allow` line, no transcript golden bump. A per-entity `Translucency3D` was considered and left out (Design, alternative 2): it would apply to every submesh of the entity and need either a `scene3d.h` field or a per-entity material copy. `capabilities.md`'s generated `Material` asset row (`.mcmat (JSON)`, `asset_create` / `asset_write`) already covers authoring; 41c confirms `capabilities --check` is unchanged and records it.
- **Editor (editor, 41f).** The `.mcmat` form (`mce/editor/asset_form.h`, 11 TUs; `asset_form.cpp` read and write; `material_panel.cpp`) gains `translucency` and `wrap` sliders (0..1) next to `Transmission`, so the material panel and its swatch (M-ASSETLOOK) show and edit them; `tests/editor/test_asset_form.cpp` round-trips both keys.
- **Reach: Luau and MCP.** Scripts and agents reach translucency as they reach every material field: MCP `asset_write` / `asset_create` on a `.mcmat` (the two keys), Luau through the same asset path, and a glTF exported from Blender with the extension or the `extras`. 41c's tests cook a `.mcmat` written that way; 41h writes the user-guide paragraph with one `.mcmat` example and one glTF `extras` example.
- **Determinism.** Translucency is a pure function of the material, the lights and the camera; it never feeds the simulation, a component or the state hash. A material without the keys cooks to the same bytes as before (the bits are 0), so every cooked asset, sample hash and golden of today is unchanged; a material with them differs only in its cooked flags word and in frames.

## Cost and budget

- **GPU work when on.** Per pixel of a translucent or wrapped draw: the decode (a `mod`, a `floor`, two multiplies) and, per light, about 15 to 25 ALU (the wrap delta, the back Lambert, on Medium and High the lobe's `normalize` and `pow`). Fetches: for the sun, a back-lit pixel pays one cascade lookup that today is skipped (the same cost as a front-lit pixel), a pixel inside the wrap band of a translucent material two; local lights pay no extra fetch (the existing shadow lookup flips its offset); the back ambient pays no fetch (the same nine SH texels). Target on a mid GPU at 1080p High: at most 0.2 ms for a frame whose lower half is back-lit foliage (measured on the GPU list, not here). An off draw pays one uniform compare per pixel.
- **Memory.** None: no texture, no buffer, no target; `ObjectU` keeps its size, `PassU` uses one of its free `vec4`.
- **CPU.** One `translucency_object` per draw (a mask, two shifts, a tier check) at the existing `ObjectU` fill sites, one `translucency_frame` per view.
- **Off.** Nothing beyond the compare: `o_skin.w` stays 0, `p_translucency` is 0, the shader runs today's statements.

## Risks

- **Self-shadowing at the terminator and the wrap band.** On a closed mesh (a head, a sphere) the cascade shadows the far side of the geometric terminator because the mesh occludes itself, so the sun's wrapped band is cut by the shadow's own penumbra and wrap shows most under local lights without shadows, the ambient and normal-mapped detail. 41d measures the band on a sphere under the sun (pixels lit with `wrap` 0.5 against `wrap` 0) and decides between (a) leaving it, (b) a wider normal offset inside the band only; the decision and its numbers go in Step status. This is the plan's one real unknown.
- **Back shadow on thick or single-sided meshes.** The flipped offset is right for a thin, double-sided card. On a thick translucent mesh the back lookup clears the near face and may find the far face as the occluder, so the glow is shadowed; on a single-sided card seen from the back the normal is face-oriented, so it is the same case as a double-sided one. Documented: the effect is for thin surfaces.
- **Light leaking.** A translucent wall lights the room behind it from the sun where its back shadow is clear. This is what translucency means (a paper screen), but an artist who sets it on a thick wall sees light through it; the user guide says to keep it on thin surfaces.
- **Impostors and LOD pops.** The impostor bake (M-OPENWORLD A3) writes its own alpha-masked material, so a far tree impostor is not translucent and a back-lit forest may pop at the LOD switch. 41c records whether the bake should copy the dominant source's bits (a one-line change in `impostor.cpp`, follow-up if not done there); mesh LOD levels share their material and are unaffected.
- **Energy and exposure.** The KHR mix keeps a translucent leaf no brighter when front-lit, but the forward-scatter lobe adds light (it is art, not energy conserving), and a back-lit canopy in the frame brightens it, so auto exposure darkens slightly. Accepted; the constants are tuned on a real display (GPU list).
- **The glTF colour deviation.** MCE tints the transmitted light by the base colour and ignores `diffuseTransmissionColorFactor`; an asset that relies on a distinct transmission colour (a white petal glowing pink) looks different than in other viewers. Recorded with a warning at import; a colour field is a follow-up once the material has room.
- **Quantization.** 8 bits per value; a material set to 0.5 reads back as 128/255 = 0.50196. Tests compare the dequantized value, never the authored one.
- **Look.** The default constants, the lobe and the wrap on skin are look choices that only a real display can judge; llvmpipe pixels prove placement, the off-checks and the shadow behaviour, not taste.
