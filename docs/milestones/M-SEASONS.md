# M-SEASONS — Foliage colour and snow bias that follow the time of year, from the world clock's day count (milestone record)

**Status:** done (2026-10-30), steps 19a to 19d. **Owner: routine 2 thread (the M-VISUAL line).** Follow-up of M-DAYNIGHT (`WorldClock` and its `day` counter), M-WINDSWAY (the extract-only batch feed the tint copies), M-SNOWCOVER and M-WEATHER (the snow cover and the kinds the bias leans on), M-GROUNDMIST (the pure-helper, no-shader, no-system pattern). Today a scene looks the same on day 1 and day 300: `WorldClock` counts days and emits `DayPassed`, but nothing reads the count for the look, so foliage is always the authored green and snow comes only with the weather. No Linear issues (free issue limit); this record is the tracker.

**Goal.** An opt-in kit component `Season` turns the `WorldClock` day count into a season phase (0..1 over an authored year length in days) and pure helpers turn the phase into a foliage tint (spring light green, summer the authored colour, autumn orange and red, winter bare and grey), a foliage density scale and a snow bias (winter leans cold, summer none). `Kit3DExtract` applies them to the *render-side copies only*: the foliage batches' tint and the snow-cover values it already builds. No new shader, pass, render field or system state. A scene without `Season` hashes, simulates and renders exactly as today.

**Not in scope:** a shader or a new `render::` field (the tint goes through the existing `InstanceBatch::tint`), per-species calendars or per-entity seasons (one global phase), leaf-fall particles, bare-branch meshes or mesh swaps (winter is a tint and a thinning, not different geometry), changing temperature or the `Weather` schedule by season (the weather stays as authored; the bias only shifts the snow cover the extract draws, see Design), terrain splat or grass-layer recolouring, sounds and a calendar or date UI, and changing `Weather`, `SnowCover`, `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Foliage3D`, `TerrainFoliage3D` or `Environment3D` bytes (the last two foliage headers are wide: `kit3d.h` is not edited).

## Design

- **Real names read while planning.** `Foliage3D` and `TerrainFoliage3D` (`mce/kit/kit3d.h`) both have `math::vec4 tint` (default white), `tint_variation`, `count` and `cull_distance`. The extract builds one `render::InstanceBatch` per kind and sets `b.tint = f.tint` (`kit3d_extract.cpp`, the `Foliage3D` loop ~1700 and the `TerrainFoliage3D` batch ~1810), then runs `foliage_look` and `apply_wind_sway(sway_wind, *sway, b)`, which writes only batch fields after the cached transforms, so a wind change never re-scatters (`entry->version` untouched). The tint is therefore a per-frame batch field and **foliage colour tinting needs no shader**: multiplying `b.tint` after `foliage_look` is the same kind of edit as `apply_wind_sway`. `WorldClock` (`mce/kit/world_clock.h`) has the engine-written `u32 day` (whole days since the start) and `f32 hour`. `SnowCover` (`mce/kit/snow_cover.h`) has the engine-written `snow_q` / `cover`; the extract (~955 to ~980) builds a local `render::SnowCover` from `cover * strength`, `coverage`, `noise_scale_m` and `wind_bias` only when a `Weather` and a `SnowCover` exist, then calls `render::apply_quality`.
- **Opt-in component, no system, no state.** New component `Season` (`mce/kit/season.h`, one per scene, the first in ECS order is read, registered by `install_weather` next to `GroundMist`; 19a confirms the spot by reading `install_weather`). Pure data: the phase is a pure function of authoring and the clock, evaluated at extract and in the Luau getter, so nothing is carried between ticks and nothing is hashed beyond the authored fields. Authoring (all `Save`, with a `Range`): `enabled` 1; `year_days` 12 (1..3650, days in a year; the default is short so a sample shows all four seasons in minutes); `start_phase` 0.1 (0..1, the phase at day 0 hour 0, so a scene can start in spring, summer, autumn or winter); `spring_tint` (default 0.75 1.05 0.7, linear multiplier of the authored tint), `autumn_tint` (1.35 0.7 0.35) and `winter_tint` (0.7 0.72 0.75), the tint at the centre of each season, with `summer` being white (the authored colour untouched); `winter_density` 0.4 (0..1, the share of copies left in the deepest winter); `snow_cover_bias` 0.5 (0..1, the extra snow cover at the centre of winter); `cold_bias` 0.25 (0..1, the lee-bias shift, see below); `strength` 1 (0..1, scales every effect; 0 = off exactly). No engine-written fields; the struct stays naturally packed with an explicit zeroed pad and a `test_padding` entry.
- **Phase (kit, 19a).** `season_phase(const Season&, u32 day, f32 hour) -> f32` is `fract(start_phase + (day + hour / 24) / year_days)` in f64 and returned as f32 in [0,1); the sum is computed in f64 so a long run does not drift in f32, and `hour` outside 0..24 or NaN counts as 0, NaN or zero `year_days` falls back to the default, out-of-range values clamp to the Range. The year is four equal quarters: spring `[0, 0.25)`, summer `[0.25, 0.5)`, autumn `[0.5, 0.75)`, winter `[0.75, 1)`, each with its centre at the middle of the quarter, and the helpers blend between neighbouring centres with a smoothstep so the colour changes smoothly all year (summer to autumn, autumn to winter, winter to spring, spring to summer) and the year wraps without a step at phase 1 to 0. A scene with no `WorldClock` has no day count: the extract and the getter use day 0 and hour 0, i.e. a fixed `start_phase` season (a still scene can choose its season, the guide says so).
- **Pure helpers (kit, 19a).** In `season.h`, inline, f32, fixed evaluation order, smoothstep polynomials only (no `pow`, `exp` or trig, no `rand`):
  - `season_foliage_tint(const Season&, f32 phase) -> vec3`: the multiplier, white at the centre of summer, `spring_tint` at the centre of spring and so on, blended by the smoothstep between adjacent centres, then lerped from white by `strength`. `strength` 0 returns exactly white.
  - `season_foliage_density(const Season&, f32 phase) -> f32`: 1 in spring, summer and most of autumn, falling in late autumn to `winter_density` at the centre of winter and back to 1 through winter's end into spring; lerped by `strength`; the extract uses it as the kept share of copies (see the extract paragraph for how and for the fallback).
  - `season_snow_bias(const Season&, f32 phase) -> SeasonSnow {cover_floor, lee_shift}`: `cover_floor = snow_cover_bias * w` and `lee_shift = cold_bias * w`, with `w` the winter weight (0 from spring to the end of summer, 1 at the centre of winter, smooth in between); summer is exactly 0. Lerped by `strength`.
  A zero `strength`, `enabled` 0 and a missing component all return the identity (white, 1, 0) as exact values, which the bit-identical gates rely on. NaN, inverted and zero ranges fall back to the defaults.
- **Extract (kit, 19b).** `Kit3DExtract::apply_environment` / the batch loops read the first `Season` and the first `WorldClock` once per frame (`.cpp` only). **Tint:** after `foliage_look` and `apply_wind_sway` in both batch loops, `b.tint` is multiplied component-wise by `season_foliage_tint` (alpha untouched); the authored component is never written, and `tint_variation` is left as authored so the per-copy variety survives. **Density:** the density scale is applied by passing a shorter `b.transforms` span (a prefix of the cached copies; the scatter order is hash-random, so a prefix is an unbiased thinning, and the cache `entry->version` is not touched, as with the wind fields). 19b first checks, against the renderer's per-batch caches (GPU culling and instance buffers key on `b.version` and the span size), whether a shorter span with an unchanged version is safe; if it is not, the density scale is **dropped from the milestone** rather than adding a renderer or cache change (it would need a `render::` field and a seam PR), winter is then the grey tint only, and the record says so. **Snow:** where the extract already builds the local `render::SnowCover` (a `Weather` and a `SnowCover` exist), `snow.cover = max(snow.cover, season cover_floor * snowc->strength)` and `snow.wind_bias` is shifted by `lee_shift` (clamped 0..1), on the local copy before `apply_quality`. `SnowCover::snow_q` / `cover` and `Weather` are not written: the *simulated* accumulation target is not raised, because that would put season state into the `SnowCover` system and into the state hash, against the no-state rule. A winter scene therefore shows a frosted ground even when the weather is clear, which the guide states plainly; a follow-up may move the bias into the system as a hashed change. With no `Season`, `enabled` 0, `strength` 0 or summer the multiplications are by exactly 1.0f and the floor by 0, and the code path is skipped entirely, so frames are bit-identical.
- **Density thinning dropped (19b).** The renderer side is fine: `Forward3D` re-uploads a batch when `version`, the count or the mesh changes, and bounds are recomputed per frame, so a shorter span with an unchanged version is cached correctly (at the cost of a re-upload whenever the count changes during a fade). The scatter is the problem: `scatter_foliage` is hash-random, but `scatter_on_terrain` walks cells row by row, so a prefix of the `TerrainFoliage3D` copies is a bare strip, not a thinning. An unbiased thinning needs a per-copy keep test (a render field or a re-ordered scatter), i.e. a render seam milestone, which this milestone does not add. The extract passes the full span; `season_foliage_density` stays in `season.h` unused by the extract.
- **Why no system.** `WorldClock.day` is an exact integer and `hour` an exact rational from the clock, so the phase is deterministic by itself; a season system would only add state to hash and a way to drift. The `WorldClock` and `Weather` column hashes are unchanged by construction.
- **Determinism and hash.** A scene without `Season` is byte-identical (one empty query in the extract; the extract is not part of the state hash). A scene with it differs only in the saved component and in frames. The phase depends only on authoring, `day` and `hour`, so two runs agree and save/load mid-year gives the same phase; a test evaluates the helpers over a `WorldClock` run in one go and in two halves.
- **Quality tiers.** Tint costs nothing, so every tier keeps it. The snow floor goes through the existing `apply_quality(quality_, snow)`, so `r.snow_cover 0` and the Low tier's noise drop apply unchanged.
- **Luau (19c).** `weather.set_season{enabled=, year_days=, start_phase=, spring_tint={r,g,b}, autumn_tint={r,g,b}, winter_tint={r,g,b}, winter_density=, snow_cover_bias=, cold_bias=, strength=}`, getters `weather.season_<key>()` (the tints return `r, g, b`) and read-only `weather.season_phase()` from the same helper on the current `WorldClock` day and hour (0..1; the fixed `start_phase` with no clock, nil-free), named errors, values clamped to the Range, nothing written on error, a missing `Season` raises (the setter never adds one), typed in `definitions.cpp`, same rules as `weather.set_mist` and `weather.set_meteor`. `samples/12_weather` gets a `Season` on the Weather entity (its entity count is unchanged) and logs `season:` (the phase and the tint) every 150 ticks; its golden and log expectations move in 19c, nothing else. 19c reads the sample first: if its `WorldClock` day length makes a year too slow to show, the sample sets a short `year_days`, the other authoring stays default.

## Gates
- A scene without `Season` (or with `enabled` 0, `strength` 0, or in summer) has an unchanged state hash and unchanged frames; every existing sample hash and golden stays byte-identical until 19a / 19c change `samples/12_weather`, and only that sample's expectations move.
- `Weather`, `SnowCover`, `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Foliage3D`, `TerrainFoliage3D` and `Environment3D` are never written by season code: the extract changes only the batch it is building and the local `render::SnowCover`; a test hashes the components and resources with and without `Season`.
- The phase and every helper are pure functions of authoring, `day` and `hour`; the year sum is in f64; no stored float, no wall clock, no `rand`.
- No shader, pass, `PassU` change or new render field is added; the only render-side values are the existing `InstanceBatch::tint` (and its span) and `render::SnowCover` members.
- `kit3d.h`, `weather.h`, `snow_cover.h` and `scene3d.h` are not edited (checked with `scripts/ci/include_fanout.py`); `season.h` is included only by the extract, the install, the script binding and the tests, so no seam PR is expected.
- Luau: setters clamp, bad types raise named errors and write nothing.

## Risks
- **Tint multiplies the authored colour.** A foliage entity whose material is already autumn-coloured, or a tint far from white, shifts in a way the author did not intend; the guide says the season values are multipliers of the authored look and summer is the authored look. A foliage kind that must not change (evergreens, rocks) has no opt-out in this milestone; per-entity opt-out is a recorded follow-up (it would need a field on `Foliage3D`, a wide header).
- **Density thinning vs the renderer caches.** The span-prefix idea relies on batch version and span size being independent for the GPU cull and instance buffers; 19b verifies it and drops thinning if not (see Design). Winter then reads as grey foliage, not bare.
- **Snow is a drawn floor, not simulation.** The bias shows frost on a clear winter day but does not make the weather snow, does not change `SnowCover.cover` and so does not feed anything gameplay reads (for example a Luau `snow_cover()` getter still reads the simulated value). Stated in the guide; moving it into the system is a hashed change and a follow-up.
- **Needs a clock to move.** With no `WorldClock` the season is fixed at `start_phase`; with the default 1200 s day a 12-day year is 4 real hours, so the sample shortens `year_days` or the day length and a long-running scene should set both.
- **Tint order against the wind code.** The tint is applied after `foliage_look` and `apply_wind_sway` and does not touch the wind fields; a test compares the wind fields with and without `Season` so the two stay independent.
- **Colour fights `DayCycle`.** `DayCycle` rewrites sun, ambient and fog colours only, not foliage tint, so there is no writer to fight; a strong orange tint under a warm dusk key can look saturated, which the sample's tint defaults are tuned against in 19c.
