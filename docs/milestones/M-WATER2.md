# M-WATER2 — Water, second pass: it reflects the scene, refracts what lies under it, and flows (milestone plan)

**Status:** done 2026-11-09: 49a built (#1192), 49b to 49d built (#1195), 49e and 49f built (#1196), 49g and 49h built (the fourth PR: the Luau current, the `tour_river` sample, the docs and this closure; entries below). Steps 49a to 49h, in four PRs (49a alone; 49b+49c+49d; 49e+49f; 49g+49h). Every step is small. The only wide-header edits are in 49a: `scene3d.h` (one carrier field) and `forward3d.h` (two stats bools), shipped together as the seam PR, merged first. **Owner: the M-VISUAL routine 2 thread.** Follow-up of M-WATER (its "Not in scope" names "flow maps or rivers with a direction field", "planar or screen-space reflections of geometry (the reflection is the sky only ...)" and "refraction of submerged geometry beyond the depth tint"), M-UNDERWATER (the Beer-Lambert absorption this plan reuses from above) and M-REFLECT2 (which kept water out of SSR). No Linear issues (free limit); this record is the tracker.

**Naming:** the water is **not rebuilt**. The kit component `Water` (`mce/kit/water.h`), the render carrier `render::WaterSurface` (`scene3d.h`), the shaders `kWaterVS` / `kWaterFS` (`shaders3d.h`), `water_params` (`forward3d.cpp`) and the cvar `r.water` (`quality.cpp`) all exist and stay. On 2026-11-09 `git grep -niE 'water2\b|M-WATER2|water_flow|waterflow|flow_dir|flow_speed|r\.water\.|water_optics'` over `src`, `tests`, `samples`, `docs` and `scripts` finds nothing (checked on `origin/master` 77a912ca; the `water2` hits are the existing `PassU::water2` / `p_water2` lane, a different name, and it stays). New names: the kit component `WaterFlow` (`mce/kit/water_flow.h`), the carrier field `WaterSurface::flow`, the narrow render header `mce/render/water_optics.h`, the PassU lane `p_water6`, the cvars `r.water.reflect` and `r.water.refract`, the stats bools `Forward3DStats::water_reflect` and `water_refract`, the Luau `water.set_flow` / `water.flow_*` functions and the sample scene `tour_river.mcscene`.

**Goal.** Make the water read as part of the world instead of a sky-coloured sheet: (1) things standing above or beside the water (a pier, a pillar, a bank, a boat) show up **mirrored in it**, broken by the waves, and fall back to today's sky reflection where the screen holds no answer; (2) what lies **under** the water (the seabed, stones, a sunken crate) is seen **through** it, bent by the wave normals and dimmed with depth by the same per-channel absorption the underwater camera already uses, instead of today's flat shallow-to-deep tint; (3) a scene can give the water a **current**: a direction and a speed that carry the waves, the ripples and the gameplay height along, so a river or a canal visibly flows and a float drifts with what is drawn. (1) and (2) are render-side only (nothing hashed, two cvars to turn them off); (3) is an opt-in kit component that a scene without it never sees (state hash, frames and the scene format unchanged).

**Not in scope:** planar reflections (a second render of the scene mirrored in the plane: correct for off-screen geometry, but a second scene pass; follow-up 1), ray-traced or hierarchical (Hi-Z) tracing, putting the water into the SSR/SSAO prepass or the velocity prepass (so SSR of other surfaces still does not see the water and TAA still reprojects it as sky; follow-up 3), caustics, chromatic dispersion, refraction seen from below (the underwater lid of M-UNDERWATER stays as it is: both effects switch off when the camera is below the level), sloped rivers, waterfalls and rapids (the water is still **one horizontal plane**), flow maps or per-position flow fields (the current is one uniform velocity per scene; follow-up 2), foam streaks and wakes that follow the current, buoyancy or drag forces in `physics3d` (the Luau `water.flow_at` gives the velocity, applying it stays with the author's script, as M-WATER 15d did for the height), changing `Water`'s fields or column (flow is a separate component, see Design 3), changing `QualitySettings`, `Environment3D` or `kit3d.h` (wide), new goldens, Metal and WebGPU verification (shader text is shared; it goes to the GPU list).

## Investigation (what the code does today; the plan below rests on these facts)

Checked on `origin/master` 77a912ca, 2026-11-09.

- **The shader.** `kWaterVS` / `kWaterFS` in `src/engine/render/src/shaders3d.h` (lines about 2340 to 2510). The vertex shader draws a camera-following square of `kWaterGrid` x `kWaterGrid` (16 x 16) cells at the floating-origin-local level `p_water.x`. The plane is flat: waves are **normals only** (up to 8 Gerstner waves, `p_wave[8]` = wave vector K, amplitude, phase, plus two fixed detail ripples). `kWaterFS` is **44 654 bytes** (measured with `strlen` on a host build of `shaders3d.h`), against clang's 65 535-byte literal limit: about 20 KB of room (for comparison `kMeshFS` is 65 206). `kSsrFS` is 4 246 bytes.
- **The reflection is the sky only.** `water_sky(r, pix, lod)` evaluates `sky_radiance` plus the shared sky layers (night, aurora, rainbow, meteors, clouds, horizon haze) along the reflected ray `r`, kept above the horizon (`r.y >= 0.002`). Schlick fresnel with F0 0.02 scaled by `reflectivity` (`p_water3.w`). The sun disk is replaced by a GGX glint. **No scene colour is read anywhere in the water shader.**
- **No refraction.** The body colour is `mix(shallow, deep, t) * light` with `t = 1 - exp(-3 path / depth_scale_m)`, where `path` is the distance along the view ray from the surface to the opaque surface behind it. Nothing of what lies under the water is visible: a striped seabed and a flat one look the same.
- **The depth read exists.** `path` and the shore foam come from `u_water_depth` at **binding 6** (`bank::kAlbedo`): the half-size opaque prepass `ao_gbuf_` (RGBA16F, view normal xyz, view depth w, 0 = nothing drawn) that SSAO, SSR, contact shadows, the flare, the outline and the haze share. `record_main` runs `record_prepass` for the water when nobody else did (`prepass_done_`), but only when the prepass pipelines exist (`screen_space_prepass(...)`) **and** the water has waves or foam (`amplitude > 0 || foam_amount > 0`). The Low tier's signature (amplitude 0, foam 0) and a desc without the prepass pipelines give `p_water4.w = 0`: no depth read, the distance tint.
- **Draw order inside the main pass** (`record_main`, `forward3d.cpp` about 2700 to 2800): opaques, batches, **water**, sky (it skips the water's pixels by depth), then, only when a transmissive draw exists, `end_pass` + **the transmission copy** (`cmd.resolve(hdr_msaa_, trans_src_)` with MSAA, else a `frame_copy_` full-screen pass from `hdr_` into `trans_src_`), a second `forward.transmissive` pass that reloads colour and depth, the blended draws, the particles. So the engine already has a tested way to break the main pass and copy the frame drawn so far, and `trans_src_` (same desc as `hdr_`, created lazily) already lives in `forward3d.h`. Transmissive draws read it at `bank::kAo` (11).
- **Sampler banks of the water draw.** `kWaterFS` declares binding 4 (`MCE_GLSL_VOLUME`, the froxel fog), 6 (`u_water_depth`) and 10 (the sky texture of `MCE_GLSL_SKY_LAYERS`). The main pass's `bind_main_state` binds the textures of banks 3 (`ssr_`), 4, 9, 10 and 11 (and 2 for a sun cookie) before the opaque draws, which bind their material maps per draw (banks 6, 7, 8, 13 through the `DrawCache`); bank 6 is rebound for the water and the `DrawCache` is reset after it so the blended draws rebind their maps. Bank 7 (`kNormal`) is such a per-draw material bank, not read by `kWaterFS` and reset with the cache: free for a second water texture.
- **Why the SSR texture cannot simply be sampled.** `ssr_` (bank 3, half size, RGBA16F, rgb = last frame's HDR at the hit, a = confidence) is traced from the **prepass** normal and depth. The water is **not in the prepass** (M-WATER 15c), so the `ssr_` texel under a water pixel is the reflection of the **seabed** (or nothing, where the prepass holds no opaque), traced from the seabed's normal, not the water's: sampling it gives a wrong reflection. SSR is also off on the Low tier and in every scene that leaves `ScreenSpaceReflections` off, and it reads last frame's HDR, which already contains last frame's water.
- **Why last frame's HDR cannot be the refraction source.** `hdr_` at the time the water draws holds the previous frame **with its water in it**: under the water, it is water-coloured. Refracting it feeds the water's own tint back into itself every frame (a compounding tint, plus a one-frame lag and TAA ghosting under camera motion). Without MSAA `hdr_` is also this pass's colour attachment and cannot be sampled at all.
- **No flow.** One plane, one wave set: wave i travels along the main direction (`wind_dir`, or the `WindDirection` heading when `wind_follow` > 0) turned by `kWaterDirDeg[i]`, with omega = `speed_scale * sqrt(g k)` **rounded to a whole multiple of 2 pi / 64 s** (`kWaterPeriodSeconds`), so a time wrapped to 64 s repeats exactly. The phases are summed in f64 on the CPU (`water_params`, with a C++ copy of the kit's tables) and wrapped to 2 pi. The kit evaluates the same set (`water_wave_set` returns a `WaterWaveSet`; `water_height(const WaterWaveSet&, ...)` / `water_normal(...)` evaluate any set), and Luau `water.height_at` uses it (`api_weather.cpp`).
- **Water ice** (M-FREEZE): `apply_water_ice` in `kit3d_extract.cpp` scales the carrier's amplitude, steepness, speed, colours, roughness, reflectivity and foam before `apply_quality`. A frozen sea should not flow either.
- **Headers.** `include_fanout.py` on 2026-11-09: `scene3d.h` 222 of 1038 TUs, `forward3d.h` 193 of 1038 (both over the 150 warning), `mce/kit/water.h` 19 (narrow). `Forward3DPassU` is 1536 bytes with **4 free vec4** (`free_[4]`, after `ssr2`).
- **Tests and scenes with water.** `tests/render_gl/test_render3d_water_gl.cpp` (8 cases), `test_render3d_underwater_gl.cpp`, the goldens `water_calm`, `water_sea`, `water_night`, the rainbow and meteor water goldens (`rainbow_water_scene`, `meteor_water_scene` with `water_carrier(0.05f)`), the underwater golden (`underwater_scene`), the sample `samples/13_water` (headless state hashes pinned by `water_test.cmake`), the `kit3d_tour` scene `tour_underwater.mcscene`, and `tests/kit/test_water*.cpp`, `tests/script/test_script_water.cpp`. Which of them have geometry above or below the surface (and so can change under (1) and (2)) is 49b's audit.

## Design

### 1. Refraction: this frame's opaque picture, copied before the water draws (49c)

Options weighed:

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A. Sample last frame's `hdr_` at an offset uv | **No.** Under the water it holds last frame's water (a self-feeding tint), it lags a frame, and without MSAA it is the attachment being written. |
| B. Keep the tint, add only a "caustic-like" fake pattern | **No.** Does not show what is under the water, which is the point. |
| **C. Break the main pass before the water, copy the opaque frame (the transmission copy, moved earlier), sample it in `kWaterFS`** | **Chosen.** It is this frame's picture (no lag, no feedback, no TAA ghost), it uses a code path that already exists and is tested (the resolve or `frame_copy_` into `trans_src_`), and the water then reads one more texture at a free bank. The cost is one pass break and one full-size copy on frames that draw water with the effect on. |

- **The copy.** When the water is drawn **and** reads the depth (`water_depth_`, so never on the Low tier's look and never without the prepass) **and** `r.water.reflect` or `r.water.refract` is on **and** the camera is above the level (below it, M-UNDERWATER's lid covers the view), `record_main` ends the main pass after the opaques and batches, copies into `trans_src_` exactly as the transmissive path does, and reopens the pass with colour and depth loaded (`depth.store = Store` for the first pass in that case). The water then binds `trans_src_` at **bank 7** (`kNormal`; reset with the cache after the draw, like bank 6). Consequence for glass: when the frame also has transmissive draws, the existing copy after the sky still runs and **rewrites** `trans_src_` with the water and the sky in it, so glass keeps refracting the water (today's behaviour). Two copies in that frame, one texture: the RHI orders "sampled by the water draw, then written by the next copy pass" (GL is in order; SDL GPU tracks the hazard). 49c confirms on both backends with a glass-over-water test. No new texture, so `forward3d.h` gets no member.
- **The sky is not in the copy** (the sky draws after the water). That is fine because the refraction only reads where the prepass says an opaque exists behind the water (below); a pixel with nothing behind keeps today's deep tint.
- **The bend.** The refracted lookup point is `uv_r = uv + water_refract_offset(n, path, zw)`: the wave normal's horizontal tilt projected to the screen (`mat3(u_view) * vec3(n.x, 0, n.z)`, its xy), times `kWaterRefractScale`, times `min(path, kWaterRefractMaxPath)`, divided by the water's view depth `zw` (a deeper path bends more, a far surface bends fewer pixels), clamped to `kWaterRefractMaxUv` of the screen. A calm plane (count 0 waves, no ripples) gives an offset of exactly 0, so the seabed is seen straight through. Constants live in `water_optics.h` and are copied into the shader as text (the M-REFLECT2 rule: the CPU helper and the shader formula are pinned together by a GL test).
- **Depth-aware: no foreground leak.** The prepass depth at `uv_r` (the same half-size lookup as `water_floor_depth`) must be **behind the water** (`z(uv_r) > zw`). A nearer value means the offset landed on an object in front of the water (a post, a leg, the bank above the waterline): the lookup falls back to the unbent `uv`. A zero value (nothing behind there) falls back the same way. So an object standing in the water never smears its above-water colour into the water around it.
- **Absorption, not a tint.** `sigma = underwater_sigma(shallow, depth_scale_m, 1)` (the M-UNDERWATER helper, `mce/render/underwater.h`; the shader repeats its formula from `p_water2`, see Uniforms; the shallow colour is the fraction of each channel that survives one absorption length) and `T = exp(-sigma * path_r)` with `path_r` the path at `uv_r`. The body becomes `refr = scene(uv_r) * T + body_today * (1 - mean(T))`, where `body_today` is today's `mix(shallow, deep, t) * light`. So at the shore line (path 0) the sand shows through, red dies first with depth, and deep water ends at exactly today's colour. The fresnel mix is unchanged: `color = refr * (1 - F) + sky_or_reflection * F + glint`, then foam and fog as today.
- **Off exactly.** `r.water.refract 0`, no depth read, the camera below the level, or nothing behind (`path < 0`) take today's body expression line for line.

### 2. Geometry reflection: a short screen-space trace in `kWaterFS`, falling back to the sky (49d)

Options weighed:

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A. Sample the existing `ssr_` texture at the water pixel | **No.** The water is not in the prepass, so `ssr_` holds the seabed's reflection there (Investigation). SSR is off on Low and in scenes without `ScreenSpaceReflections`. |
| B. Put the water into the SSR prepass so `kSsrFS` traces it | **No (follow-up 3).** The prepass is shared by about a dozen readers: SSAO, contact shadows, the outline, the flare, the haze and the water's own depth read would all see the plane as an opaque floor (the depth tint and foam would read the water itself). It needs a separate water normal/depth target: a milestone of plumbing. |
| C. Planar reflection (render the scene mirrored in the plane) | **No (follow-up 1).** The correct answer, off-screen objects included, but a second render of the scene per frame. |
| **D. A water-only screen-space march in `kWaterFS` against `u_water_depth`, colour from the copy of section 1, fade to `water_sky`** | **Chosen.** No new pass, target or pipeline: the depth is already bound, the colour source is section 1's copy (one copy serves both effects), and the ray already exists (`r`, the reflected ray of the **wave** normal, so the reflection is broken by the waves for free). |

- **The march.** From the surface point `p` along the reflected ray `r` (already kept above the horizon), `kWaterTraceSteps` (24) samples at distances growing quadratically up to `kWaterTraceMaxDist` (an authored-free constant, 80 m, tuned in 49d; dense near the surface where reflections of a pier or a bank are), each projected with `u_view_proj` and its view depth from `u_view`. A hit is the first sample whose ray depth is behind the prepass depth there (`z_ray > z_scene > 0`) by less than a thickness that grows with the step length; four bisection steps refine it, as `kSsrFS` does. A sample off screen ends the march as a miss.
- **Confidence.** `conf = water_reflect_fade(uv_hit, travel)`: an edge fade (the last `kWaterEdgeFade` of the screen on every side), a distance fade over the last 30 % of `kWaterTraceMaxDist`, and a fade for rays that point toward the camera (they leave the visible picture). The reflected colour is `mix(water_sky(r, ...), copy(uv_hit), conf)`, computed only when `conf > 0`, so a miss (the sky, an empty scene, rays that leave the screen) returns `water_sky` **bit for bit**.
- **Roughness.** The water's `roughness` already widens the sky lookup (lod) and the glint; the trace takes one tap at the hit (no gather). The waves are the blur: a rough, wavy sea scatters the hit point from pixel to pixel. If the image shows sparkle on a high-roughness calm sea, 49d adds the M-REFLECT2 Vogel gather (8 taps) on `trans_src_` with the radius from `ssr_blur_radius(roughness, 0)`; written into the as-built entry either way.
- **What it cannot do (said in the docs):** only what is on screen this frame and in the opaque pass reflects; an object above the top of the screen, behind the camera or hidden behind another object is not in the reflection (the sky shows instead); transparent draws, particles and the sky do not reflect as geometry (the sky is already `water_sky`); the reflection of a tall object close to the camera ends where the object leaves the screen, with a fade, not a pop. This is the standard screen-space trade-off and the reason follow-up 1 exists.
- **Off exactly.** `r.water.reflect 0`, no depth read, the camera below the level: `water_sky` only, as today.

### 3. Flow: an opt-in `WaterFlow` component that carries the waves (49e, 49f, 49g)

- **Physics, kept simple.** Waves on a uniform current `U` are the still-water waves carried along: the phase of wave i becomes `K_i . x - (omega_i + K_i . U) t` (the Doppler shift). The whole pattern, the long swell, the short waves and the two detail ripples, drifts with the current; nothing else changes (the wavelengths, the amplitudes and the steepness stay authored).
- **The 64 s wrap.** Every frequency must stay a whole multiple of `2 pi / 64 s` or the wrapped clock pops once every 64 s. So the Doppler term is rounded the same way omega already is: `omega_i + 2 pi / 64 * round(K_i . U * 64 / (2 pi))` (`water_flow_cycles` in the kit and in `water_optics.h`, the same f32 and f64 steps). The rounding makes the drift speed of wave i exact to within `(pi / 64) / k_i` m/s: 0.19 m/s for the default 24 m swell, 0.01 m/s for a 1.4 m short wave, 0.006 m/s for the 1.1 m ripple. A slow current therefore moves the long swell in steps of that size (a 0.1 m/s current does not move a 24 m swell at all) while the short waves and ripples, which are what the eye reads as flow, follow it closely. Recorded as a limit; no extra period is introduced.
- **A separate component, not new `Water` fields.** `Water`'s column is hashed whole; adding fields to it changes the state hash of every scene with water (`samples/13_water` pins its hashes). A new component **`WaterFlow`** in its own narrow header `mce/kit/water_flow.h` (includes `water.h`, which stays untouched) keeps every existing scene, hash and saved file as it is: the format change is purely additive. Pure data, no system, no state (the drift is a pure function of the scene time, like the waves): `enabled` 1; `dir_deg` 0 (0..360, the direction the water moves to, 0 = +x, counter-clockwise, the same convention as `Water::wind_dir_deg`); `speed` 0 (0..5 m/s); explicit zeroed pad and a `test_padding` entry. Registered by `install_weather` next to `Water` (49e reads `install_weather` first). The first `WaterFlow` in ECS order is read, as for `Water`; it only acts when a `Water` exists.
- **Pure kit helpers (`water_flow.h`).** `math::vec2 water_flow_velocity(const WaterFlow*)` (zero for null, disabled, NaN or zero speed), `WaterWaveSet water_wave_set_flow(const Water&, f32 wind_dir_deg, math::vec2 velocity)` (the existing set with each omega Doppler-shifted and rounded as above; velocity zero returns **the same bits** as `water_wave_set`), and `water_height_at` / `water_normal_at` overloads that take a `const WaterFlow*`. Gameplay and the picture therefore stay on the same surface (M-WATER's rule).
- **Carrier and feed.** `WaterSurface::flow` (`math::vec2`, m/s on world xz, default zero) is the seam field (49a). `Kit3DExtract` fills it from `water_flow_velocity` of the first `WaterFlow` (`.cpp` only); `apply_water_ice` scales it by the ice look's `speed_scale`, so a frozen river stops with its waves; `apply_quality` clamps it (each component to -5..5, NaN to 0). `r.water 0` removes the plane and with it the flow.
- **Renderer (49f).** `water_params` adds the rounded Doppler term to each wave's f64 phase and to the two detail ripples' phases (the same `water_flow_cycles`); **no shader change** and no PassU lane: the shader already reads one phase per wave. A zero `flow` gives exactly today's phases.
- **Luau (49g).** In the existing `water` table (`api_weather.cpp`): `water.set_flow{enabled=, dir=, speed=}` (clamped, named errors, nothing written on error, raises when the scene has no `WaterFlow`: the setter never adds one, as `water.set` never adds a `Water`), getters `water.flow_enabled()`, `water.flow_dir()`, `water.flow_speed()`, read-only `water.flow_at(x, z)` returning `vx, vz` (uniform today; the position argument is there for follow-up 2's flow maps). `water.height_at`, `normal_at` and `submerged` use the flowing wave set when a `WaterFlow` exists, so a float scripted on `height_at` stays on the drawn surface. Typed in `definitions.cpp`.

### Uniforms, cvars, stats, tiers

- **PassU.** One free vec4 becomes `p_water6` (x: reflection trace on, y: refraction on, z: the copy is bound at bank 7, w: 0), filled by `water_params`. `sigma` needs no lane: the shader computes it from `p_water2` (shallow rgb, depth scale) with `underwater_sigma`'s formula (`-log(clamp(shallow, 0.02, 1)) / max(depth_scale, 0.5)`), and the 49c test pins the shader's absorption against the CPU helper. PassU stays 1536 bytes (free vec4 4 to 3); the offset `static_assert`s and `MCE_GLSL_PASS_U` move together. Every other PassU fill already zeroes the free lanes, so captures, GI and the volumetric injection see the effects off.
- **Cvars** next to `cv_ssr` in `forward3d.cpp` (no header): `r.water.reflect` (bool, default 1, "Water reflects on-screen geometry; 0 = the sky only") and `r.water.refract` (bool, default 1, "Water shows what lies under it; 0 = the depth tint only"). No quality-tier row (`QualitySettings` is wide): both need the depth read, which the Low tier's look and a desc without the prepass pipelines already turn off. Flow has no cvar (authored data).
- **Stats** (49a, `forward3d.h`): `Forward3DStats::water_reflect` and `water_refract`, true when this frame's water draw ran the trace or the refraction. They let the tests ask what ran without a pixel guess and say whether the pass break happened.
- **Defaults.** Both effects are on by default: a water that reflects the pier standing in it is what the milestone is for, and it costs nothing in a scene without water. The goldens are kept byte-identical by rendering the water goldens that would move with both cvars at 0 (49b's audit names them), which also pins the off path; the on path is pinned by measured GL tests (below), not by a new golden.

### Determinism

Rendering is outside the state hash and the replay; the new shader math uses positions, the copy, the prepass depth and constants (no time beyond the existing wrapped phases, no `rand`). `WaterFlow` is saved data with no system and no state: the drift is a pure function of the scene time, so a save/load, a split run and two runs give the same height. A scene without `WaterFlow` hashes, saves and simulates exactly as today.

## Risks

- **The pass break on tiled GPUs.** Ending and reopening the main pass and copying the frame costs a full-size store and load on a tile-based GPU (mobile, Apple). Desktop cost is estimated at 0.05 to 0.15 ms at 1080p for the copy, **an estimate, measured on the GPU list**. The off switches return the single pass; a half-size copy is a possible later saving if the measurement asks for it.
- **The trace's cost.** 24 steps plus 4 bisections of a half-size texel fetch per water pixel; a full-screen sea at 1080p is the worst case, estimated at 0.3 to 0.6 ms on a mid GPU. The step count is a constant; `r.water.reflect 0` is the panic switch. llvmpipe cannot judge it.
- **Half-size depth against full-size colour.** At a silhouette the half-size depth can say "opaque" where the full-size copy holds the clear colour (the sky is not in the copy), so a refracted or reflected pixel can pick up the clear colour at an object's edge. Mitigation: the 2 x 2 nearest-behind rule `water_floor_depth` already uses, applied to the bent lookup; measured in 49c and 49d's silhouette tests.
- **Screen-space limits look wrong at times.** A tall object whose top leaves the screen loses the top of its reflection; a camera turning reveals and hides reflections. The fades keep it continuous; the docs say it; follow-up 1 is the real fix.
- **TAA.** The water is still not in the velocity prepass, so TAA reprojects it as sky; reflections and refractions of moving things smear more than the sky reflection did. Recorded; follow-up 3.
- **Flow quantisation.** The 64 s period rounds each wave's drift; a slow current leaves the long swell standing (Design 3). If that reads wrong, the fix is a longer common period for flowing water, which touches the kit's wrap and the renderer's (follow-up 4), not a per-frame accumulator (which would break determinism and the gameplay match).
- **Two copies with glass.** A frame with water effects and transmissive draws copies twice into one texture; if a backend does not order the read before the write, the fallback is a second texture, which needs a `forward3d.h` member (a second seam). 49c's glass-over-water case decides early.
- **Shader size.** About 20 KB of room in `kWaterFS`; the trace, the bend and the absorption are estimated at 3 to 5 KB.

## Follow-ups

1. Planar reflections for the water (a mirrored render of the scene, or of a reduced scene, into a half-size target): reflects what is off screen and behind other objects; the screen-space trace stays as the cheap tier.
2. Flow maps: a texture of per-position flow (a river bend, an eddy behind a rock) read by the shader and the kit; needs the waves to be advected per position (flow-map blending of two phases) and the gameplay height to follow it.
3. The water in the prepass (its own normal and depth target, not `ao_gbuf_`) and in the velocity prepass: SSR of other surfaces sees the water, TAA reprojects the surface correctly, the reflection and refraction stop smearing under motion.
4. A longer common period for flowing water (or per-scene), so slow currents move the long swell too.
5. Foam streaks and wakes that follow the current; foam advected along the flow.
6. Sloped water surfaces (a river that drops, a weir), and several water planes at different levels.
7. Caustics on the seabed, light shafts under water (with M-SUNSHAFTS), dispersion at the refraction edge.
8. 49a to 49h as built: one entry per step below, written when each lands.

## Deviations from the plan

- 49b: `water_refract_accept` also takes the surface's world height and the level: the plan's depth test alone lets a post's dry part, behind the water point but above the surface, refract into the water in front of it (a foreground leak the plan's box test would catch); `kWaterFS` reconstructs each half-size texel's height from its ray (`u_inv_view_proj`) and requires all 2 x 2 texels around the bent lookup to pass, instead of the 2 x 2 nearest-behind rule of `water_floor_depth`. `water_reflect_fade` takes a third argument, the ray's view-space z (the plan's "rays that point toward the camera"). Extra constants `kWaterTraceRefine`, `kWaterTraceFadeStart`, `kWaterFacingFade`, `kWaterOpticsPeriod`.
- 49c: `stats_.water_refract` / `water_reflect` carry `record_main`'s decision into `bind_main_state` (no `forward3d.h` member, as M-REFLECT2 48e did with `ssr_motion`); with several views in a frame the stats say what the last view's water did. Bank 7 is always bound for the water draw (the `ao_off_` stand-in when no copy ran), since `kWaterFS` declares the sampler. The deep-basin check compares against today's body at the same pixel (exactly equal), and the "stripes at the dry positions" check compares bright-or-dark classes per row (90 % or more agree) rather than edge positions.
- 49d: the march also refines after a depth step that follows a sample in front of a surface (a thin pillar between two samples), and weights the hit by the share of drawn half-size texels around it instead of rejecting hits next to an undrawn texel (the rejection cut the image at every silhouette's half-size edge). The mirror-position tolerance is **3 px, not 2** (measured 2.4 px, the half-size depth's quantisation). The reflection cases use a black body and the refraction off so the measured pixels are the reflection only (with the default tint the water's body dominates the 2-10 % fresnel reflection at these angles). The edge-fade case turns in quarter-degree steps over 20 degrees (bound: no step above 15 % of the peak), not tenth-degree steps. The width is measured across the image's side edges (the fresnel term varies down a column, not along a row). No gather (Design 2 allowed it).
- 49e: the helpers are declared in `water_flow.h` and defined in `src/water_flow.cpp` (not inline as `water.h`); `water_wave_set_flow` also clamps its velocity (-5..5, NaN 0) so a caller passing a raw vector meets the carrier's clamp; `water_flow.h` is also included by the three `engine_components.cpp` lists (as every component header). The extract test checks the velocity against `water_flow_velocity` bit for bit and against `speed * (cos, sin)` within 1e-5.
- 49f: "flow zero is byte-identical to the frame before 49f" is checked outside the test binary (the water goldens rewritten by the new build are byte-identical files); inside it, flow zero, negative zero, 1e-5 m/s and NaN are byte-identical to each other. `water_params` clamps `flow` again (-5..5, NaN 0), as it does every other carrier field.
- 49g: `water.flow_at` returns `0, 0` when the scene has a `Water` but no `WaterFlow` instead of raising (the still water's velocity, so a float script works in any water scene); it raises without a `Water`, like the other queries. The test file is `tests/script/test_script_water_flow.cpp` as planned; `submerged` is checked against the flowing height inside the script (1 mm either side) and on the C++ side through the height it read.
- 49h: the seabed has 24 stripes of 1 m (the first look at 16 stripes of 2 m showed one stripe across the bottom quarter of the frame), so the scene has 43 entities. Every box uses the plain `reflect_ball` material (the slightly emissive `reflect_pillar` washed the tints out). No new material, mesh or texture, so no cook golden changes. The guide's 9.19l and 9.19m got new paragraphs rather than rows (they are prose sections); the rows went into 8.3 and 27.3.
