# M-SUNSHAFTS — Sun light shafts: a screen-space radial scatter from the sun through the gaps in the scene (milestone record)

**Status:** 45a and 45b done (2026-11-07; data seam, pure helpers and quality tiers), 45c and 45d done (2026-11-07; the pass, the `SunShafts3D` component and the extract feed; independent review of the shader pending), 45e and 45f done (2026-11-07; sample scene, docs and close). **Milestone done** (the independent review of the 45c shader is still open). **Owner: routine 2 thread (the M-VISUAL line).** Follow-up of M-VISUAL3 (its "Not in scope" names "subsurface scattering and light shafts"), M-UNDERWATER (its "Not in scope" names "sun shafts"), M-LENSFLARE (the sun projection and the prepass read), M-HEATHAZE and M-LENSRAIN (the same post-pass pattern) and the 3D visual assessment (`assessments/3d-gorsellik-degerlendirmesi.md`, item 8, post-process). Today the renderer has volumetric fog (M-VISUAL 7: a froxel height fog that scatters the sun through its shadow cascades, so true shafts exist, but only inside the volume's `max_distance` of 48 m and only with `Volumetrics` on) and a sun lens flare, but a sun low behind trees, a building or a cloud deck throws no rays across the sky and the haze: with the volume off (or beyond 48 m) the picture has nothing between the sun disk and the geometry in front of it. No Linear issues (free issue limit); this record is the tracker.

**Naming:** no type, file, cvar or milestone called `SunShafts`, `sun_shafts`, `GodRays`, `godray`, `LightShafts` or `M-SUNSHAFTS` exists (`src/`, `docs/`, `tests/`, `samples/` checked 2026-11-07; the only hits for `shaft` are prose about the volumetric fog's shafts (`scene3d.h`, `shaders3d.h`, `kit3d.h`, the `M-VISUAL`, `M-VISUAL3`, `M-LIGHTCOOKIE`, `M-CLOUDS`, `M-GROUNDMIST`, `M-OUTLINE`, `M-LENSFLARE` and `M-UNDERWATER` records, `09-physics.md` and `08-3d.md`), the editor gizmo's "arrow shaft" (`gizmo.cpp`, `gizmo.h`), a design note's emissive beams (`docs/design/solstice-reach.md`), a wall-jump shaft in the platformer record (`M6-ember-step.md`) and a fog citation in `docs/research/rendering-3d.md`; none is a type, file or cvar). The render setting is `render::SunShafts` (in `PostFx::shafts`), the pure helpers live in `mce/render/sun_shafts.h`, the kit component is `SunShafts3D` (`mce/kit/sun_shafts.h`), the cvar is `r.sun_shafts`. Re-grep right before 45a: a milestone added in the meantime may take the names.

**Goal.** An opt-in screen-space radial scatter pass. For every pixel the pass walks a fixed number of samples along the line from the pixel toward the sun's screen position and accumulates how much *bright sun* it can see along that line: a sample counts only where the opaque prepass says sky (opaque geometry occludes) and the HDR frame there is brighter than a threshold (only the sun disk and its glow, and only where clouds do not dim it, are a source). The sum is tinted by the sun colour and the fog, faded by the sun's elevation, by the sun leaving the screen and by the sun being behind the camera, soft-clamped so it cannot blow out, and added to the HDR frame **before the lens flare, bloom and the tonemapper**. The result is the classic god-ray picture: rays fan out from the sun past the edges of a building, a tree line or a cloud gap, and fade with distance from the sun. **Render-side only: nothing is written to a component and nothing is hashed.** With `PostFx::shafts.enabled` false (the default), no `SunShafts3D`, the Low tier or `r.sun_shafts 0`, the pass is not recorded and the frame is bit-identical to today.

**Not in scope:** shafts through the water surface (M-UNDERWATER keeps its own look; the pass fades out with the submersion, see the pass design), shafts from point and spot lights or from the moon (the sun, that is `Scene3D::light()`, only), a physically based replacement of the volumetric fog (the volume stays as it is and the pass backs off where the volume is on), shafts that need shadow-map ray marching (screen space only: a caster that is off screen or not in the prepass casts no shaft; a transparent caster, which writes no depth, casts none either), per-light shaft scale (still the follow-up M-VISUAL3 named), a temporal history for the shafts (the pass runs after TAA on the resolved frame and keeps no state), a Luau API beyond the component fields, and orthographic views.

## Design

- **Render setting (seam, 45a).** A POD `render::SunShafts` in `scene3d.h` just above `PostFx`, and `SunShafts shafts;` in `PostFx` after `underwater`. Fields (ranges `apply_quality` clamps in 45b; NaN falls back to the default): `bool enabled = false` (off draws the frame without the pass); `f32 intensity = 1.0` (0..4, gain on the sun colour); `f32 length = 0.85` (0.1..1, how far toward the sun the walk goes, as a fraction of the pixel-to-sun distance); `f32 decay = 0.97` (0.8..1, the per-sample weight falloff, so the nearer part of the walk counts more); `f32 threshold = 1.0` (0.05..16, HDR luminance above which a sky sample is a source); `f32 cap = 1.5` (0..8, the most the pass can add per channel, applied as a soft clamp); `f32 visibility = 1.0` (0..1, the caller's dimming, the kit folds cloud cover into it as `LensFlare::visibility`); `f32 scatter_m = 60` (5..500, the view distance over which a geometry pixel gains its full veil; sky pixels always get it). `scene3d.h` and `forward3d.h` are over the 150 warning, so 45a is the seam PR for both, data only, merged first, with the `include_fanout.py` numbers: `Forward3DStats::sun_shafts` (bool) and `sun_shafts_strength`, and the private members 45c needs (`shafts_pipe_`, `shafts_comp_pipe_`, `shafts_buf_`, `record_sun_shafts`), the handles in the destroy list.
- **Where the sun comes from (no new data).** The sun's direction, colour and intensity are `Scene3D::light()` (the `DirectionalLight` the extract already fills from `Environment3D`, and, when a `DayCycle` is enabled, from the cycle's clock: `kit3d_extract.cpp` `scene.set_light(sun)`), exactly the direction the sky pass draws its disk with (`kSkyFS`: `-p_light_dir`), as `record_flare` does. So the shafts follow the day cycle, a moving sun and a scene-set sun with no extra authoring: at night the sun is below the horizon and the elevation fade is 0. The fog tint and amount come from `Scene3D::fog()` (the exp² `Fog`) and `Scene3D::volumetrics()`. Nothing else is read from the kit.
- **Pure CPU helpers (render, 45b).** Narrow header `mce/render/sun_shafts.h` (math only, forward-declares `struct SunShafts; struct Fog; struct Volumetrics; struct DirectionalLight;`), bodies in `src/sun_shafts.cpp`; f32, fixed evaluation order, no `rand`, no state; a NaN or infinite input gives a strength of exactly 0:
  - `bool sun_shafts_project(const mat4& view_proj, vec3 toward_sun, vec2& uv, f32& facing)`: the sun's uv in the fullscreen-pass convention (row 0 is v = 0) *without* clamping it to the screen (an off-screen sun gives a uv outside 0..1), and `facing` = `dot(camera forward, toward_sun)` read from the projection; false when clip `w <= 0` (behind the camera) or any input is not finite. The uv magnitude is clamped to 4 so a sun almost on the camera's side plane keeps finite numbers.
  - `f32 sun_shafts_elevation_fade(vec3 toward_sun, f32 high_floor)`: `smoothstep(-0.02, 0.04, y) * mix(1, high_floor, smoothstep(0.35, 0.9, y))`, so 0 at and below the horizon band (no pop at sunset), 1 for a low sun (the moment shafts look best) and a gentle drop toward `high_floor` (0.35) at noon, where the rays are short and a flat white veil is not wanted.
  - `f32 sun_shafts_view_fade(vec2 sun_uv, f32 facing)`: `smoothstep(0.0, 0.3, facing) * (1 - smoothstep(0.0, 0.75, border_distance(sun_uv)))` with `border_distance` the distance the uv lies outside the 0..1 square (0 inside). Behind the camera or at 90 degrees the shafts are gone; the sun may leave the screen by up to about three quarters of a screen before they are, so rays do not vanish the frame the disk touches the border (the flare's edge fade does that, shafts must not).
  - `f32 sun_shafts_medium(const Fog&, const Volumetrics&, bool volumetrics_drawn)`: the share of the picture that has air to scatter in: `0.35 + 0.65 * (1 - exp(-(density * 40)^2))` for the exp² fog (so a clear day keeps a faint ray and a hazy one a strong one), and **times 0.4 where the volumetric fog is drawn** (`stats_.volumetrics`: the froxel volume already scatters the sun with real shadows up to its distance, so the screen-space pass only adds the part the volume cannot reach and does not double it; recorded as a limit).
  - `vec3 sun_shafts_tint(vec3 sun_color, vec3 fog_color, f32 fog_amount)`: `sun_color * mix(vec3(1), fog_color / max(luma(fog_color), 1e-3), 0.5 * fog_amount)`, normalised so the tint changes hue and not brightness: a warm sun in warm haze gives orange rays, a pale sun in blue fog bluish ones.
  - `f32 sun_shafts_soft_clamp(f32 x, f32 cap)`: `cap * (1 - exp(-x / cap))` for `cap > 0`, 0 for `cap <= 0`: monotone, C1, equal to `x` for small `x` and never above `cap`. The shader applies the same function per channel (the port is pinned by the GPU test).
  - `u32 sun_shafts_samples(u32 detail)`: 0 (Low, off), 16 (Medium), 32 (High); anything else clamps to 32.
  - `u32 sun_shafts_hash(u32 x, u32 y, u32 seed)`: the integer hash the jitter in the shader mirrors, the lowbias32 `mix` of `heat_haze_hash` (`h = mix(x); h = mix(h ^ y * 0x85ebca6b); h = mix(h ^ seed * 0xc2b2ae35)`), pinned by goldens as in M-LENSRAIN 42b and M-HEATHAZE 43b.
  - `bool sun_shafts_frame(const SunShafts&, const DirectionalLight&, const Fog&, const Volumetrics&, bool volumetrics_drawn, const mat4& view_proj, f32 aspect, u32 detail, f32 submersion, SunShaftsFrame& out)`: false (the pass records nothing) when the setting is off, `intensity` 0 or `visibility` 0, the sun is behind the camera or below the horizon, the combined strength is 0 or any input is not finite; otherwise fills the whole uniform content (sun uv, aspect, length, decay, threshold, cap, sample count, tint times intensity times strength, the veil distance). `strength = elevation_fade * view_fade * medium * visibility * (1 - submersion)`.
- **Quality tiers + cvar `r.sun_shafts` (render, 45b).** `QualitySettings::sun_shafts` (Low false, Medium and High true) and `sun_shafts_samples` (Low 0, Medium 16, High 32), cvar `r.sun_shafts` (bool, Saved, "Sun light shafts (PostFx::shafts; off = the frame without it)") next to `r.underwater` in `quality.cpp`; `apply_quality` clamps the ranges and turns `enabled` off for the Low tier and `r.sun_shafts 0`. The sample count is a tier property read by the renderer, not a `SunShafts` field: the kit never sets it, so a scene cannot ask for 64 taps on a phone. The count is a shader uniform that picks one of two constant loop bounds, see the pass design.
- **Kit component: `SunShafts3D`, opt-in and separate (kit, 45d).** The sun and the fog already come from `Environment3D` and the `DayCycle` through `Scene3D::light()`, so no sun data is added. A switch is still needed: the pass must be off by default so that every existing golden and sample stays byte-identical, and nothing in the existing components can say "this scene wants shafts" without editing `kit3d.h` (wide, and `Environment3D` is `Save`d, so a field there would also move the scene format). So the one new thing is a separate, small, opt-in component in its own header (`mce/kit/sun_shafts.h`, the shape of `LensFlare3D` and `HeatHaze3D`; the first enabled one by entity index is read, registered by `install_kit3d` next to `LensFlare3D`; **`kit3d.h` stays untouched**). Fields (all `Save`): `enabled` 1, `intensity` 1 (0..4), `length` 0.85 (0.1..1), `decay` 0.97 (0.8..1), `threshold` 1 (0.05..16), `cap` 1.5 (0..8), `cloud_follow` 0.5 (0..1: how far the cloud cover dims the shafts on top of the dimming the sky pass already gives the sun disk, which is why the default is half), `scatter_m` 60 (5..500), explicit zeroed `pad_`. No runtime state: it never hashes anything. `Kit3DExtract` (`.cpp` only) fills `post.shafts` from the first enabled component and `visibility = 1 - cloud_follow * cloud_cover` (the post-`apply_quality` cover, as the flare; rain comes in through the cover the weather already raises, no separate rain term), and runs it through `render::apply_quality`.
- **Determinism.** The shafts are a pure function of the camera, the sun, the fog, the settings, this frame's depth and HDR frame and a fixed per-pixel hash; they never feed the simulation. The jitter is a hash of the half-resolution pixel and a constant seed, **not** of the frame number: the pass runs after TAA, so a per-frame jitter would only flicker (there is no history to average it away), while a fixed pattern is stable, bit-reproducible and the same on every backend. A scene without the component is byte-identical.

## Pass design (45c)

- **Where it sits.** `record_sun_shafts(cmd, scene)` runs right after `record_post_fx` (depth of field, motion blur, the exposure histogram) and **before `record_flare`**: the order is TAA, depth of field and motion blur, exposure histogram, **sun shafts**, flare, underwater, lens rain, heat haze, then `record_post` (bloom, lens dirt, tonemap, grade). Reasons, in order of weight: (1) *before bloom*, as asked: bloom, the lens dirt and the tonemapper then act on the rays like on any bright light; (2) *before the flare*, because the flare's halo and ghosts are bright, smooth and radial about the same point, and if they were in the frame the shafts' source mask would pick the halo up and smear it a second time along every ray (a double glare that no tuning can remove), while the shafts under the flare cost nothing; (3) *after the exposure histogram*, as the flare: the rays do not pump the auto exposure (a low sun behind trees would otherwise darken the whole frame as the rays appear); (4) *after TAA*: the pass must not enter the history the next frame reprojects, and TAA would otherwise smear the radial pattern. Underwater, the lens rain and the heat haze come after: the camera under water attenuates the shafts like everything else, a lens drop refracts them, a haze volume bends them. Same ping-pong as the flare and the haze: reads `post_source_`, writes `post_fx_target(post_size_)`, then `post_source_` = that target (never in place: with TAA `post_source_` is the history slot). Skipped for render-to-texture views (`out_.linear_output`: a monitor in the scene does not get the player's rays), like the lens rain. `sun_shafts_frame` is checked first: off, a sun behind the camera, below the horizon or a zero strength return before anything is created, drawn or touched, so the off frame is bit-identical. The cvar and the setting are read every frame; the pipelines are created at startup in `create_pipelines` (as the lens rain: a refusal is per renderer and there is no first-use compile hitch).
- **Interaction with the underwater look.** `submersion` comes from the M-UNDERWATER helper `underwater_submersion` (the camera's depth below the water plane through the same crossing band), so the shafts fade out over the same band the underwater look fades in over and there is no frame where both are strong; this reads `Scene3D::water()` and the camera only (no new data) and includes the narrow `mce/render/underwater.h` from `sun_shafts.cpp` or `forward3d.cpp`. If that coupling proves awkward in 45b the fallback is to skip the pass when `underwater_frame` is true, which is the same on the frames that matter.
- **Depth.** The main depth buffer is not sampled, so the pass reads the half-resolution opaque prepass `ao_gbuf_` (`a` = view depth, 0 for sky), through `record_prepass` (a no-op when SSAO, SSR, contact shadows, the water, the flare, the outline, the underwater or the haze already drew it this frame). Cost when none of those is on: one half-resolution opaque prepass; recorded in the risks. The prepass holds opaques only, so **sky counts as a bright source and opaque geometry occludes**; transparent things (glass, particles) write no depth and therefore never occlude (a limit). Clouds are drawn in the sky pass, write no depth and dim the sun disk in the HDR frame (`kSkyFS` `apply_clouds`: "a cloud in front of the sun dims it"), so the cloud gaps shape the rays through the *HDR* part of the mask with no cloud-specific code in the pass.
- **Two passes, half resolution.** Accumulating 32 samples (two taps each) per full-resolution pixel is the expensive part, and shafts are a low-frequency picture, so the accumulation runs at half resolution into `shafts_buf_` (R11G11B10F, the HDR frame's own format, half of `post_size_`, recreated on a size change by the first view that draws shafts, unlike `outline_dst_`, which uses the frame's first view; the usual per-view rule: never free a handle recorded earlier in the same frame) and a composite pass at full resolution adds it. Pass 1 (`kShaftsFS`) reads the HDR frame (`kAlbedo`, linear clamp) and the prepass (`kNormal`, point), writes `shafts_buf_`. Pass 2 (`kShaftsCompFS`) reads `post_source_` (`kAlbedo`, point) and `shafts_buf_` (`kOrm`, linear clamp), writes `post_fx_target`: `out = src + upsampled`. The composite is where the pixel's own veil is applied (one prepass tap at its half-resolution texel), so the buffer holds the unveiled rays.
- **Shader (`kShaftsFS`, GLSL 450).** `ShaftsU` (bank `kPass`, a C++ mirror `struct ShaftsU` in `forward3d.cpp` with `static_assert`s on `sizeof` and every `offsetof`, std140: only `vec4`, as `HazeU` and `UnderwaterU`): `sun` (uv xy, aspect, length), `shape` (decay, threshold, cap, sample count as float), `color` (rgb tint x intensity x strength, w seed as float), = 3 `vec4`, 48 bytes (final count as built; the composite adds `comp` = veil distance and texel size, in a second block or the same). Per pixel, with `uv` the pixel centre and `uv_sun` possibly off screen:
  1. `delta = (uv_sun - uv)`; the step is `delta * length / N`, so a pixel far from the sun walks a long line and one next to it a short one (the usual radial blur, its fall-off with distance from the sun is the walk length, plus `decay`). The start is offset by `jitter = sun_shafts_hash(px, py, seed) * (1 / 4294967296.0)` times one step, so the 16 or 32 samples of neighbouring pixels interleave instead of banding (a fixed pattern, see determinism).
  2. For `i` in `0 .. N-1` (a constant bound of 32; an early `break` on the uniform sample count, **uniform control flow**, so Medium runs 16 iterations of the same code as High's 32): `p = uv + step * (float(i) + jitter)`; `w = decay^i` kept as a running product (no `pow`); a sample outside `0..1` contributes 0 and does not clamp (clamping would smear the border pixel along the ray; an off-screen sun therefore loses rays smoothly as the walk leaves the picture, on top of the CPU fade); `sky = textureLod(prepass, p, 0).a == 0.0` taken as a `step` value (nearest tap, sky is exactly 0 as in the flare's occlusion pass), `lum = dot(textureLod(src, p, 0).rgb, vec3(0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722))`, `src_mask = smoothstep(threshold, threshold * 2.0, lum)` (the **bright-sun mask**: only a source brighter than the threshold counts, so the sky gradient, a bright wall and a white cloud do not radiate, and only the sun disk and its glow do), `acc += w * sky * src_mask * lum_weight` where `lum_weight = min(lum / threshold, 16.0)` (the disk is much brighter than its glow: the rays carry that, bounded). All `textureLod` with an explicit level 0, no derivatives, no `texture()` in the loop.
  3. `rays = acc * (1 / N_norm)` where `N_norm` = sum of the weights of the iterations run (computed in the same loop, so 16 and 32 samples give the same brightness, only a different smoothness); the result is `shafts = soft_clamp(rays * color.rgb, cap)` per channel, with the soft clamp the CPU helper defines (`cap * (1 - exp(-x / cap))`, `exp` of a non-positive argument only), so no `x` or tint can produce more than `cap` per channel.
  4. Composite: `veil = sky_here ? 1.0 : 1.0 - exp(-view_depth / scatter_m)` (a pixel on geometry near the camera gets almost no veil, a far one more: the air in front of a pixel is what scatters the light, so a wall at 2 m does not glow like the sky behind it), `out = src + upsampled * veil`. At strength 0 the pass is not recorded, so there is no `mix` term that survives at zero.
- **Fades and no-pop rules (what the pass must satisfy).** Elevation: `sun_shafts_elevation_fade` is 0 at and below the horizon band and continuous above it. Off screen: `sun_shafts_view_fade` falls to 0 smoothly over three quarters of a screen beyond the border, and the in-shader out-of-range rule lets the rays leave the picture sample by sample. Behind the camera: `facing` fades to 0 by 0.3 before the sun passes the camera's side plane, and `sun_shafts_project` is false from clip `w <= 0`, so the projected uv (which flips there) is never used. Cloud cover and rain: the HDR source dims by itself where a cloud covers the disk, and `visibility` (kit) dims it further by `cloud_follow * cover`, both continuous. Fog: the medium factor is continuous in the density, and only the volumetric switch (`stats_.volumetrics`, a scene's property, not a per-frame flicker) steps it by 0.4. A GPU test sweeps the sun across and past the screen border, up through the horizon and around behind the camera in small steps and bounds the largest pixel-mean difference between adjacent frames.
- **TAA.** The pass runs on the resolved frame after TAA and never writes the history. The prepass is drawn with the jittered projection and the HDR frame is the unjittered resolve, so the occlusion taps are off by under one half-resolution texel from the resolved picture; the rays are soft and the half-resolution buffer is blurry on purpose, so this is not corrected (the flare uses the jittered projection for its single occlusion tap; the shafts use `view.view_proj` unjittered for the sun uv, the same as the haze). Recorded as a limit.
- **Per backend.** Plain GLSL 450 like every pass; the banks the post passes use (`kAlbedo` for the source, `kNormal` for the prepass, `kOrm` for the buffer in the composite), one uniform block, no SSBO, no compute, no derivatives (no `texture()` without a level, no `dFdx`, so nothing in non-uniform flow), explicit `textureLod`/`texelFetch`, constant loop bounds (32, with a uniform `break`), `exp` only of non-positive numbers, `max(x, 1e-3)` before every division, no `pow`, the hash on `uint` with the same wrap-around multiplies as the haze's `MCE_GLSL_HEAT_HAZE_HASH` (a new macro `MCE_GLSL_SUN_SHAFTS_HASH` so the GL test runs it alone and compares 64 hashes with `sun_shafts_hash` bit for bit, GL and SDL GPU). That is what lets the SPIR-V (lavapipe) and the Metal and WGSL cross paths take it unchanged; the new shader joins the `test_shader_spirv` and `test_shader_cross` cases.

## Risks

- **Look.** The ray length, the decay, the threshold, the cap, the tint strength and the elevation knee are look choices only a real display can judge; llvmpipe pixels prove placement, occlusion, the fades, determinism and the off checks. Goes to the GPU list (row A18).
- **Prepass cost and availability.** The pass reads the half-resolution opaque prepass, drawn once a frame if no other pass drew it, and the pass only runs while the sun is in front of the camera above the horizon. The prepass pipeline exists only where SSAO, SSR, contact shadows or the edge outline are on at startup (the outline is on by default); when all four are off the pass records nothing at all, even with the component on (one `MCE_LOG_WARN` per process says so, added with 45d), like the flare and the haze. Cost of the pass itself: Medium 16 and High 32 iterations of two taps at half resolution plus one full-resolution composite; the real cost is a GPU-list question.
- **Screen-space limits.** A caster that is off screen, or a thin one the half-resolution prepass misses, casts no shaft; a caster that leaves the screen takes its shaft with it. Transparent casters (glass, particles, foliage cards drawn alpha-blended) write no depth and cast none. Recorded as limits; the volumetric fog remains the right tool for shafts that must be exact.
- **Overlap with the volumetric fog.** With `Volumetrics` on the volume already draws real shafts inside 48 m; the pass backs off to 0.4 of its strength and adds the far part. A scene that wants only the volume's shafts leaves the component off. The factor is a first guess; real-GPU tuning, row A18.
- **A source that is not the sun.** The mask is brightness plus sky, so a very bright emissive sky element (a moon at a high exposure, a lightning flash in the sky pass) above the threshold would also radiate toward the *sun's* uv, which is wrong. The moon is dim in the HDR frame by design and a flash is brief; recorded, with the threshold as the lever. A source-position test (only pixels within an angular radius of the sun count) is the follow-up if it shows.
- **Half-resolution edges.** The rays are soft, but a bright sky seen through a one-pixel gap can leak a faint ray through the gap; the composite's veil and the half-resolution blur hide most of it. Recorded.
- **Auto exposure.** The pass runs after the histogram, so a scene that is mostly rays does not adapt its exposure to them (as the flare). Recorded.
- **Orthographic views** are unsupported: the sun projection assumes a perspective camera.

## GPU verification

Headless (llvmpipe, lavapipe): off equals the plain frame exactly (the setting, the cvar, the Low tier, a sun below the horizon and a sun behind the camera); with the sun in front and a column between it and a patch of sky, pixels along the line from the column's far side toward the sun are brighter than the same pixels without the pass and pixels on the opposite side are unchanged; opaque geometry occludes (a wall across the whole sky gives no rays) and the sky does not (an empty sky gives the full rays); a cloud in front of the sun dims the rays through the HDR mask; Medium (16) and High (32) agree in mean brightness within a tolerance and differ in smoothness; the output never exceeds `cap` above the source per channel at an extreme intensity; the same frame twice gives the same bytes; the sweep test (no pop); the TAA history stays free of the rays; the Low tier is a no-op; with `Volumetrics` on the added energy is the 0.4 share. ## Deviations from the plan

- 45b: `sun_shafts_project` normalizes `toward_sun` itself and reads `facing` as the clip `w` of the direction (`view_proj * (dir, 0)`), which equals `dot(camera forward, dir)` for a perspective projection; it limits each uv component (not the magnitude) to +-4. `sun_shafts_frame` returns false also when the tier's detail is 0 (no samples, the Low tier), and takes `detail` as the tier index (0 / 1 / 2) that `sun_shafts_samples` maps to 0 / 16 / 32. The fog amount that drives `sun_shafts_tint` inside the frame is the haze term of the medium factor, `1 - exp(-(density * 40)^2)` (the plan left it open); the `Volumetrics` argument of `sun_shafts_medium` is unused (the drawn flag carries the state). `sun_shafts_soft_clamp` treats a negative `x` as 0 and NaN as 0.
- 45c: the decay is per 1/32 of the walk, not per sample: the CPU passes `decay^(32 / N)` as the per-sample factor (`decay` for 32, `decay^2` for 16), so the weight is a function of the position along the walk and the normalized result is the same for 16 and 32 samples (with a per-sample `decay` the 16-sample walk would weigh its far end more, and the two tiers would differ in brightness whenever the source is not uniform along the walk). `ShaftsU` is one block of 4 `vec4` (64 bytes) shared by both passes (`comp.x` the veil distance; the texel size the plan mentioned is not needed: both passes use explicit-level taps by uv). `s_sun.z` (aspect) rides along but the walk is in uv and does not use it. The out-of-range rule multiplies each tap by an inside factor instead of branching around it (both taps are still taken, at an explicit level, clamped by the sampler), which keeps the loop body uniform. The composite reads the source with `texelFetch` (bank `kAlbedo`, point) and the prepass for the veil at bank `kNormal`. The submersion is computed in `record_sun_shafts` from `underwater_submersion` / `underwater_band` whether or not the underwater look is on (the fallback of skipping on `underwater_frame` was not needed). A later view of a different size than `shafts_buf_` in the same frame accumulates into its `trans_src_` (render size, full resolution: more costly, the same picture) rather than recreating the shared buffer; when `trans_src_` is that view's `post_source_` (the outline wrote it and no later pass moved it) that view draws no shafts. The default `threshold` stays 1.0 (measured, see 45c as built). The stale "declared only until then" comment on `record_sun_shafts` in `forward3d.h` is left for 45f or a cleanup (a comment edit there rebuilds about 185 TUs).
- 45c tests: the gate's sweep "around behind the camera" cannot show the facing fade with the fixture's 45-degree field of view (the disk, the only source, leaves the screen at about 29 degrees off axis, long before the facing fade acts), so that sweep checks that nothing is added from just past the border to behind the camera and that the pass is off at the end; the border sweep moves the sun 1 px a step because the rays leave with the disk (about 10 px of motion), which is the designed behaviour. The bright-sun mask's source is the disk only on a gradient sky (no glow beyond the disk), so at the default `length` 0.85 the rays reach about r / (1 - length), 6.7 disk radii (13 degrees), from the sun; a look question for row A18. The "the Volumetrics share" gate is checked on the strength the renderer drew with (exactly 0.4 times), not on added pixel energy (the volume changes the frame under the rays).
- 45d: the first enabled component by entity index is read (the plan's wording), unlike `LensFlare3D` (the first in ECS order, enabled or not). No `MCE_LOG_WARN` lives in the extract: the one-time warning for a missing prepass pipeline is in `record_sun_shafts` (45c), where the pipeline state is known, as the haze does it.
