# M-RAINBOW — An opt-in rainbow: sunlit sky after rain, drawn as a view-direction layer (milestone record)

**Status:** planned (2026-10-30), steps 17a to 17e. **Owner: routine 2 thread (the M-VISUAL line).** Follow-up of M-GROUNDMIST (which listed the rainbow under "not in scope: separate ideas, rainbow and aurora need shader work"), M-AURORA (the sky-layer pattern: kit component, `render::` carrier seam, `Kit3DExtract` feed, one shader step, Luau) and M-WEATHER / M-WETSURFACE (rain, wetness and the clouds the level reads). Today the sky has the gradient or atmosphere, the sun disk, clouds, the moon and stars (M-NIGHTSKY) and the aurora (M-AURORA); nothing happens in the sky when rain ends and the sun comes out. No Linear issues (free issue limit); this record is the tracker. The shader step is Opus-written and merged after an independent Opus review plus a green `--task` (as M-CLOUDS 9b, M-NIGHTSKY 14d, M-WATER 15c and M-AURORA 16c).

**Goal.** An opt-in kit component `Rainbow` describes a rainbow: a strength, the width of the bands and a switch for the faint secondary bow. Pure helpers turn the sun's elevation, the weather (wetness left on the ground, rain still falling, cloud cover) into a level, and an angle from the anti-solar point into a band intensity and a spectral colour. `Kit3DExtract` hands the result to the renderer as a small `render::RainbowSky` carrier and one shader step adds a primary bow at about 42 degrees from the anti-solar point (red outside, violet inside) and a fainter, colour-reversed secondary bow at about 51 degrees with the darker Alexander band between them, as additive emission in the sky pass in the shared `MCE_GLSL_SKY_LAYERS` string, so the water reflects it with no second copy. Deterministic, saved with the scene, opt-in; a scene without `Rainbow` hashes, simulates and renders exactly as today.

**Not in scope:** moonbows, fogbows, sun dogs, halos or glories (other optical effects), a real scattering model or drop-size distribution (the bow is a fixed spectral ramp), supernumerary bows, the rainbow reflecting in puddles or wet surfaces (the layer is sky emission only; the water gets the reflection through the sky function), a rainbow in the IBL bake, reflection probes or GI captures (main sky pass and water reflection only, like clouds, the night sky and the aurora), a rainbow under an equirect `sky_texture` (the texture wins), parallax or a ground-anchored bow (the bow is a pure function of the view direction and the sun direction, so it follows the camera like the stars), several simultaneous rainbows, sound, and changing `Weather`, `WetSurfaces`, `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Moon`, `Aurora`, `Water` or `Environment3D` bytes (the last is a wide header).

## Design

- **Opt-in component, no system, no state.** New component `Rainbow` (`mce/kit/rainbow.h`, one per scene, the first in ECS order is read, registered by `install_weather` next to `Aurora`; 17a confirms the spot). Authoring (all `Save`, with a `Range`): `enabled` 1; `strength` 1 (0..4, brightness gain); `band_width` 1 (0.5..2, scales the angular width of the bands); `secondary` 1 (0..1, strength of the secondary bow; 0 draws none); `min_wetness` 0.25 (0..1, the ground wetness below which no bow shows); `max_sun_elevation_deg` 42 (10..60, the sun height above which the bow is below the horizon and fades out, with a 10 degree smoothstep). No engine-written fields; explicit zeroed pad and a `test_padding` entry.
- **Pure helpers (kit, 17a).** In `rainbow.h`, inline, f32, fixed evaluation order, no `rand`: `rainbow_level(const Rainbow&, f32 sun_elevation_deg, f32 wetness, f32 rain_intensity, f32 cloud_coverage) -> f32` is `enabled * strength * sun_gate * wet_gate * (1 - rain_intensity) * (1 - cloud_coverage)^2` with a smoothstep sun gate (0 below the horizon, full above 3 degrees, fading out toward `max_sun_elevation_deg`), so it shows after the rain stops with the sun out and fades as the ground dries; `rainbow_intensity(const Rainbow&, f32 angle_deg) -> f32` the brightness of the two bows at an angle from the anti-solar point (a smooth band around 42.0 degrees about 1.8 wide and a fainter one around 51.0 about 3.0 wide times `secondary`, and a slight darkening between them); `rainbow_color(f32 angle_deg) -> vec3` the spectral ramp across a bow (red on the outer edge, violet on the inner edge, reversed for the secondary), a fixed polynomial table so it is bit-stable. NaN, inverted and zero ranges fall back to the defaults; a zero level or strength returns exactly 0, which the render off-check relies on.
- **Render carrier (seam, 17b).** `render::RainbowSky {level, band_width, secondary, anti_solar (xyz, unit), enabled, pad_}` in `scene3d.h` next to `AuroraSky`, `set_rainbow()` / `rainbow()`, `QualitySettings::rainbow` and cvar `r.rainbow` (Low is off; `apply_quality` by-value overload clamps ranges, normalises the direction, NaN to 0, zeroes `level` for `r.rainbow 0` or the Low tier), exactly the shape of 16b. `scene3d.h` is a wide header (`include_fanout.py` reported 145 of 891 TUs at M-AURORA 16b, under the 150 warning but a rebuild of every consumer), so this ships as its own small seam PR merged first, with the reason and the current number in the description.
- **Extract feed (kit, 17b).** `Kit3DExtract::apply_environment` fills `render::RainbowSky` from the first `Rainbow`, the sun direction it already has, `Weather` (wetness, rain intensity, `current`) and the cloud coverage it already computes (`.cpp` only). Nothing is set with no `Rainbow`, `enabled` 0, a level of 0 or an equirect `sky_texture`; the carrier is written every frame (default when off) so a stale one never survives, as in 16b. The bow needs the sun above the horizon, so a scene with no clock and a fixed `sun_direction` works from that direction.
- **Sky shader (Opus, 17c).** `rainbow_layer(vec3 c, vec3 d, float pix)` in the shared `MCE_GLSL_SKY_LAYERS` string behind `p_rainbow.x > 0.0` so a frame without a rainbow is bit-identical. The angle from the anti-solar point is `acos(dot(d, anti_solar))`, the intensity and spectral colour are the shader copies of `rainbow_intensity` / `rainbow_color` (constants as a commented block with a pixel test comparing them at probe points), the layer is additive, fades below the horizon (`smoothstep(0, 0.1, d.y)`), is band-limited by `pix` so the thin violet edge does not alias or crawl, and is applied in `kSkyFS` after the aurora and before the cloud composite and the fog, so a cloud in front dims it and fog hides it. `water_sky` calls the same function on the mirrored ray behind the same off-check. PassU: two new `vec4` (`p_rainbow`: level, band width, secondary, spare; `p_rainbow2`: anti-solar xyz, spare) taken from the 12 free `vec4` of the 1536-byte block (`p_free[12]` becomes `p_free[10]`, the size stays 1536); 17c re-checks this before writing the shader. The Low tier draws none.
- **Luau (17d).** `weather.set_rainbow{strength=, band_width=, secondary=, min_wetness=, max_sun_elevation=, enabled=}`, getters and read-only `weather.rainbow_level()` from the same helper (the level reads the current sun elevation, wetness, rain and cloud cover), named errors, values clamped, nothing written on error, a missing `Rainbow` raises, typed in `definitions.cpp`, same rules as `weather.set_aurora`. The sample: `samples/12_weather` already runs rain and clear spells with a clock; 17d adds a `Rainbow` to its Weather entity and a log line `rainbow:` every 150 ticks if that scene's weather script already has a rain-to-clear transition with the sun up (otherwise it adds one to the script), and only that sample's expectations move.
- **Determinism and hash.** A scene without `Rainbow` is byte-identical. The level and the colours are pure functions of authoring, the sun elevation, the weather state and the cloud coverage, so two runs agree and a save/load gives the same values. The layer has no time term, so there is nothing to wrap.

## Gates
- A scene without `Rainbow` (or with `enabled` 0, a closed level, night, rain or a dry ground) has an unchanged state hash and unchanged frames; every existing sample hash and golden stays byte-identical except `12_weather`'s expectations (17a scene, 17d log), and existing goldens under `tests/render_gl/*` stay byte-identical through 17b and 17c on both backends.
- `Weather`, `WetSurfaces`, `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Moon`, `Aurora`, `CloudLayer`, `Water` and `Environment3D` are never written by rainbow code (a test hashes them with and without `Rainbow`).
- Level, intensity and colour are pure functions of authoring, sun elevation, weather state and cloud coverage. The shader and the kit helper agree (pixel test of 17c).
- PassU stays 1536 bytes with 10 free `vec4` afterwards; no other pass's block changes.
- Include fan-out: the only wide header touched is `scene3d.h`, in the 17b seam PR; `rainbow.h` is included only by the extract, the install, the script binding and the tests.
- Slow tests: a GPU test over 60 s ships a smoke variant and the long version is labelled `slow` with `mce_mark_slow`.

## Risks
- **Look and cost.** The layer runs over every up-going sky pixel but is a few arithmetic operations plus an `acos` and a ramp lookup, cheaper than the aurora; 17c measures what it can and records that `perf_run` at 1080p needs a GPU box.
- **Narrow bands alias.** The violet edge is under a degree wide; 17c band-limits by the pixel footprint and includes the yaw frame-difference test.
- **Where the sun is.** With the sun high the bow is under the horizon; the level fades by `max_sun_elevation_deg` and the guide explains why a noon scene shows none. A scene whose sun is fixed below the horizon shows none by design.
- **Rain state.** `Weather` has no "just stopped raining" event, so the level uses wetness (which dries on the existing curve) and the current rain intensity; the guide says the bow follows the drying of the ground, not a timer.
- **Interaction with the aurora, night sky, clouds and fog.** Day and night are disjoint (the rainbow needs the sun up, the others need night), but the order is fixed anyway (after the aurora, before the clouds and fog) and tested.
