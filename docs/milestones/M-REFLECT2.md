# M-REFLECT2 — Screen-space reflections, second pass: rough reflections blur, bumpy surfaces, moving hits (milestone plan)

**Status:** done 2026-11-08: 48a to 48c built (first PR), 48d and 48e built (second PR), 48f built (third PR: the sample, the docs and this closure). Steps 48a to 48f, in two PRs (48a+48b+48c, 48d+48e+48f); every step is small and none edits a wide header except 48a (one header, `forward3d.h`). **Owner: the M-VISUAL routine 2 thread.** Follow-up of M-VISUAL2 step 1 (screen-space reflections, shipped: `render::ScreenSpaceReflections`, `kSsrFS`, `Forward3D::record_ssr`, lit shader bank `bank::kSsr` = 3). It takes the three "known limits" that `docs/milestones/M-VISUAL2.md` step 1 lists and decides each one honestly: moving objects reflect where they were, the prepass has no normal maps, rough reflections fade to the probes instead of blurring. No Linear issues (free limit).

**Naming:** SSR already exists and is **not rebuilt**. The cvar `r.ssr` (`forward3d.cpp`, `cv_ssr`) exists and stays the one off switch; this milestone adds no `r.ssr` and no second SSR setting. `git grep -niE 'reflect2|ssr_blur|ssr\.blur|ssr\.motion'` finds nothing on 2026-11-08 (checked on `origin/master` b997e59a). The only new names are the two A/B cvars `r.ssr.blur` and `r.ssr.motion` (48c, 48e) and the stats bools of 48a.

**Goal.** Make the existing reflections hold up on the surfaces a game has most of: a roughness between 0.1 and about 0.8 shows a *blurred* version of what the screen saw instead of fading to the sky and probes; a normal-mapped floor does not show a mirror-flat reflection (or none); a character or a crate that moves reflects where it is, not where the camera alone would put it. Every change is in the shaders and `forward3d.cpp`; no scene format, no component, no setting and no quality tier changes. With SSR off, or at roughness below the blur threshold on a static scene, the frame is byte-identical to today (the `ssr_room` golden and every other golden stay).

**Not in scope:** a new SSR, a second reflection system, planar reflections, ray-traced or hierarchical (Hi-Z) tracing, a denoiser or temporal accumulation of the reflection (the TAA already resolves the jitter), contact-hardening blur from the hit distance (needs the distance stored; see the follow-ups), a change to `ScreenSpaceReflections`, `Environment3D` or `QualitySettings` (all wide: `scene3d.h`, `kit3d.h`, `quality.h`), reflections of transmissive, water or translucent surfaces, reflecting the back faces, Metal or WebGPU verification (shader text is shared; the check goes to the GPU list).

## Investigation (what the code does today; the plan below rests on these facts)

- **The trace.** `record_ssr` (`forward3d.cpp`) draws one full-screen pass at **half size** into `ssr_` (RGBA16F: rgb = last frame's HDR color at the hit, a = confidence). `kSsrFS` reads `ao_gbuf_` (the prepass: RGBA16F, **view normal xyz, view depth w**, 0 = nothing drawn) and `hdr_` (last frame's resolved HDR) and knows nothing else: no roughness, no material, no velocity. It marches the mirror ray of the *geometric* normal, refines with four bisections, reprojects the hit with `ssr_prev_vp_ * inverse(view)` (the **camera only**) and reads `hdr_` there.
- **The consumer.** `kMeshFS` (the lit main pass) already has the material's roughness `rough` (after the maps, decals, wet, snow) and the mapped normal `n`. When `p_ssr.x > 0.5` it takes **one bilinear tap** of `u_ssr` at the pixel's own screen position, `w = s.a * clamp((p_ssr.y - rough) / p_ssr.z, 0, 1)` and `amb_spec = mix(amb_spec, s.rgb * spec_brdf * p_ssr.w, w)`: a **sharp** reflection that fades out between `max_roughness - roughness_fade` and `max_roughness` (defaults 0.5 and 0.2; kit fields `ssr_max_roughness`, `ssr_roughness_fade`). Roughness is a main-pass quantity; the trace never sees it.
- **No mips, and no free slot.** `bank::kSsr` = 3 is "the last free fragment slot of 16", so a second texture (a blurred copy) cannot be bound to the main pass. The RHI renders into a layer and a viewport but **not into a mip** and has no texture copy (the cookie atlas in `record_cookie_bake` works around it with packed levels), and a pass cannot sample the texture it writes. A blur pyramid of `ssr_` therefore needs ping-pong targets plus a compose pass, or a slot we do not have. This decides the blur design below.
- **The prepass is shared.** `ao_gbuf_` is read by SSAO, SSR, contact shadows, the sun shafts, the flare occlusion, the outline, depth of field, the light shafts and the water depth (about a dozen bind sites, `forward3d.cpp` 2313 to 4246). Its pipelines are `ao_prepass_`, `ao_prepass_skinned_`, `ao_prepass_inst_` plus the batch draws. `kAoPrepassFS` writes `normalize(mat3(u_view) * n)` of the **interpolated vertex normal** and binds only the albedo array (alpha mask); it has no normal map, no tangent, no material flags beyond the alpha cutoff.
- **The main pass's normal is not one thing.** `kMeshFS` builds `n` from the normal map (`perturb_normal`, derivative based, `o_maps.x`), the **terrain** splat layers (`MCE_TERRAIN`, triplanar layers), decals, wet surfaces (`apply_wet` sees the geometric normal), snow, ripples and anisotropy. A prepass normal that equals it would have to repeat all of that.
- **Velocity exists, but later.** `record_velocity` writes `velocity_` (RGBA16F at the frame's size: rg = the current minus previous screen position, in uv units, **unjittered**; b = 1, or rg = 16 when the point is behind last frame's camera; a = view depth) for TAA, motion blur and depth of field (`PostFx::needs_velocity()`), skinned and instanced draws included (instanced runs "never move", previous = current). It is recorded **after** `record_main` and the MSAA resolve (`forward3d.cpp` around line 1835), i.e. after `record_ssr`, so the current frame's velocity is not yet there when SSR runs. With none of TAA, motion blur or depth of field on, no velocity exists at all.
- **Which scenes this reaches.** SSR is on from the Medium tier up (`QualitySettings::ssr`, off on Low); TAA (and so velocity) is on in the High tier. The golden `ssr_room` (mirror floor, `render3d_gl` mesh 7) and the 06_showcase profiles are the only SSR consumers in the tests; no golden has a normal-mapped SSR surface or a moving SSR hit (checked again in 48c, 48d, 48e: the first sub-step of each is the audit).

## Design

### 1. Rough reflections: a roughness-driven cone gather in the main pass (48c)

Options weighed:

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A. Blur inside `kSsrFS` by hit distance and roughness (a real cone trace) | **No.** The trace has no roughness (the prepass holds normal and depth only). Adding it means repacking the prepass (a dozen readers) or a second MRT: out of this milestone's size. |
| B. Mip chain of the hit colour (sample `hdr_` at a lod) | **No.** `hdr_` has no mips, the RHI cannot render a mip, and the pyramid costs a full-size chain per frame for a feature few pixels use. |
| C. Pyramid of `ssr_` (half, quarter, eighth) read by lod in `kMeshFS` | **Later, if B-tap noise is not acceptable** (follow-up 1). Needs ping-pong targets, a compose pass into a packed atlas and uv remapping in the main shader. The cleanest look, the most plumbing. |
| **D. A rotated disc gather over `ssr_` in `kMeshFS`, radius from roughness** | **Chosen.** The shader already has `rough`; no new target, pass, slot or pipeline; a pixel pays only if it is a reflecting rough pixel. Noise is the cost, and TAA (High tier) removes it. |

- **The gather.** In the `p_ssr.x > 0.5` branch, a pixel with `rough <= kSsrBlurMinRough` (0.08, a constant in the shader and in the pure helper) keeps the **one tap it takes today, bit for bit**. Above it, `kSsrTaps` = 8 taps on a Vogel disc (golden-angle spiral, deterministic) around the screen position, rotated per pixel by an interleaved-gradient-noise angle from `gl_FragCoord`, radius `R` half-size texels: `R = clamp(kSsrBlurScale * rough_eff^2 * view_scale, 0, kSsrBlurMax)` with `kSsrBlurScale` 24, `kSsrBlurMax` 8 texels. Using `rough^2` (the GGX `alpha`) makes the cone grow where the lobe does. Final constants are tuned in 48c against a roughness ladder image, written into the as-built entry.
- **Weights.** The taps are alpha-weighted: `rgb = sum(s_i.rgb * s_i.a * k_i) / max(sum(s_i.a * k_i), 1e-4)`, `conf = sum(s_i.a * k_i) / sum(k_i)`. A miss tap (a = 0, rgb = 0) then lowers the confidence instead of darkening the colour, so a blurred edge fades into the probes, not into black. `k_i` is a smooth radial falloff. Taps are clamped inside the texture (`linear_clamp_`).
- **Replacing the hard fade.** The reflection no longer needs to be dropped early: the blur makes a rough reflection look right, so the fade to the probes is meant to sit at a higher roughness. **Defaults stay as they are** (`max_roughness` 0.5, `roughness_fade` 0.2: they live in `scene3d.h` and `kit3d.h`, both wide, and a default change would move every SSR golden); what changes is that a scene which raises `ssr_max_roughness` to 0.7 or 0.8 now gets a good-looking result, which it could not before (it got a razor-sharp reflection on a rough surface). The sample (48f) and the `08-3d` paragraph say so, and a default change is a recorded follow-up (one line plus golden regeneration by the integration runner).
- **Cost.** Per reflecting rough pixel 8 fetches of an RGBA16F half-size texture, with no pass added. At most all main-pass pixels of a full-screen rough floor; estimated under 0.5 ms at 1080p on a mid GPU, **an estimate, measured on the GPU list**. The tap count is a shader constant, not a setting; `r.ssr.blur 0` (default 1) is an A/B and panic switch that returns the single tap.
- **Interaction with SSR confidence and the probes.** `w` keeps its formula (the roughness fade and `conf`); only `s` changes from one tap to the gather. Nothing else in the lit path moves, the coat and the sheen still ignore SSR (a recorded limit of M-VISUAL2), captures (probes, GI) still bind the stand-in and never read it.
- **Uniforms.** The blur needs two numbers (on/off and the texel size can be had from `textureSize(u_ssr, 0)`): `p_ssr` has no spare lane, so one `vec4` is added to the frame uniform block (`MCE_GLSL_FRAME_U` and `FrameU`, both private to `shaders3d.h` / `forward3d.cpp`, not an installed header). Check the UBO size, the Metal and SDL GPU layouts and every pass that fills `FrameU` (`fu` is built in several places; unfilled lanes must be zero).

### 2. Normal-mapped normals: decided, and why the full route is not planned (48d)

The question: can the prepass output the perturbed normal so that the ray follows the bumps? **Technically yes for the plain normal-map case, practically too costly and wrong for the cases that matter.** The honest accounting:

| Route | What it takes | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1. `kAoPrepassFS` samples the normal map and writes the perturbed normal into `ao_gbuf_` | per-draw normal-map bind in the prepass (`draw_meshes` binds only the albedo for `prepass` today: one more texture bind per draw), the derivative tangent frame (`perturb_normal` fits, no tangent attribute), a material flag in the prepass, the same change in **four** pipelines (`ao_prepass_`, `_skinned_`, `_inst_`, the batch draws). **Every consumer** of `ao_gbuf_` sees the change: SSAO would darken every bump (the Alchemy-style obscurance reads the normal), contact shadows test `dot(n, l)` against it, and the outline and the depth of field read it. Goldens with normal maps and any of those effects change. | **Not planned.** Too costly and too wide for a "small follow-up", and it makes SSAO worse. |
| 2. A second prepass MRT (`ao_gbuf_n_`, perturbed normal only for SSR) | the route 1 plumbing plus an extra color attachment on every prepass pipeline (twin pipelines, so SSAO-only scenes do not pay the write), a half-size RG16F target, a third sampler in `kSsrFS`. No other consumer changes. | **Not planned.** About a milestone on its own (follow-up 2). Even then terrain splat normals (triplanar layers), decals, wet, snow and ripples cannot be repeated cheaply: the prepass normal would still differ from the lit one. |
| 3. **Treat the normal map as extra roughness for the reflection lookup (a Toksvig-style widening), in `kMeshFS` only** | the shader already has the geometric normal `n_geo` and the mapped normal `n`: `d = 1 - dot(n, n_geo)` is how far the map tilts this pixel. The blur of section 1 then uses `rough_eff^2 = rough^2 + kSsrBumpGain * d` (gain 2 to 4, tuned in 48d), while the **blend weight `w` keeps the material roughness**, so a bumpy mirror floor still reflects. | **Chosen.** It touches only the main pass shader, reuses 48c's gather, costs no tap beyond it and costs nothing on a flat normal map. It is a *plausible* reflection (a bumpy surface smears the reflection over the area the bumps would send it to), not a correct one: no individual bump mirrors anything in particular. |

- **What 48d states honestly in the docs:** SSR on a normal-mapped surface follows the geometric normal for *where* it looks and the mapped normal for *how it is shaded*; the bump only widens the reflection. A normal map with strong relief on a mirror (a wet cobblestone with high gain) shows a blurred copy, not per-stone reflections. Hammered metal, cloth, wet ground, rough stone are fine; a glass-like bumpy mirror is not.
- Applies when `o_maps.x > 0.5` (a normal map exists) and, for `MCE_TERRAIN`, with the terrain's own `t_n` against the geometric normal (same formula, `n` is `t_n` there); `fill` (decals, wet) scales `d` down with `(1 - fill)`, as it scales the map itself.
- **The decision is rechecked at the end of 48d**, with the measured cost of route 2's extra target (one RG16F half-size texture plus twin pipelines) written into the as-built entry; the plan stays route 3 unless the image shows route 3 failing on a real material.

### 3. Moving-object hits: the velocity of the hit pixel (48e)

- **Idea.** Today the hit's previous screen position is the camera's reprojection of its current view position, so a moving object is looked up where a *static* point at that place would have been. The velocity buffer already holds, per pixel, how far its surface point moved on screen since last frame (skinned meshes included). At the hit `huv`, `puv = huv - velocity_(huv).xy` is where the hit surface *was*, camera and object motion together, and replaces the camera-only `puv`.
- **Optional, cheap only when velocity exists.** SSR does **not** turn the velocity prepass on (a full opaque draw pass for one effect is not cheap). It is used when `velocity_on_` already holds for the frame (TAA, motion blur or depth of field: the High tier and any scene that asks for them). Otherwise `kSsrFS` runs today's path unchanged (`r_misc.y = 0`).
- **Ordering.** `record_velocity` moves from after the MSAA resolve to **before `record_ssr`** (it needs only the opaque draws and the previous matrices, not the lit frame): the pass reads nothing the main pass writes, so the pixels do not change; the stamp bucket changes (its time moves from the TAA bucket to the SSR one when SSR is on; recorded in the as-built entry and the docs; `kTsSsr` stays the end of the SSR stage). When SSR is off or velocity is off, the order is unchanged. Check `velocity_prev_valid_` and `prev_view_proj_` handling stay in one place (`record_velocity` owns them).
- **The shader.** `kSsrFS` gets a third sampler (`kFirstSampler + 2`, `velocity_`, nearest) and `r_misc.y`. At the hit: `v = texelFetch(velocity_, full-size texel of huv)`; use it when `v.b > 0.5` (not the behind-camera marker) **and** `abs(v.a - hg.w) < tol * hg.w` (the full-size texel is the same surface as the half-size hit: at a moving object's silhouette the nearest full-size texel can be the background; tol 0.1, tuned); otherwise fall back to the camera path for that pixel. The velocity is unjittered while `hdr_` was drawn jittered: the shader adds the jitter difference so a **static scene gives the same `puv` within half a texel with the velocity path on and off** (a gate, below).
- **What it still does not do.** Only the hit surface's own motion is used; a reflected object that is itself hidden from last frame's camera (disocclusion), or that the previous frame's HDR drew differently (shadow, light, animation) gives the old pixel. The reflecting surface's own motion is irrelevant (the lookup is by screen position now). Rays that hit a surface the velocity pass did not draw (a transparent or batch draw missing from the velocity pass) fall back to the camera path by the depth test.
- **Why optional.** The visible gain is a character or a crate moving in front of a mirror or a wet floor: without it the reflection trails by the object's per-frame travel (a few pixels at 60 Hz walking speed, tens for a fast vehicle); with TAA a trailing ghost. `r.ssr.motion 0` (default 1) returns the camera path. If the image test of 48e does not show a gain worth the reorder, the step ships as "decided against, reason written" and the reorder is dropped; the plan allows that outcome.

### Cvars, stats, tiers

- Cvars next to `cv_ssr` in `forward3d.cpp` (declared the same way, **no header**): `r.ssr.blur` (bool, default 1, "SSR roughness blur; 0 = one sharp tap") and `r.ssr.motion` (bool, default 1, "SSR reads the velocity buffer at hits; 0 = camera reprojection only"). No quality tier row (QualitySettings is wide); both follow `r.ssr` and the Low tier's SSR-off.
- Stats (48a, `forward3d.h`): `Forward3DStats::ssr_blur` (this frame's gather was on) and `ssr_motion` (this frame's trace read the velocity), as `ssr` and `ssr_history` are today; they let the tests ask what ran without a pixel guess.
- The off checks: `r.ssr 0` or `ScreenSpaceReflections::enabled` false skips everything (no sampler, no reorder, no uniform use); `r.ssr.blur 0` and `r.ssr.motion 0` return today's code path exactly (byte-identical frame); a perfectly rough-free mirror (roughness at or below 0.08) stays one tap.

### Determinism

Rendering is outside the state hash and the replay. The new shader math uses the pixel's `gl_FragCoord` and constants only (no time, no rand), so two runs of one frame agree bit for bit on one device. Nothing here reaches the simulation, a component or the Luau API.

- **Kit and headless:** `kit3d` needs no new test (no field changes); the 48f sample's headless run twice agrees and prints the expected log.

## Risks

- **Gather noise.** 8 taps at a radius up to 8 half-size texels is under-sampled at the top of the range; the per-pixel rotation turns it into noise, which TAA (High) removes and Medium (no TAA) shows. Mitigations: the radius cap, the Vogel pattern, the tuned scale; the fallback is the pyramid of follow-up 1. Judged on a real display (GPU list).
- **Cost of the gather on a full-screen rough floor.** An estimate only; llvmpipe cannot judge it. `r.ssr.blur 0` is the panic switch and the tap count a constant to lower.
- **The widening is not a reflection.** It hides the mismatch between the geometric ray and the mapped shading; it cannot show a per-bump reflection. Said in the docs so nobody expects it.
- **The velocity reorder.** Moving `record_velocity` earlier changes the command order only when SSR and velocity are both on; pixels are unchanged by construction but the TAA and motion blur goldens and the stamp buckets must be rechecked (the test above). The jitter correction is the part most likely to be wrong by a texel; its gate is the static-scene comparison.
- **Half-size hit against full-size velocity.** At a silhouette the nearest velocity texel can belong to the background; the depth-match falls back to the camera path, so a thin moving object's reflection can still trail at its edge. Recorded.
- **Defaults.** Raising `max_roughness` by default would make the blur matter to every scene, and the golden effect needs the integration runner; left to a follow-up so this milestone's goldens stay byte-identical.
- **The mapped normal's cost and the prepass.** If a later milestone does route 2, the extra MRT must not be paid by SSAO-only scenes (twin pipelines): recorded in the table above.

## GPU verification

Headless (llvmpipe, lavapipe): the CPU helper (48b) is exact; on the GL and SDL GPU fixture the off equalities (`r.ssr 0`, `r.ssr.blur 0`, `r.ssr.motion 0`, roughness at or below 0.08, a flat normal map, no velocity) hold byte for byte; the roughness ladder widens monotonically; the noisy-map floor reflects wider and no darker than the flat one; a moving sphere's reflection is closer to the truth with the velocity path; the static-scene jitter gate; the stamp ordering; two runs agree. Real-GPU behaviour and look (the noise of the gather without TAA, its cost on a full-screen rough floor at 1080p, the best constants, the look of the bump widening on real normal maps, the trailing of a fast object with and without the velocity, Metal and WebGPU shader compilation of the new lane and sampler): proposed rows on the GPU list (`/mnt/project-files/assessments/gpu-gereken-isler.md`, next free numbers after A19; the coordinator adds them, as for A19).

## Follow-ups

1. A pyramid of `ssr_` (half, quarter, eighth) built into a packed atlas through ping-pong targets and read by lod in `kMeshFS` (the smooth version of 48c; needs the main shader's uv remap and a compose pass), if the gather's noise is not acceptable without TAA.
2. The perturbed normal for SSR through a second prepass MRT (twin pipelines, a half-size RG16F target, a third sampler in `kSsrFS`): the correct per-bump reflection for normal maps; costs about a milestone and still cannot cover terrain splats, wet, snow or ripples.
3. Contact-hardening blur from the hit distance (sharp near the contact, soft far away): needs the distance stored next to the colour (a second target or a repacked `ssr_`).
4. Raise the default `ssr_max_roughness` / `ssr_roughness_fade` (a `scene3d.h` and `kit3d.h` default change; the goldens are regenerated by the integration runner).
5. A coat and sheen that read SSR (M-VISUAL2 limit); reflections of the back faces and of transparent surfaces.
6. A temporal accumulation of `ssr_` for scenes without TAA.
7. 48a to 48f as built: one entry per step below, written when each lands.

## Deviations from the plan

- 48a: the stats seam shipped in the same PR as 48b and 48c (the plan allowed it if the three-step PR is merged first).
- 48b: `kSsrBlurMinRough` is **0.1, not 0.08** (the audit: the `ssr_room` golden's floor is 0.1); the radius is `24 * (rough^2 - 0.1^2)`, continuous at the threshold, instead of `24 * rough^2`; no `view_scale` factor: the radius is in half-size texels at every resolution (a resolution-relative radius is a GPU-list tuning question).
- 48c: the blur's on switch is a lane of `PassU` (`p_ssr2`, from the free vec4s, size unchanged) instead of a new `FrameU` vec4: `p_ssr` lives in `PassU`, the free lanes are already zero in every other fill, and `FrameU`'s size and its Metal and SDL GPU layouts stay untouched. `r.ssr.blur 0` against the pre-change frame is pinned through the `ssr_room` golden (roughness 0.1) and the 0.8 floor case; other roughnesses were not compared with a pre-change binary (the blur-off branch is the old code path, line for line).
- 48d and 48e shipped in their own PR, without 48f (the sample, docs and close followed in a third PR).
- 48f: the moving object is a sphere driven by a timeline (the plan's 'sphere moving back and forth'); the plan's `ssr_max_roughness` 0.8 is in the scene, the roughness 0.7 strip sits inside its fade so it shows little reflection (the point: the range is usable, the default stays). The GPU test checks the log, not pixels, as the other tour captures do. There is no `-r.ssr` row in the 8.3 table before this PR, so it was added with the two new ones.
- 48d: a mirror at or below `kSsrBlurMinRough` (0.1) stays one sharp tap whatever its normal map (the 48b helper returns 0 there), instead of the plan's `rough_eff^2 = rough^2 + gain * d`, which would have widened a bumpy mirror too; the bump adds to the alpha above the threshold. The fill scaling is `(1 - fill)` on the unmixed map's tilt. The planned test "a flat map is byte-identical to no map" does not hold (silhouette pixels of `perturb_normal`, above); the test bounds it instead. The terrain check compares against the mesh floor's width and mean (and byte-identity at 0.05), not against a pre-change binary.
- 48e: the moving object is a cube, not a sphere (the test fixture builds boxes). The depth tolerance is 0.1 untuned (the gate passed at the first value). The velocity's previous jitter comes from the matrices (`ssr_prev_vp_ * inverse(velocity_prev_vp_used_)`) because no member can be added without editing `forward3d.h`; for the same reason `stats_.ssr_motion` carries the decision from `record_view` to `record_ssr`.
