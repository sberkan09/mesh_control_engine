# M-NIGHTSKY — A moon with phases, moonlight and a night star field (milestone record)

**Status:** planned (2026-10-22). **Owner: routine 2 thread (the M-VISUAL line).** Follow-up of M-DAYNIGHT (`WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Environment3D.time_of_day`), M-VISUAL3 (the HDR sky and `Environment3D.sky_rotation_deg`) and M-CLOUDS (the sky pass shader and its quality switch). M-DAYNIGHT listed "a moving moon with phases" as out of scope. Today the night is a dark gradient: `DayCycle` dims the sun colour and intensity (the night key is a blue 0.05), `Kit3DExtract` still places the one directional light with `render::sun_direction_at(time_of_day)`, so after sunset the light points up from below the horizon, and the sky shows no moon and no stars (an author can only add an equirect star texture through `sky_texture_additive`, which does not follow the clock or the weather). No Linear issues (free issue limit); this record is the tracker. Shader steps are Opus-written and merged after an independent Opus review plus a green `--task` (as M-CLOUDS 9b).

**Goal.** An opt-in kit component `Moon` turns the `WorldClock` day count and hour into a moon phase, a direction and a brightness. At night `Kit3DExtract` hands the scene's directional light to the moon (cool tint, low intensity, soft shadows from the same cascades) instead of the sun that is below the horizon. The sky pass draws the moon disk with its phase and a procedural star field that fades in with darkness and out under cloud, fog and moonlight. Deterministic, saved with the scene, opt-in; a scene without `Moon` hashes, simulates and renders exactly as today.

**Not in scope:** aurora (a moving curtain needs its own noise, latitude and animation budget; a separate shader follow-up if the stars read well), a *second* simultaneous directional light (the renderer has one `DirectionalLight` slot, see Design; adding one touches the forward, shadow and cluster paths), eclipses, tides, moon-cast cloud lighting beyond what the cloud shader already takes from the frame light, stars or the moon in the IBL bake, reflection probes or GI captures (the main sky pass only, like clouds), the moon under an equirect `sky_texture` (the texture wins, as with clouds), planets, a real ephemeris (the moon is a cheap model: a 29.53 day phase cycle and a path derived from the sun's), changing `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Weather` or `Environment3D` bytes (the last is a wide header).

## Design

- **Opt-in component, no system, no state.** New component `Moon` (`mce/kit/moon.h`, one per scene, the first in ECS order is read, registered by `install_weather` next to `GroundMist`; 14a confirms the registration spot by reading `install_weather`). Pure data: phase, direction and brightness are pure functions of authoring, `WorldClock.day`, `time_of_day` and `Weather`, evaluated at extract and in the Luau getters, so nothing is carried between ticks and nothing is hashed beyond the authored fields. Authoring (all `Save`, with a `Range`): `enabled` 1; `phase_offset` 0.5 (0..1, the phase at day 0, hour 0; 0.5 is a full moon, so a fresh scene has moonlight to show); `cycle_days` 29.53 (2..120, the synodic period, so a short in-game year can still cycle through phases); `freeze_phase` -1 (-1..1, 0..1 pins the phase for a scene or a screenshot, negative = follow the clock); `inclination_deg` 5 (0..30, how far the path sits off the sun's arc); `light_intensity` 0.12 (0..2, directional intensity at full moon, scaled by the phase); `light_color` (default 0.55 0.65 1.0, linear); `light_shadows` 1; `disk_size` 0.9990 (0.99..1, cos of the angular radius, bigger than it is in life so it reads; M-VISUAL3 style); `disk_intensity` 6 (0..50); `star_density` 0.6 (0..1; 0 = no stars); `star_brightness` 1 (0..4); `star_twinkle` 0.3 (0..1). No engine-written fields, so no padding audit beyond keeping the struct naturally packed with explicit zeroed pad.
- **Phase and path (kit, 14a).** Pure inline helpers in `moon.h`: `moon_phase(const Moon&, u32 day, f32 hour) -> f32` (0 new, 0.5 full, 1 wraps; `frac((day + hour / 24) / cycle_days + phase_offset)` in f64 with a fixed evaluation order, or `freeze_phase` when it is >= 0), `moon_illumination(f32 phase) -> f32` (`0.5 - 0.5 * cos(2 pi phase)` through a polynomial/table-free fixed form so results are platform-stable, 0 at new, 1 at full), `moon_direction(const Moon&, f32 hour, f32 phase) -> vec3` (the direction the light travels, like `sun_direction_at`: the moon sits `phase * 24` hours behind the sun on the sun's own arc, so a full moon rises at sunset and a new moon is up with the sun, tilted off the arc by `inclination_deg`; built from `render::sun_direction_at(hour - phase * 24)`, which `kit3d_extract.cpp` already calls), and `moon_level(...)` the effective light strength `illumination * smoothstep(moon elevation) * weather_factor` (Overcast 0.5, Rain 0.3, Storm 0.15 times `Weather.intensity` blended from 1; no `Weather` means no damping). A negative hour (`time_of_day` is -1: no clock) gives level 0 and no moon. `WorldClock.day` is read only; without a `WorldClock` the day is 0.
- **Moonlight in the extract (kit, 14b).** `Kit3DExtract::apply_environment` builds the one `render::DirectionalLight sun` (`kit3d_extract.cpp` ~811 to ~818). With a `Moon`, `enabled`, a clock and a moon level above 0, on the local `sun` copy only, never on `Environment3D`: a sun weight `sw = smoothstep(-0.05, 0.1, sun_elevation)` and a moon weight `mw = (1 - sw) * moon_level`; the light keeps the sun when `sw * sun_intensity >= mw * light_intensity` and takes the moon (direction, `light_color`, `light_intensity * mw`, `light_shadows`) otherwise. The renderer has exactly one directional slot (`Scene3D::set_light`, `light_`), so "moonlight as a second directional light" is realised as a hand-off of that slot at the point where the sun's and the moon's contributions are equal and both small, which keeps the pop bounded and costs no shader, shadow or cluster change. Ambient stays the `DayCycle` night key; the moon adds a small cool ambient lift (`light_color * level * 0.02`) so shadowed ground is not pure black. 14b measures the hand-off (largest per-tick change of intensity and of direction across a full dusk) and records it. With no `Moon`, `enabled` 0, a zero level or the sun dominant, the code path leaves `sun` unchanged, bit-identical to today.
- **Render carrier (seam, 14c).** `render::NightSky {moon_dir (xyz, direction toward the moon), moon_cos_size, moon_intensity, moon_color, moon_phase, star_density, star_brightness, star_twinkle, star_time, night_fade, enabled, pad_}` in `scene3d.h` next to `Clouds`, `set_night_sky()` / `night_sky()`, `QualitySettings::night_sky` and cvar `r.nightsky` (Low draws the moon disk without twinkle and no stars; `apply_quality` by-value overload clamps ranges and normalises `moon_dir`, NaN to 0), exactly the shape of 9a. `scene3d.h` is a wide header (`include_fanout.py` reports 134 of 817 TUs for `src/engine/render/include/mce/render/scene3d.h`, under the 150 warning but a rebuild of every consumer), so this ships as its own small seam PR merged first, with the reason in the description. `star_density` 0 and `moon_intensity` 0 mean off: the frame is the one without it.
- **Sky shader (Opus, 14d).** One `vec4 p_night` (moon cos size, moon intensity, phase, night fade) and one `vec4 p_night2` (star density, brightness, twinkle, time) plus a `vec4 p_moon` (direction, spare) appended after the last `PassU` member in `Forward3DPassU` and `MCE_GLSL_PASS_U`; GLSL in `kSkyFS` only, behind `p_night.y > 0.0 || p_night2.x > 0.0` so a frame without the night sky is bit-identical. Stars: the view direction is cut into an integer cell grid (octahedral or cube-face mapping, no `sin()`), each cell hashed with the pcg2d family `cloud_hash` already uses to decide a star, its position in the cell, a magnitude (a few bright, many faint) and a slight colour; the point is a small smoothstep falloff sized in pixels-per-radian so it does not alias; twinkle is a hash-phased brightness wobble from `star_time` wrapped on the CPU in f64 to a period (as the cloud drift is); the whole layer is scaled by `night_fade` and masked for `d.y <= 0` and by the horizon haze. Moon: a disk of `moon_cos_size` around `moon_dir` shaded as a sphere with the terminator set by the phase (the lit-side normal from a 2D reconstruction of the sphere facing the camera, the sun-moon-viewer angle from `moon_phase`; no texture), a soft limb and a faint additive halo. Both are added before the cloud composite so a cloud in front dims them (the same line that dims the sun disk), and before the volumetric or exp² fog application so fog hides them. Nothing here reads the IBL path (`sky_radiance()` is not touched).
- **Extract feed of the night sky (kit, 14e).** `Kit3DExtract` fills `render::NightSky` when a `Moon` exists: direction and phase from the helpers, `night_fade = 1 - smoothstep(-0.1, 0.1, sun_elevation)` times a cloud/weather factor (the cloud deck coverage already derived at ~977 dims the stars: `1 - coverage`, and the fog density dims them further), moon intensity from `disk_intensity`, `star_time` from `Scene3D::time()`; the result goes through `apply_quality`. Under an equirect `sky_texture` (non-null `env.sky_texture`) the night sky is not set. A scene without `Moon` gets `night_sky()` with density and intensity 0.
- **Why no system.** `WorldClock` is already an exact tick-quantized counter and `time_of_day` an exact rational; the phase, direction and level are smooth pure functions of them. A moon system would only add state to hash and a way to drift. The `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Weather` and `Environment3D` bytes are unchanged by construction.
- **Determinism and hash.** A scene without `Moon` is byte-identical (one empty query in the extract; the extract is not part of the state hash). A scene with it differs only in the saved component and in frames. Phase and direction depend only on authoring, `day` and the hour, so two runs agree and a save/load at midnight gives the same phase; a test evaluates the helpers at fixed ticks of a `WorldClock` run in one go and in two halves. Stars are a pure function of the view direction and the wrapped time; no `rand`, no wall clock.
- **Luau (14f).** `weather.set_moon{phase_offset=, cycle_days=, freeze_phase=, inclination=, light_intensity=, light_color={r,g,b}, light_shadows=, disk_size=, disk_intensity=, star_density=, star_brightness=, star_twinkle=, enabled=}` and getters (the authoring plus read-only `weather.moon_phase()`, `weather.moon_illumination()`, `weather.moon_direction()` and `weather.moon_level()` from the same helpers on the current clock); named errors, values clamped to the Range, nothing written on error, a missing `Moon` raises (the setter never adds one), typed in `definitions.cpp`, same rules as `set_mist`, `set_gust` and `set_clouds`. The `weather` table is where the other follow-ups live; if 14f finds a `sky` or `clock` table fits better it records the choice. `samples/12_weather` gets a `Moon` on the Weather entity (entity count unchanged) when the sample has a `WorldClock` (14f reads the sample first; if it has none it sets `time_of_day` through the existing Luau clock calls, as 13c does for the mist), logs `moon:` every 150 ticks, and its golden and log expectations move in that step, nothing else. A night capture of `samples/11_daynight` is an optional second consumer; it is decided in 14f and not required.

## Gates
- A scene without `Moon` (or with `enabled` 0, no clock or a zero level) has an unchanged state hash and unchanged frames; every existing sample hash and golden stays byte-identical until 14f changes `samples/12_weather`, and only that sample's expectations move. Existing goldens under `tests/render_gl/*` stay byte-identical through 14c and 14d on both backends.
- `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `Weather`, `CloudLayer` and `Environment3D` are never written by moon code: the extract changes only its local `render::DirectionalLight` and `render::NightSky`; a test hashes the resource and the `Weather` column with and without `Moon`.
- Phase, direction and level are pure functions of authoring, `WorldClock.day`, `time_of_day` and `Weather`; no stored float, no wall clock, no `rand`. Star positions are a pure function of the view direction.
- New golden `night_sky` with pixel tests as in 14d; a night sky at coverage 1 (overcast) shows fewer stars than a clear one.
- Include fan-out: the only wide header touched is `scene3d.h`, in the 14c seam PR (checked with `scripts/ci/include_fanout.py`, 134 of 817 TUs at planning time); `moon.h` is included only by the extract, the install, the script binding and the tests; `kit3d.h`, `weather.h`, `world_clock.h`, `day_cycle.h` are not edited.
- Slow tests: a night-sky GPU test over 60 s ships a smoke variant (one frame, one phase) that `--task` runs and the multi-phase sweep is labelled `slow` with `mce_mark_slow(<test>)`; the sample's added `moon:` run does not lengthen `sample_weather_headless`.
- Luau: setters clamp, bad types raise named errors and write nothing.

## Risks
- **One directional slot.** The brief's "second directional light" does not exist as a renderer feature; the hand-off in 14b reuses the single slot. The risk is a visible pop where sun and moon trade places. Mitigations: the crossover is at equal, small contributions, `DayCycle`'s own night key keeps the ambient continuous, and 14b measures it; if the pop is unacceptable the fallback is a short cross-fade of the intensity through zero over the hand-off (still one light), and a true second light becomes a separate render milestone.
- **Night key already lights from below.** After sunset today's light comes from under the horizon with `DayCycle`'s 0.05 blue; the moon replaces it only with a `Moon`. A scene with a `DayCycle` and no `Moon` keeps that look by the bit-identical gate, so the improvement is opt-in.
- **Main shader risk: stars aliasing and shimmer.** Point stars minified to sub-pixel sizes crawl when the camera turns. 14d sizes the point by pixel footprint, clamps the minimum coverage, and includes a camera-rotation temporal test (frame difference below a threshold for a one-degree turn); if it still crawls the density is capped and recorded.
- **Moon phase read-ability.** A disk a few pixels wide cannot show a terminator; the default `disk_size` is exaggerated and the guide says so. Low tier draws the lit disk without the terminator.
- **Interaction with fog and clouds.** The moon and stars must sit behind the cloud composite and the fog application; a mis-ordered line would show stars through a deck or through fog. Tests cover both, and the ordering is part of the Opus review checklist.
- **Dependence on `time_of_day`.** Scenes with no `WorldClock` have `time_of_day` -1 and show no moon and no stars; the guide says to add a clock and the Luau setter's help text repeats it. A scene with only a hand-set `time_of_day` shows day 0's phase.
- **Aurora deferred.** The user-visible ask mentions an aurora; it is left out on purpose (needs its own noise and animation budget and a latitude concept), and recorded here so a follow-up can pick it up once the star field exists.
