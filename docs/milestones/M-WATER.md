# M-WATER — An opt-in water surface for 3D scenes: animated waves, sky reflection, depth tint, shore foam (milestone record)

**Status:** planned (2026-10-22), steps 15a to 15e. **Owner: routine 2 thread (the M-VISUAL line).** Follow-up of M-WEATHER, M-WETSURFACE, M-NIGHTSKY, M-TERRAIN and M-CLOUDS. Today a scene has no water surface: a grep of `src/engine/render` and `src/engine/kit` finds "water" only in the puddle and ripple code of M-WETSURFACE and M-RAINFX (standing water on materials, `apply_ripples`), in `nav3d.h` and `navmesh3d.h` (walkability), and M-RAINFX and M-SNOWCOVER list "water volumes and real ocean/river" as not in scope. M-TERRAIN builds islands (`m-terrain-island.png`) whose sea level is a flat clear colour or a hand-placed mesh. No Linear issues (free issue limit); this record is the tracker. The shader step is Opus-written and merged after an independent Opus review plus a green `--task` (as M-CLOUDS 9b and M-NIGHTSKY 14d).

**Goal.** An opt-in kit component `Water` describes one horizontal water plane (level, size, wave set, colours). `Kit3DExtract` hands it to the renderer as a small `render::WaterSurface` carrier, and one shader step draws it with animated Gerstner-wave normals, a fresnel reflection of the existing sky (day sky, M-NIGHTSKY moon and stars, clouds and fog all as the sky pass already draws them), a depth tint and, where terrain is available, shore foam. Gameplay reads the same waves through a pure `water_height_at`, so a boat or a float can bob on exactly what is drawn. Deterministic, saved with the scene, opt-in; a scene without `Water` hashes, simulates and renders exactly as today.

**Not in scope:** fluid simulation, flow maps or rivers with a direction field (one plane, one wave set), planar or screen-space reflections of geometry (the reflection is the sky only; geometry reflection needs an extra pass and is a separate render milestone), refraction of submerged geometry beyond the depth tint, caustics, underwater post-process and camera-under-water look, buoyancy forces in physics3d (15d gives the height query; applying forces stays with the author's script), tessellation or vertex displacement of the plane (normals only, the plane stays flat; see Risks), foam on moving objects (wakes), tides, ocean spray particles, water sounds, changing `Weather`, `WorldClock`, `DayCycle` or `Environment3D` bytes (the last is a wide header), the water in the IBL bake or reflection probes (main pass only, like clouds and the night sky).

## Design

- **Opt-in component, no system, no state.** New component `Water` (`mce/kit/water.h`, one per scene, the first in ECS order is read, registered by `install_weather` next to `GroundMist` and `Moon`; 15a confirms the registration spot by reading `install_weather`, and that the component belongs in the weather install rather than a new one). Pure data: wave height and normal are pure functions of authoring, position and time, evaluated at extract and in the Luau getters, so nothing is carried between ticks and nothing is hashed beyond the authored fields. Authoring (all `Save`, with a `Range`): `enabled` 1; `level_y` 0 (-1000..1000, the world height of the calm surface); `half_extent` 500 (1..20000 m, the plane is a square of that half size centred on the camera's xz snapped to a grid, so the plane follows the camera and never ends); `wave_count` 4 (1..8, how many Gerstner waves are summed); `amplitude` 0.4 (0..10 m, the height of the largest wave); `wavelength` 24 (1..500 m, the longest wavelength; the others follow by a fixed ratio table); `steepness` 0.6 (0..1, the Gerstner Q factor, divided by the wave count so the sum never loops); `wind_dir_deg` 0 (0..360, the main travel direction; the other waves spread around it by a fixed angle table); `wind_follow` 1 (0..1, 1 = the main direction follows `WindDirection` when the scene has one, so water and clouds agree); `speed_scale` 1 (0..4, scales the deep-water phase speed `sqrt(g * k)`); `shallow_color` (default 0.10 0.38 0.40, linear), `deep_color` (default 0.01 0.08 0.15), `depth_scale_m` 8 (0.1..200, the depth at which the tint is fully deep), `reflectivity` 1 (0..1, scales the fresnel), `roughness` 0.08 (0..1, blurs the reflection and the sun glint), `foam_amount` 0.5 (0..1, 0 = no foam), `foam_width_m` 1.5 (0.05..20, the shore band). No engine-written fields, so there is no padding audit beyond keeping the struct naturally packed with explicit zeroed pad (`test_padding` entry next to `GroundMist`).
- **Waves (kit, 15a).** Pure inline helpers in `water.h`: `water_wave_set(const Water&, float wind_dir_deg)` expands the authoring into at most 8 `{dir, k, amplitude, steepness, omega, phase_seed}` entries from fixed tables (no `rand`, no hash of pointers), `water_height(const Water&, vec2 xz, f32 t)` and `water_normal(const Water&, vec2 xz, f32 t)` (the Gerstner sum: the horizontal displacement is applied by a fixed 2-iteration fixed-point inverse so the height is evaluated at the undisplaced xz that lands on the query point, the standard way to make `water_height_at` match the drawn surface), `water_phase_speed(k)`. f32 with a fixed evaluation order; `t` is the scene time wrapped in f64 to the common period of the wave set (every `omega` is a whole multiple of a base frequency, so the wrap is seamless, as the cloud drift and the star time are). std trig is used (the result is never hashed and the same pure-function rule as `moon_direction` applies); NaN, inverted and zero ranges fall back to the defaults. A zero `amplitude` returns exactly the level and a straight-up normal.
- **Render carrier (seam, 15b).** `render::WaterSurface {level_y, half_extent, wave_count, amplitude, wavelength, steepness, wind_dir (xy), time, speed_scale, shallow_color, deep_color, depth_scale_m, reflectivity, roughness, foam_amount, foam_width_m, enabled, pad_}` in `scene3d.h` next to `Clouds` and `NightSky`, `set_water()` / `water()`, `QualitySettings::water` and cvar `r.water` (Low draws a flat tinted plane with the sky reflection and no animated normals, no foam; `apply_quality` by-value overload clamps ranges, NaN to 0), exactly the shape of 9a and 14c. `scene3d.h` is a wide header (`include_fanout.py` reported 136 of 822 TUs at M-NIGHTSKY 14c, under the 150 warning but a rebuild of every consumer), so this ships as its own small seam PR merged first, with the reason and the current `include_fanout.py` number in the description. `enabled` false or `amplitude` and plane both off means no plane: the frame is the one without it. The carrier holds the wave set as authoring parameters, not as eight expanded entries, so the shader re-derives the same tables the kit uses (the table is shared as a constant block copied into both, with a test that evaluates the GLSL constants against `water_wave_set` through the pixel tests of 15c).
- **Extract feed (kit, 15b).** `Kit3DExtract::apply_environment` fills `render::WaterSurface` from the first `Water` (`.cpp` only; no header changed beyond the carrier): `time` is the value `set_time` gets later in the same extract (as 14e's `star_time`, because `Scene3D::time()` still holds last frame's value), wrapped in f64; the main direction is `wind_dir_deg`, or the `WindDirection` angle when `wind_follow` is above 0; the result goes through `apply_quality`. No water is set with no `Water`, `enabled` 0 or an invalid level. Sea colour does not touch `Fog`, `Sky` or the light.
- **Water shader (Opus, 15c).** The plane is a camera-following quad (two triangles, snapped to a grid so the pattern does not swim) drawn in the forward pass after opaque geometry and before the transparent sorted pass, with depth test on and depth write on so geometry in front of it still occludes it. A new `kWaterVS` / `kWaterFS` pair in `shaders3d.h` and a `WaterU` block (two or three `vec4`: level, extent, amplitude and steepness; wavelength, wind dir and time; colours and depth scale; reflectivity, roughness, foam) appended after the last `PassU` member in `Forward3DPassU` and `MCE_GLSL_PASS_U`, filled by a `water_params` next to `night_sky_params` in `forward3d.cpp`. The fragment shader: Gerstner normal summed from the shared constants with a distance fade to a flat normal (no sparkle at the horizon; the fade also fixes the aliasing of the shortest wave) plus a small second octave of cheap noise-free detail from two scrolled sines; fresnel (Schlick, F0 0.02) mixing the refracted tint and the reflection; the reflection samples the same sky function the sky pass uses (`sky_radiance()` for the IBL colour and the analytic sun, moon and star layer of `kSkyFS` along the mirrored view ray, so night, clouds and the moon appear in the water through the shared function and not through a second copy: 15c reads `kSkyFS` first and records how it reuses it, and keeps the moon and stars cheap by sampling only the disk and the star layer for rays above the horizon); the sun glint from the frame's directional light (or the moon's while 14b holds the light) with `roughness`; the volumetric or exp² fog is applied last as for opaque geometry, so mist (M-GROUNDMIST) and fog hide the water at distance. Depth tint: the optical depth is `(surface depth - opaque depth behind)` when the forward pass can read an opaque depth copy; otherwise, and on Low, it is the distance-based tint of a deep ocean. Shore foam: a band `foam_width_m` wide where that depth difference is small, broken by the wave crests (`smoothstep` on the Gerstner `Jacobian`-style foldover term) and a scrolled noise-free stripe pattern; if no readable opaque depth exists in the forward pass on one of the backends, foam uses the terrain height instead (the extract can hand `level_y` and the shader compares it with a world-height ramp only where the author supplies one; 15c decides, records the choice, and ships foam off on a backend that cannot do either, with the guide saying so). The GL and SDL GPU paths both render it; Metal and WebGPU translate the GLSL as for the other passes.
- **Gameplay query and Luau (15d).** `water_height_at(const Water&, vec2 xz, f32 t) -> f32` and `water_normal_at` in `water.h` are the same functions 15a ships, exposed without drawing; a `water_level_at(world, vec3)` convenience reads the first `Water` and the scene time so a script does not pass `t`. Luau `water.set{level=, half_extent=, wave_count=, amplitude=, wavelength=, steepness=, wind_dir=, wind_follow=, speed_scale=, shallow_color={r,g,b}, deep_color={r,g,b}, depth_scale=, reflectivity=, roughness=, foam_amount=, foam_width=, enabled=}`, the authoring getters, and read-only `water.height_at(x, z)`, `water.normal_at(x, z)` and `water.submerged(x, y, z)` (true when y is below the wave height there); named errors, values clamped to the Range, nothing written on error, a missing `Water` raises (the setter never adds one), typed in `definitions.cpp`, same rules as `weather.set_moon`. New table `water` (the weather table is full of unrelated follow-ups; 15d records the choice). `samples/12_weather` gets a `Water` on the Weather entity only if it has terrain or a place for a plane (15d reads the sample first); otherwise a new small sample `samples/13_water` (a floating crate that follows `water.height_at`, a terrain island for the shore) is added. 15d decides and records; the other sample's goldens must not move unless it is the one chosen.
- **Why no system.** The scene time is already an exact tick-quantized counter; the waves are smooth pure functions of it. A water system would only add state to hash and a way for the drawn waves and the gameplay height to drift apart. `Weather`, `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `WindDirection` and `Environment3D` bytes are unchanged by construction.
- **Determinism and hash.** A scene without `Water` is byte-identical (one empty query in the extract; the extract is not part of the state hash). A scene with it differs only in the saved component and in frames. Height and normal depend only on authoring, position and the wrapped time, so two runs agree, a save/load gives the same height and a test evaluates the helpers at fixed ticks in one go and in two halves. The GPU pattern is stable over long runs (time wrapped in f64 on the CPU).

15c is the only step that writes a shader or a draw path, so it is Opus-written with an independent Opus review (the author does not review it), as M-CLOUDS 9b and M-NIGHTSKY 14d were. The review checklist covers the draw order against the transparent pass, the fog and sky reuse, and the depth read. ## Gates
- A scene without `Water` (or with `enabled` 0) has an unchanged state hash and unchanged frames; every existing sample hash and golden stays byte-identical until 15d changes the chosen sample, and only that sample's expectations move. Existing goldens under `tests/render_gl/*` stay byte-identical through 15b and 15c on both backends.
- `Weather`, `WorldClock`, `DayCycle`, `WindDirection`, `CloudLayer` and `Environment3D` are never written by water code: the extract changes only its local `render::WaterSurface`; a test hashes the resource and the `Weather` column with and without `Water`.
- Height and normal are pure functions of authoring, position and time; no stored float, no wall clock, no `rand`. The shader and the kit helper agree (pixel test of 15c).
- New goldens `water_calm`, `water_sea`, `water_night` with pixel tests as in 15c; the horizon reflects more than the ground below the camera.
- Include fan-out: the only wide header touched is `scene3d.h`, in the 15b seam PR (checked with `scripts/ci/include_fanout.py`); `water.h` is included only by the extract, the install, the script binding and the tests; `kit3d.h`, `weather.h`, `world_clock.h`, `day_cycle.h` are not edited.
- Slow tests: a water GPU test over 60 s ships a smoke variant (one frame, one scene) that `--task` runs and the long-time sweep is labelled `slow` with `mce_mark_slow(<test>)`; the sample's added `water:` run does not lengthen its headless test.
- Luau: setters clamp, bad types raise named errors and write nothing.

## Risks
- **Main shader risk: the cost and the look.** A full-screen plane with a Gerstner sum, a sky lookup and fog is more fragment work than any existing pass; 15c measures with `perf_run` at 1080p, caps `wave_count` by tier (Low is flat) and fades the normal with distance. A normal-only surface has a flat silhouette and no parallax at grazing angles; the guide says so, and displaced geometry is a separate follow-up.
- **Depth read.** Depth tint and shore foam need the opaque depth behind the surface. If the forward pass has no readable depth copy on a backend, 15c either adds one (a draw-path change the reviewer must check, and a possible extra seam) or ships the tint by distance and foam off on that backend, recorded in the status row.
- **Sky reuse.** Reflecting the night sky (moon and stars) must reuse `kSkyFS` functions and not copy them, or the two drift apart; if the sky code is not factored well enough to call from the water shader without touching existing goldens, 15c records the limit (day sky and fog only) and the night reflection becomes a follow-up.
- **Kit and shader tables must agree.** The wave table lives in the kit (15a) and in GLSL (15c); the pixel test compares them at probe points, and a change to one without the other fails it.
- **Draw order and transparency.** The plane is drawn before the sorted transparent pass, so a transparent object under the water is drawn after it; the reviewer checks particles and glass near the surface.
- **Dependence on the scene time.** Water animates with the extract's frame time; a paused scene freezes the waves, and the gameplay query uses the same time, so a bobbing object stops with it.
- **Overlap.** Puddles and rain ripples (M-WETSURFACE, M-RAINFX) are material effects and stay untouched; rain ripples on the sea surface are not added here.
